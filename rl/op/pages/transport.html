Driving in Norway

SPEED LIMITS
All Scandinavian roads have speed limits, and speed checks are made frequently without advance warning. Speed limits are always posted in kilometres (1 kilometre = about 2/3 mile).

SEAT BELTS
As in the other Scandinavian countries, seat belts are compulsory for driver and front seat passengers, in Norway also for back seat passengers if belts are fitted. The restrictions do not apply to busses and professional transport.

DRIVING LIGHT
Driving light must be used 24 hours per day.

DRINGKING AND DRIVING
The penalties for driving a motor vehicle under the influence of alcohol are very severe, and no exception is made for foreign visitors. Alcohol test are made frequently without advance warning, and the limit is 0,05%.

PARKING
There are 10 multi-storey car parks in the centre of Oslo. Time limits for parking outside range from 15 minutes to 9 hours. Long-time parking in multi-storey car parks. Holders of Oslo Cards may park free. However, the time limits are the same as for those who pay.

THE OSLO TOLL ROAD SYSTEM
The toll plazas are located in a ring around Oslo. The passholder lanes (blue signs) are always located on the left. These lanes may only be used by drivers who have purchased toll passes.

The sign "Manual" (grey sign) and "Mynt/Coin" (yellow sign) lanes are used for cash payment.

Trucks, or vehicles without passes and with a total permissible weight of 3500 kgs or more must drive through in the "Manuell" lane.

Motorcycles drive through free.

The Oslo Toll Road System was implemented to help finance the development of the main road system in the Oslo and Akershus area. A total of 50 new road construction projects within the next 15 years. Rates applicable as of 01.01.95.

Most petrol stations have self-service. Most stations have automatic pumps that are in service 24 hours a day and accept bills payment.

To and from Oslo

Oslo is the junction for national and international communications. In Oslo roads, air routes, railways and boats from all over Norway meet. Routes from abroad to different parts of Norway, mostly run through Oslo.

OSLO AIRPORT, FORNEBU,
is situated at Snarøya, about 9 km from the city centre and it is Norway's main airport for domestic and international traffic. (Public transport; bus 31 "Snarøya" from National Theatre or Airport Bus (see below). OSLO AIRPORT, GARDERMOEN,

Railway connections

The Norwegian State Railway (NSB) system fans out across the country from Oslo and links up with bus and boat service all over Norway. Visitors may choose first or second class travel in modern and comfortable coaches. For overnight trips, there are first class single compartments and second class double or tree-berth compartments. Public Transportation in Oslo

The public transportation system in Oslo is well developed, comprising subways, trams, local trains, busses and ferries. There is a single fare system and/or discount cards. Free travel with the Oslo-card.

SUBWAY

There are 8 subway lines, converging at Stortinget station. 4 westbound lines run through Majorstuen station and 4 eastbound lines through Tøyen station.

STREETCARS

There are 5 tram lines running through the city east - west.

BUS

There are 20 bus routes operating within the city limits. These busses carry 2-digit numbers. The converging point for the bus routes is Oslo M at Vaterland east of centre. Here you will find all busses operating Oslo and the surrounding areas.

BIKING

Biking is becoming increasingly popular in Norway, and bike rental is possible in many of the towns and cities. You can bike on your own or follow arranged bike tours. Much work is being done to improve biking conditions inside the towns and along the highway. Most places you can find alternative routes with little traffic. For further information contact: Syklistenes Landsforening (SLF), Maridalsveien 60, N-0458 Oslo, tel.: +(47) 22 71 92 93.

FERRIES

There is a ferry connection to Hovedøya and some islands in inner Oslofjord-area, starting at Vippetangen (bus 29) year round. In June-August also to the beaches at Langøyene (islands).

From Rådhusbrygge 3 (City Hall, pier 3) there is a ferry connection to the Bygdøy peninsula April-September. The summer sightseeing boats leave from pier 3.

From the Aker Brygge pier there is a ferry connection to Nesodden all year, and to Håøya island by Drøbak on summer weekends.

TAXI
Approx. 1.700 taxis are ready to service you while in Oslo. The taxi taxes are regulated by the authorities.
