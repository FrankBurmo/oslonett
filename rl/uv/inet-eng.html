Route 40 - The “Silver Road”

The stretch between Larvik and Geilo is called the “Silver Road”. The “Silver Road” is a result of the silver mining in Kongsberg where large amounts of silver were transported to Drammen for further processing. The surrounding areas were also strongly influenced by the silver mining industry in Kongsberg, during the years of 1624 - 1957.

If you begin your travel in Larvik and drive north trough Kongsberg along the “Silver Road”, there will be many points of interest along the way. The Numedal Valley has Norway's largest collection of wooden houses from the Middle Ages, with more than 40 preserved buildings dating from 1150 to 1537. You will find “Alfstadloftet” in Rollag, with its runic inscriptions from the 12th century, as well as Norway's oldest occupied wooden house (built before 1350). Numedal Valley also has the largest collection of stave churches in the country, and they are all worth a visit.

Our ancestors have used this valley as the main trade route between eastern and western Norway for 4000 - 5000 years. It has come to be called “Nordmannsslepene” after the wayfarers from the west; the “Nordmenn”. If you enjoy hiking, many people choose to set out from Nore and Uvdal on their hikes across “Hardangervidda”, Northern Europe's largest mountain plateau. There are signposted walking paths and staffed/unstaffed cabins. Eidsfjell, Imingfjell and Dagalifjell also offer excellent hiking terrain. “Hardangervidda” is known for its good fishing. Trout as large as 17 kg have been caught in the Tunhovd fjord!

Did you know that the shortest route between Oslo and Bergen runs through the Numedal Valley? The road standard is good, the traffic is less heavy than the main route which runs through Hallingdal, and there are numerous cultural landmarks and beautiful landscape along the way.
