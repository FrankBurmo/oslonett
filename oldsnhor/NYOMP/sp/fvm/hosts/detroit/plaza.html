HART PLAZA

Background:
Philip A. Hart Plaza with its fountain, sculptures and surrounding lawns, is one of Detroit's favorite outdoor places. The site of the summer outdoor Riverfront Festivals, Hart Plaza offers concerts, riverboat watching and winter ice skating. The Plaza is approximately 11 acres with an additional 3.2 acres located on the underground level.

When the internationally famous sculptor Isamu Noguchi created the Plaza in 1973 he expressed it as "an illusion of the new relationship between man and his environment. It gives them a feeling that they can play in it."
Named in memory of Philip A. Hart, who served as Michigan's U.S. senator from 1959 until prior to his death in December 1976, the Plaza is dedicated to all those who work, live and play in Metropolitan Detroit.

What's Special:
Hart Plaza is the site of several well known special events including the Montreux-Detroit Jazz Festival, the world's largest free jazz festival, the International Freedom Festival fireworks, the world's largest annual display, and various ethnic festivals featuring the music, dance and cuisine of several cultures.

Extra Special:
Hart Plaza's Dodge Fountain, the area's centerpiece, can send more than one million gallons of water per hour into the air in various configurations through its 300-plus nozzles and jets.
At the entrance of the Plaza is a spiral like structure known as the "Pylon." The 120-foot steel and aluminum art work has a twisted look which is meant to resemble the deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) molecules we are all made of.

Details:
Hart Plaza is located in the center of Detroit's Civic Center, just west of the Renaissance Center.
