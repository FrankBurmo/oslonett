FOX THEATRE

Background:
Anchoring Detroit's revitalized Theatre District, the Fox Theatre is the largest movie theater in the nation. Built in 1928 by William Fox, the theatre was designed after an Arabian tent. In 1988, the Fox underwent an $8.1-million restoration. The theatre orginally cost $10 million. Since the restoration, the Fox has become the most successful theatre operation of its kind in the nation programming Broadway musicals, concerts, film epics and special events.

What's Special:
The Exotic Siamese Byzantine architecture (a masterful blend of Persian, Burmese, Indian, Siamese and Chinese) boasts gold-leaf, hand-stencilled walls, leather-lined elevators, 300,000 sparkling glass jewels embedded in decorative figures, a sunburst ceiling, brass ornamentation and marble-finished pillars. The lobby is six stories high and contains a grand chandelier stretching 13 feet and weighing two tons. The restoration brought back 5,000 original velvet seats and 5,000 yards of carpet embedded with gold tusked elephant heads. Golden ornaments cover the walls and ceilings, along with an array of niches, balconies, stairways, borders and scrolls, mirrored glass and marble pillars.
The winged lion has come to symbolize the vigilance and strength of the Fox Theatre. Two massive figures can be found at the entrance of the elegant theatre.
Visitors can spot the theatre from the street because of its multi-colored neon marquee. The marquee stretches 125 feet, up to the 10th floor of the Fox Building.

Details:
