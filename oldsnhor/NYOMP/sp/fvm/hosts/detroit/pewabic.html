PEWABIC POTTERY

Background:
Founded in 1903 by Mary Chase Perry and Horace J. Caulkins, Pewabic Pottery is nationally renowned for its handcrafted ceramic vessels and architectural tiles and its unique glazes. Originally located in a stable/carriage house on the corner of Alfred and John R Street, the Pottery moved in 1907 to its present East Jefferson Avenue facility, a charming Tudor Revival building designed by architect William B. Stratton for Miss Perry, whom he later married. Today, Pewabic Pottery is a non profit ceramic arts center and "living" museum that continues to produce handcrafted vessels and architectural tiles for public and private installations. In 1991, the Pottery was designated a National Historic Landmark.

What's Special:
The Pottery's gift shop offers a wide range of vases, candlesticks, unique embossed tiles, and other items fabricated on site, and its galleries exhibit and sell works by emerging and established ceramists from all across the country.

Activities:
The Pottery also offers a broad range of educational programs for children and adults and maintains its historic archives and collections for use by historians and other researchers.
Visitors may take self-guided tours of the Pottery's kiln room and other production areas during regular business hours. Guided group tours are available (for a nominal fee) by appointment.

Extra Special:
Four of the 13 People Mover stations are adorned with ceramic murals created at Pewabic Pottery. Other examples of Pewabic tile installations can be found in homes, churches, and public buildings (such as the Guardian Building in Detroit) throughout the Metropolitan Detroit area and all across the country.
