GREEKTOWN

Background:
Around World War I, a thriving Greek community had as its nucleus a prospering one block section of Monroe Street. This area is known today as Greektown, one of the most popular areas to visit in Detroit. Included in this ethnic neighborhood is a collection of bakeries, restaurants, bars, markets and coffeehouses.
The anchor of this district is St. Mary's Roman Catholic Church, built in 1865 to serve the immigrants. Many of the descendants still worship here.

What's Special:
In 1985, Trappers Alley Festival Marketplace debuted with five levels of restaurants, specialty shops and nightspots in the former fur pelting processing company of German immigrant Traugot Schmidt. Trappers Alley, renamed "The Alley," has been recognized internationally for its innovative design.
A People Mover station opened adjacent to Trappers Alley in 1987, providing a popular way to get to Greektown from various parts of downtown.
In 1988, International Marketplace, a $20 million, 220,000 mixed retail and office complex opened. The Marketplace features a 103-foot solid marble water fountain, the world's largest indoor fountain and the popular Fishbone's Rhythm Kitchen Cafe specializing in Cajun food. In July 1992, the luxurious, 175-suite Atheneum Suite Hotel & Conference Center opened in Greektown.

Extra Special:
Each year in May, the Greektown Merchant's Association sponsors the Greektown Art Fair. The streets come alive with artisans and merchants displaying unique wares from across the United States and abroad. The 1994 event is scheduled for May 6, 7 and 8.

Details:
Greektown is located on Monroe Street in downtown Detroit. The area is organized by the Greektown Merchants Association, (313) 963-3357.
