THE MICHIGAN OPERA THEATRE

Background:
Founded in 1971 and directed by internationally recognized impresario David DiChiera, The Michigan Opera Theatre is the state of Michigan's premiere opera company serving as a state-wide cultural resource committed to producing the best professional productions from the grand opera, operetta, musical theater and, most recently, ballet repertory.
In addition to a main stage repertory ranging from the comedy of Mozart to the drama of Verdi and the verismo of Sondeim, the company boasts the presentation of neglected works that have been rewarded with national PBS telecasts.

What's Special:
The company's philosophy of offering young aspiring artists performance opportunities is well regarded, and special recognition has come for its role in the emergence of such outstanding American singers as Carmen Balthrop, Kathleen Battle, Rockwell Blake and Leona Mitchell.
The Michigan Opera Theatre has purchased the Grand Circus Theatre, a 1922 movie palace designed by C. Howard Crane, as its permanent home. Renovation and expansion has begun on the 2,700-seat facility which will open for performances in the fall of 1995.
The Michigan Opera Theatre produces a season of five main stage productions. The Fall Season at the 2,100-seat Fisher Theatre features two productions from the opera, operetta/or musical repertory. The spring season at the 4,300-seat Masonic Temple Theatre features two grand operas and a full-length classical ballet.
