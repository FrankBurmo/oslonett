HOLOCAUST MEMORIAL CENTER

Background:
The Holocaust Memorial Center is the country's first memorial dedicated exclusively to the horrors of the Nazis' persecution of Jews and others. Rabbi Charles Rosenzveig developed the memorial in 1984 to be a historical, educational institute that would introduce visitors to Jewish culture and let them witness its destruction.

What's Special:
The hall depicting the central events of the Holocaust is titled "The Unbelievable." It includes rare documentary films and a memorial for the "Righteous Gentiles" who helped hide and protect Jews.
Small theaters have continuous video footage of Jews being deported to labor camps, concentration camps and death camps. The museum includes Nazi propaganda, uniforms, photographs, yellow stars and authentic identification papers.
A memorial flame burns for those who were murdered in the Holocaust. It sits near a panel that lists the names of European Jewish communities that were destroyed.
The exhibition ends with a video game called Life Chance. The player identifies with a young Jew in Germany and is given a series of choices--where you live, what you do, who you marry. Almost every choice, inevitably, leads to destruction.

Extra Special:
"The Heritage Connection," the center's community retrieval service, now gives visitors a printout highlighting the history of every European Jewish community of 500 families or larger. This is the first such service available in the U.S.
The center also houses the Morris & Emma Schaver Library-Archive, one of the most comprehensive collections of Holocaust materials in the United States.
