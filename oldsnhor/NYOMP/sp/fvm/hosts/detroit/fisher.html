THE FISHER MANSION

Background:
Lawrence Fisher, founder of the Fisher Body Company and Cadillac Motors, built his home on the Detroit riverfront in 1927. More than 200 expert craftsman from all over Europe came to work on this masterpiece. The Fisher Mansion features original works of art from all over the world, from Italian marble fireplaces to walnut pillars. It features original works of art from Asia and India, a blend of Italian Renaissance and Vintage Hollywood architecture, and more than 75 ounces of pure gold and 140 ounces of silver leaf highlight its ceilings and moldings.
The Fisher Mansion was purchased in 1975 by Alfred Brush Ford (great grandson of Henry Ford) and Elisabeth Reuther Dickmeyer (daughter of UAW president Walter Reuther) and now operates as the Bhaktivedanta Cultural Center in addition to being open for viewing as part of the Auto Baron Homes Tour.

What's Special:
The Mansion is also home to Govinda's, a vegetarian restaurant offering international cuisine (and known worldwide for its authentic tastes). The Mansion can be reached from the Detroit River. Boat docking facilities are also available.
