THE DETROIT ZOO

Background:
Open year 'round and situated on 125 acres, The Detroit Zoo houses more than 1,200 animals from 286 different species. Opened in 1928, it was the first Zoo in the United States to make extensive use of barless exhibits. Indoor exhibits such as the penguinarium, reptile, bird and the elephant/rhinoceros house are favorites, particularly in the winter months.

Visitors to the zoo can take a guided tour of the beautifully landscaped Zoo grounds aboard the miniature railroad that carries passengers to and from the Main Station near the park entrance and the Africa Station at the farthest corner of the park.
In 1993, The Detroit Zoo saw a record 1.5 million visitors, a dramatic increase from normal attendance, due in part from its special Dinosaria Exhibit. The Zoo normally attracts roughly one million people on an annual basis.

What's Special:
The Detroit Zoo exhibits the "Chimps of Harambee." The seven and a half million dollar exhibit encompasses four acres of the Zoo property and features a two and one half acre naturalistic chimpanzee habitat surrounded by a dry moat on the north side with indoor viewing, and a water moat on the south side for outdoor viewing.

Extra Special:
Detroit's Zoological Parks Commission also operates the Belle Isle Zoo, a smaller 13- acre facility featuring a 3/4 mile-long elevated walkway. Visitors may view exotic and native animals in their various exhibits. The Belle Isle Zoo is open April through October.
