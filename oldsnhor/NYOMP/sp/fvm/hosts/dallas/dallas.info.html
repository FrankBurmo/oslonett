Areas of Interest

West End Historic District
Enter at Market and Elm and stroll down brick-lined streets enhanced by turn-of-the-century offices, warehouses and a brewery that include diverse boutiques, restaurants and clubs. Just south of the West End entry is the Old Red 1891 Courthouse, the John F. Kennedy Memorial Plaza, Dealy Plaza, and the Texas School Book Depository which houses the Sixth Floor Exhibit, an interpretation of the life and assassination of President Kennedy which took place at the site.

Dallas Arts District
Tour the Dallas Museum of Art and the sculpture garden of the Trammell Crow Center which features 22 French bronzes highlighted by the works of Rodin. Visit the Morton H. Meyerson Symphony Center, home of the Dallas Symphony Orchestra.

Financial Center
In Downtown Dallas see the Magnolia Building topped by the landmark red neon Pegasus. Look up at the NationsBank Plaza rising 72 stories and outlined at night with over two miles of green argon. Shop at the orig, then pause at Thanks-Giving Square to admire the spiral-shaped chapel with its stained-glass ceiling. Perhaps the biggest treat is beneath the city's street - three miles of walkways accessing 250 shops and restaurants.

Old City Park
Step into the mid-19th century life-styles of Texas as you walk through an authentic prairie-style country store, blacksmith shed, church, bank, home, and even a school house in Old City Park.

Civic Center
Tour some of the city's landmarks: the Dallas Convention Center and I.M. Pei's futuristic architectural masterpiece, Dallas' City Hall.

Reunion
Behind Union Station and accessible by an underground walkway are the Hyatt Regency and Reunion Tower, an international landmark. Ride in a glass enclosed elevator 50 stories to the geodesic dome on top where an observation deck and revolving restaurant offer a spectacular view of the entire area.

Deep Ellum
In the 1920's it featured jazz joints and rhythm and blues, today it steps to a different beat. In transformed low-rent office and warehouses each of downtown, Deep Ellum hosts an eclectic collection of art galleries, restaurants, and clubs where you can hears bands perform everything from rock to reggae, from country to heavy metal.

Fair Park
Seven major museums are located in this scenic 277-acre park: Museum of Natural History, Science Place I and II, Museum of African-American Life and Culture, Dallas Aquarium, Dallas Civic Garden Center, Hall of State, and the Age of Steam Railroad Museum. Sports spectacles, concerts, and other major happenings are staged at the Cotton Bowl Stadium, Coliseum, Music Hall, Bank Shell, StarPlex Amphitheater, and the Margo Jones Theatre. Fair Park also features the nation's largest collection of art deco buildings, erected in 1936 for the Texas Centennial Celebration.

STRONG>Six Flags Over Texas

Interstate 30 at Highway 360, Arlington, (817) 640-8900
Theme park based on the six nations that have governed Texas: U.S., Spain, Mexico, France, Confederacy, and Republic of Texas. Enjoy rides, shows, concerts, games and restaurants.

Biblical Arts Center
7500 Park Lane at Boedeker, (214) 691-4661
Home of the 124 foot x 20 foot mural featuring 200 biblical characters. Shown with a dramatic 30-minute light and sound presentation. Includes the replica of Christ's Garden Tomb at Calvary and early Christian-era architecture. Tuesday-Saturday, 10am-5pm, Sunday 1pm-5pm.

Museum of African-American Life and Culture
Fair Park, (214) 565-9026
111 S. Michigan Avenue (312) 443-3600
Explore the African-American experience through numerous exhibits and displays, including historical artifacts, art and folk art, and a black religious center.

Dallas Arboretum and Botanical Garden
8525 Garland Road, (213) 327-8263
Sixty-six acres of rolling laws and blooming gardens on the shore of White Rock Lake.

Dallas World Aquarium
1801 N. Griffin Street, (214) 720-2224
Features sharks, stingrays, and hundreds of reef fish all in living coral reef ecosystems. Open Monday-Friday, 11am-6pm, Saturday 10am-6pm, Sunday 12pm-ypm. Admission for adults $5 nd for children $3.

Dallas Zoo
621 East Clarendon, (214) 946-5154
Over 1,400 animals, including rare and endangered species. The 25-acre Wilds of Africa exhibit, features mile-long monorail, nature trail, African plaza, gorilla conservation center, and animals roaming freely in natural habitats. Every day 9am-5pm. Admission $5 adults, $4 senior, $2.50 children.

Deep Ellum
2622 Commerce Street, (214) 748-4332
Offers 25 restaurants, 42 retail shops, and 16 nightspots featuring music from jazz/lues to heavy metal. This is a place for new music talent, which gave birth to Vanilla Ice, Edie Brickell, and the New Bohemians.

Southfork Ranch

3700 Hogge Road, Parker, (214) 442-7800
Southern Hospitality to make you feel right at home. While longhorns and horses graze in the background, you can tour the Ewing Mansion and Ranch made famous by J.R., East a bite at Miss Ellie's Deli...and take home some unique western wear. Just north of Dallas, call for information.

Galleria
13350 Dallas Parkway
Spectacular shopping experience. Surround yourself with over 200 of the world's most celebrated stores like Tiffany & Co., Gucci, Louis Vitton, and stores such as Macy's, Marshall Field's and Saks Fifth Avenue. Feast in nineteen restaurants. Ice skate under a breathtaking, vaulted skylight. Enjoy first-run movies in five cinemas.
