Marketplaces

Though shopping the neighborhoods is a favorite San Francisco pastime, the lures of retail and restaurant complexes scattered throughout the city are well documented, too. There are storehouses of serendipity along the northern waterfront, oases in the financial district and landscaped malls steeped in history or fresh from the architect's drafting table. The best known are profiled here:

Embarcadero Center

 A virtual city within The City, the internationally acclaimed Embarcadero Center is a popular destination for San Francisco visitors and residents alike. This spectacular eight-block complex, set on three levels of open air, tree-lined plazas, was designed by famed architect John C. Portman, Jr., and developed in partnership with David Rockefeller and Associates, and The Prudential.

Interconnected by pedestrian walkways and lined with ultra chic restaurants and outdoor cafes, Embarcadero Center has it all. From fashionable shops and boutiques, to the renowned Hyatt Regency San Francisco with its dramatic 17-story atrium lobby and revolving rooftop restaurant to the luxurious Park Hyatt hotel, Embarcadero Center has something for everyone.

Embarcadero Center extends from the Park Hyatt at Clay and Battery Streets to the Hyatt Regency at Drumm and Sacramento, just steps from the Embarcadero BART and Muni stations. Free validated parking is available evenings and weekends.

Ghirardelli Square

 Although its oldest building -- the Woolen Mill -- dates back to 1864 (Civil War uniforms were made there), Ghirardelli Square is renowned for its years as a chocolate factory. The Domingo Ghirardelli family produced chocolate here from 1893 until the early 1960s. Some of the original vats and ovens are still in operation at the Ghirardelli Chocolate Manufactory on the plaza level of the Clock Tower. In 1964, after several years of renovation and restoration, Ghirardelli Square became the country's first manufacturing complex preserved and renovated into a retail/restaurant center. Today Ghirardelli Square is a favorite spot for visitors and San Franciscans alike. In addition to fabulous views of the Bay, it boasts more than 70 shops and award-winning restaurants. The Square's West Plaza stage and two-tiered bandstand regularly host a variety of free entertainment. The Square occupies the block bounded by North Point, Polk, Beach and Larkin on the northern waterfront.

San Francisco Shopping Centre

 At the center there's the San Francisco Shopping Centre. At Market and Fifth Streets, just three blocks from Union Square, the nine-story, $140 million vertical mall officially opened in October 1988. It made retailing history with a daring ``store-in-the-sky'' on the top five floors and six spiral escalators, a Japanese innovation which made its U.S. debut here. Over 100 establishments purveying California-style panache fill nine levels of shopping. Designed by the local architectural firm of Whisler-Patri, the center makes extensive use of hand-quarried Italian marble, polished green granite and sparkling glass. A retractable skylight covers a 150-foot-high atrium. An entrance to the Powell Street BART/Muni station is at the concourse level; topside the cable car turnaround and Muni buses are at hand.

PIER 39

 PIER 39 is a waterfront festival marketplace. Among the city's most visited attractions, PIER 39 houses two levels of outstanding restaurants, 100 one-of-a-kind shops, a 350-berth marina, waterfront park and the Blue & Gold Fleet. Entertainment includes the Breyers Venetian Carousel; Turbo Ride, the ultimate thrill adventure, and a complete family amusement area. As many as 600 sea lions have taken up residence on the pier's K-dock. Located just two blocks east of Fisherman's Wharf, the 45-acre complex draws millions of visitors year-round with its unparalleled views of the bay. A pedestrian bridge connects this specialty center to a 1,000-car garage.

Union Square

 Someone once praised Union Square as one of the world's most remarkable ``outdoor living rooms.'' Pull up a chair, the company's swell. The hub of San Francisco's downtown is strictly uptown. It's Fifth Avenue, Rodeo Drive, State Street and local success stories all in one. Elegant stores and richly appointed shops cater to every taste and do it with style. Within a four-block radius shoppers can purchase designer sheets or sheet music, golden retrievers or golden caviar. Window displays approach state-of-the-art, whether it's the coy charms of Maiden Lane or the high tech extravaganzas of the major tenants. Add to these a palette of posies brimming from sidewalk flower stands which brighten every corner. Serious shoppers concentrate on the blocks bounded by Geary, Powell, Post and Stockton Streets and surrounding blocks between Market and Sutter Streets, Kearny to Mason.

The Anchorage

 Located in the heart of Fisherman's Wharf, The Anchorage Shopping Center is a one-stop destination for unique shopping, dining and lodging. This discovery in Fisherman's Wharf is within walking distance of San Francisco Bay, fresh Dungeness crab stands and the cable cars. The Anchorage's gleaming, contemporary design is bedecked with flags and banners that add to its nautical flair. Outdoor promenades and decks offer an abundance of bay view vantage points. Visitors will find tantalizing snacks, local mementos, galleries, restaurants and an array of apparel, leather, jewelry, craft and gift boutiques. In the courtyard musicians, magicians and other street performers entertain frequently. An impressive two-story anchor sculpture is the focal point of the flower-filled Leavenworth Street Plaza. The 2.6-acre site is bounded by Jefferson, Beach, Leavenworth and Jones Streets. Validated parking available.

The Cannery

 Built in 1906 The Cannery was once a Del Monte peach cannery. These historic buildings were renovated in the late 60s with three levels of walkways, balconies and bridges, all wrapped around an inviting courtyard. Here one can relax under 100-year-old olive trees and have an alfresco snack or an elegant meal while being entertained by street performers. Ride the outdoor elevator past nearly a century of San Francisco history and discover a variety of shops and galleries filled with the latest in objects and fashions so new that they create a fashion of their own. Live entertainment is featured daily and The Cannery offers one of the finest comedy clubs in the city. Just one-half block from the Hyde Street cable car turnaround, The Cannery is located at the corner of Leavenworth and Beach.

Crocker Galleria

 Located near Union Square on the block bounded by Post, Kearny, Sutter and Montgomery Streets, the Crocker Galleria encompasses more than 50 fine shops, restaurants and services under a spectacular glass dome. Modeled after Milan's vast Galleria Vittorio Emmanuelle, the three-level pavilion features elegant boutiques offering the best of American and European designers, ``one-of-a-kind'' collectibles and treasures from near and far. Enjoy a quiet rest or a picnic lunch in two rooftop gardens, both accessible from the Galleria. Parking is available near the Sutter Street entrance.

Stonestown Galleria

 Enter a world of vaulted glass skylights and Italian Renaissance marble. Stonestown Galleria offers a sophisticated mix of shops filled with exciting fashion, inspired gifts, distinctive home decor, international foods, plus a number of services including a post office, banks, movie theater, medical offices and a market. Conveniently located in San Francisco's Sunset District at 19th Avenue and Winston Drive, Stonestown Galleria offers plenty of free parking and is easily accessible by taking the ``M'' streetcar line from downtown.

Japan Center

 Stretching for three square blocks bounded by Post, Geary, Laguna and Fillmore Streets, the Japan Center opened in 1968. Designed by one of America's foremost architects, Minoru Yamasaki, the five-acre complex houses restaurants, sushi bars, art galleries, bookstores, hotels, theaters, convention facilities, Japanese baths and shops offering intriguing wares from the Orient.

The Buchanan Mall leading to the center's main entrance is graced by flowering plum and cherry trees, fountains and a river of stones resembling a meandering mountain stream.

The Center -- and the surrounding blocks of Japantown -- is the site of the colorful spring Cherry Blossom Festival and several smaller festivals.

Rincon Center

 Once the Rincon Annex Post Office, this San Francisco historical landmark has been transformed into one of the city's most spectacular eating and shopping places. The 1930s Deco-style post office lobby has been preserved and murals by Russian-born artist Anton Refregier meticulously restored. A soaring skylit atrium has at its focal point a dramatic 90-foot continuously cascading water column. Over 30 shops and some of San Francisco's finest restaurants surround the atrium courtyard. Conveniently located near the Ferry Building and bounded by Mission, Howard, Steuart and Spear Streets, Rincon Center has ample parking available at the Rincon Center Garage.
