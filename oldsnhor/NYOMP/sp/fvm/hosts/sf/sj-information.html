San Jose - Capital of Silicon Valley

For decades, end-users in this forward-thinking city have been developing ways to interface, network and electronically link with other users around the world. Yet, this focus on the technology of the future does not dim San Jose's rich and venerable history.

San Jose's Historic Firsts

 California's oldest civil settlement under Spanish rule, El Pueblo de San José de Guadalupe was founded in 1777 to raise crops and cattle to feed soldiers encamped at the presidios of Monterey and San Francisco. San Jose is also known as California's first state capital, with the first legislature convening in a two-story adobe at the corner of Market Street and Paseo de San Antonio in December 1849. In March 1850, the settlement officially became the City of San Jose. Today, San Jose has emerged from its agricultural origins to reign as the ``Capital of Silicon Valley,'' known throughout the world for its rich harvest of innovative technology.

Location, Location, Location!

San Jose's central location between San Francisco to the north and Monterey/Carmel to the south makes the Capital of Silicon Valley a gateway to adventures throughout California. Within minutes, you can be relaxing on a picturesque beach along the Pacific Coast, hiking through scenic recreation areas or marveling at the majestic California redwoods.

Information Centers

Information about San Jose is easy to obtain by contacting or visiting one of the city's three Visitor Information Centers. Visitors arriving at the San Jose International Airport will find two information centers. One is located in Terminal C at the entrance to the baggage claim area, and the second is situated in Terminal A in the baggage claim area. In addition to answering any questions you may have about the San Jose area, the professional staff at the Visitor & Business Center located in the San Jose McEnery Convention Center in downtown San Jose will assist you with free brochures, calendar of events, dining and lodging reservation services, business supplies and services, San Jose souvenirs, and much more.

Driving Laws

 California state law requires use of seat belts and infant seats for small children in all private vehicles. The state has a very strict drunk-driving law. If there are no limits posted, maximum speed limit is 25 mph on surface streets and 55 mph on California highways. International drivers must possess a valid drivers license from their country and a current International Drivers License to rent and/or operate an automobile in California.

Electricity

In the U.S. the current is 110 volts A/C and plugs are flat. It is best to buy a converter before entering the United States.

Foreign Currency Exchange

Many major banks and independent currency exchange bureaus will exchange foreign currencies during normal banking hours. Most banks are open from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., Monday through Thursday; 10 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. on Fridays; and 8 a.m. to noon on Saturdays. Very few hotels or shops will accept foreign currency.

Gratuities

Gratuities are not automatically totalled into the bill of fare, however a 15% gratuity is usually added to the bill for large parties. Although voluntary, waiters are customarily awarded a gratuity which is 15% of the bill, increasing to 20% for excellent service. Bartenders average $.50-$1.00 per drink. Taxicab drivers usually receive 15% added to the fare. Tips to doormen, bellmen and skycaps for luggage handling are approximately $.50-$1.00 per bag.

Liquor

The legal age in California for both purchase and consumption of alcoholic beverages is 21, with proof of age required. Most restaurants, bars, nightclubs and pubs are licensed to serve a full line of beverages, while some have permits for beer and wine only. Alcoholic beverages are sold daily from 6 a.m. to 2 a.m.

Taxicabs

Rates are $1.80 at flagdrop; then an additional $1.80 per mile (approximately 30 cents each minute). For passengers hiring taxis at San Jose International Airport, the flagdrop is $3.55.

Telephone

Within most cities in Santa Clara County, callers do not need to dial 408 area code; however, to reach San Francisco, the Peninsula and Marin County, callers must dial 415 area code. For communities in the East Bay, including Oakland, Berkeley and Fremont, use the 510 area code. The basic price for a local telephone call is $.20. If you are not sure whether or not you are calling a local number, instructions will be given on how much to deposit after dialing the telephone number.

San Jose Transportation

San Jose International Airport is among the world's 39 busiest airports and is used by more than seven million passengers annually. The West Coast Hub for American Airlines, San Jose International is served by 11 major airlines, three commuter services.

Located only three miles from downtown, San Jose International Airport opened a state-of-the-art 16-gate terminal in 1991. A new long-term master plan for future development is expected to be finalized in 1994.

A state-of-the-art, 20-mile-long Light Rail Transit System ranges from South San Jose's residential and shopping areas to Paramount's Great America theme park in Santa Clara to the north. A network of buses links San Jose with other Santa Clara County destinations.

Charming historic trolleys share a portion of the Light Rail line, circling downtown accommodations, restaurants, nightlife, attractions, and shopping.

Accommodations

The City of San Jose has approximately 4,800 guest rooms, and some 10,000 guest rooms throughout the region. A total of 1,449 guest rooms are within a three-block radius of the San Jose McEnery Convention Center. San Jose's full-service convention hotels include: The Fairmont, Hotel De Anza, Holiday Inn-Park Center Plaza, Hyatt San Jose, Le Baron Hotel, Radisson Plaza Hotel, Red Lion San Jose, San Jose Hilton & Towers, and the Hotel Sainte Claire.
