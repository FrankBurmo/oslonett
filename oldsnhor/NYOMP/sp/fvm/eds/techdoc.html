Technical Synopsis

Electronic Data Systems Corporation
World Cup USA 1994

Overview and Scope

The 1994 World Cup will be hosted by nine cities across America. Twenty-four teams will compete in 52 games over a period of 30 days, from June 18th to July 17th. More than 25,000 volunteers will complement the staff of World Cup USA 1994 in Boston, Chicago, Dallas, Detroit, Los Angeles, New York, Orlando, San Francisco and Washington, D.C. More than 15,000 journalists will visit the United States to report the championship tournaments for the world's most popular sport.

EDS, the Official Information Technology Provider for World Cup USA 1994, has teamed with Sun Microsystems and Sprint to provide the most technologically advanced World Cup in history. EDS has provided World Cup USA 1994 with a broad range of information technology services including consulting, software development, systems integration and management, process design and project management. EDS is the largest provider of human resources to the World Cup technology effort, staffing the nine venue cities with more than one hundred engineers and technicians supporting many key functions including accreditation, security, logistics and telecommuncations.

Software Development

For World Cup USA 1994, EDS has developed a powerful set of applications to manage critical components of World Cup operations. These Event Management Applications (EMA) include Accreditation and Security systems, the World Cup News Service (WCNS) and logistics-support applications to manage Inventory, Staffing and Protocol.

The Accreditation system creates security identification badges, maintains information about accredited individuals and monitors the use of the badges. Accreditation badges consists of a full-color photograph of an individual, various color and character codes indicating where the individual is permitted to be, and a bar-code which may be scanned at a security checkpoint to control access. It is estimated that more than 100,000 people will be accredited by the system. This number includes all people at the stadiums who are not ticket-holders as well as World Cup staff and venue office personnel.

The accreditation badge production operation consists of having the individual stand or sit before a video camera which captures a still image of the individual. The camera equipment and a photographic printer are both connected to a workstation. The image is recorded digitally and stored in the system's database. Additional information about the person is entered and their access privileges are defined using the workstation's access to the database. When all the information has been entered, the badge is printed.

The World Cup News Service (WCNS) is a source for statistical and biographical information for all World Cup tournaments since the first World Cup in 1930. The principal user-base for WCNS is the press. Print and broadcast journalists at each venue will have immediate access to historical statistics and up-to-the-minute results of World Cup 1994. In addition, past news articles and soccer-related announcements from around the world will be available online.

The 1994 Results, Statistics and Biographies section of WCNS will track the progress of each team through every game of the 1994 World Cup. Spotters at each game will enter all pertinent statistics as they occur, including goals, assists, penalties and substitutions. This information, being stored in the central database, will be immediately accessible to all other workstations. In addition, certain key statistics, such as goals scored, will trigger the sending of messages to the broadcast booths at the stadiums, insuring that broadcast journalists have up-to-the-minute results information from other venues.

WCNS will include a fully-automated query facility for accessing detailed statistics from all previous World Cup tournaments. All official FIFA records from every World Cup tournament can be accessed based on tournament, team, player or statistic e.g. the best scorer, the most tournaments won by a team, the highest scoring game in a tournament,etc.

In addition to WCNS, the official FIFA record book will also be available around the world via the Compuserve network. This subscription-based service will permit access to the World Cup database from virtually any personal computer equipped with a modem and communications software and access to a telephone network. This online service will present in a textual format the same statistical information available graphically through WCNS.

The News and Announcements section of WCNS provides background information for all aspects of world soccer, based on published articles in newspapers or other news services. This information may be available in as many as four languages: English, French, German and Spanish.

The Media Logistics system provides support for the press who will be in attendance at World Cup USA 1994 games. Media representatives are identified to the system by having information about them entered into the database. Seats or sections at each stadium may be reserved for use by the press through the system. In addition, other press requirements including telecommunications equipment, accomodations, and travel may be specified in the system.

The Inventory system provides complete inventory management for World Cup USA 1994. Information pertaining to all of World Cup's physical assets e.g. computers, office furniture, telecommunications equipment and merchandise may be entered into the database along with a digitized full-color photograph of the item. For World Cup merchandise available for sale, an item's sale price, stock-on-hand and reorder quantity may be tracked.

The Staffing system provides for the management of the more than 25,000 World Cup staff volunteers. Each volunteer is identified in the database, along with information about the person describing their work experience and skills, especially foreign-language skills. This information is useful in identifying the most qualified volunteers for those positions or assignments which require specialized knowledge, such as greeting or providing transportation services for a visiting foreign party.

The Protocol system assists in the support of visiting dignitaries who will be attending the World Cup games. VIP's are identified in the database along with their protocol requirements such as whether they require full or part-time escorts and what level of gift, if any, they may receive.

In addition to the systems described above, EDS has also integrated a full suite of Office Management Applications (OMA) for World Cup USA 1994. A single graphical menu directs World Cup personnel to access standard off-the-shelf packages including word processing, spreadsheet and electronic mail applications.

All of the information described above supporting the EMA systems resides in a relational database management system (RDBMS) provided by Sybase, Inc. Sybase's database products provide state-of-the-art database technology including automated data replication accross the network and full support for embedded SQL (structure query language) access from applications. The primary World Cup database is located at EDS' Plano, Texas headquarters. Backup database servers containing replicated database images are located at other geographic locations.

In addition to providing the database technology, Sybase has also provided the graphical development environment and object-oriented database used to develop the EMA systems. Known as GAIN Momentum, this development toolkit provides programmers with graphical setting to rapidly develop applications. Graphic objects including buttons, icons, lists and bitmaps are drawn on application "pages." Event-handling code is then written and attached to these graphical object to provide the "programming" aspect of the application. GAIN Momentum interfaces with the Sybase SQL server through a set of function primitives that establish server connections, open queries and retrieve result rows.

Hardware Platforms

The workstations and servers used by World Cup USA 1994 are provided by Sun Microsystems, Inc. The basic workstation configuration consists of either the Sun Sparcstation 10 or Sun Sparc IPX. The operating system used is SunOS 4.1.3 ( Solaris 1 ). As mentioned above, the workstations used for accreditation require additional camera and photographic production equipment. Also, accreditation checkpoints will employ wireless bar-code scanners to transmit scanned information to the workstation.

A total of approximately 1,000 workstations will comprise the World Cup USA 1994 computer network. Of these, approximately 40 will be specially equipped to provide full-motion video and audio playback of game highlights and player and team interviews. These 40 multi-media "kiosk"-based systems will contain video adapter cards providing JPEG decompression and playback of video and audio. In addition these 40 multi-media kiosks will be equipped with touch-screen video monitors. The kiosks will be deployed in four of the nine venue cities: Dallas, Los Angeles, New York and San Francisco.

Two Sun Sparcstation 2000 servers are being used in Plano, Texas as the primary and backup database serviers. In addition, the Sybase Replication Server is deployed on a Sun Sparcstation 10.

Network

The network developed by EDS for World Cup USA 1994 is one of the ten largest private data networks ever installed. The network is a full, class-B, private data network consisting of T-1 (1.544 Megabits per second ) data circuits connecting all nine venues. The network utilizes digital fiber-optic cabling owned and operated by Sprint Communications, Inc. The network utilizes an Ethernet topology running TCP/IP protocols. Connecting the various network segments are multi-protocol routers in each venue city. Furthermore, the network is fully redundant, providing multiple data pathways to each venue. Typically, within each venue city, the stadium, host hotels, and venue offices will be interconnected. In addition, the network also is accessible from FIFA headquarters in Zurich, Switzerland, the United States Soccer Federation headquarters, the Internation Broadcast Center (IBC) in Dallas, Texas, press centers in each venue and World Cup USA 1994 headquarters in Los Angeles. In total, more than 50 Local Area Networks (LANs) will be interconnected. In addition, to support the deployment of full-motion video and audio playback in four venue cities, selected network segements will be upgraded to fractional DS3, the equivalent of six concurrent T-1 lines, using Asynchronous Transfer Mode (ATM) technology.
