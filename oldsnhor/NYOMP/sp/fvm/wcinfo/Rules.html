Rules of the Game

Some new rules instituted for World Cup USA '94 and some of the game's other key rules.

New Rules

* Tackles From Behind: If an attacking player with the ball is tackled - a sliding attempt to steal the ball - directly from behind and, in the referee's discreation, the defender wasn't playing the ball, the defender will be ejected. Previously, ejection was not automatic.

* Deliberate Foul: A player who, in the referee's opinion, deliberately commits a foul to prevent a goal is ejected. Previously, the rule did not call for automatic ejection.

* Everyone suits up: Teams will be allowed to dress all 22 players, six more than in the past. Officials figured it better to have players on the sideline than in the stands exposed to the fans.

* Substitutions: In addition to the previously allowed two substitutions per match, teams can change goalkeepers without the move counting against the limit.

* Back Pass: A goalkeeper can't pick up a ball in the penalty area that has been kicked to him by a teammate. The goalie can collect the ball with his feet or use his hands to stop the ball and he can pick up a ball in the penalty area after it was passed to him off the head or chest of a teammate. If the goalie does pick up a ball in the penalty area after it was kicked by a teammate, the opposing team will receive an inderect free kick from the point of the infraction. The rule was made to reduce delay tactics by the team on defense.

* Standing Points: Three points for a win, one for a tie in first-round matches. Previously, it was two points for a win and one for a tie. The intention is to discourage teams from playing for ties in the opening round. Italy won the '82 Cup after advancing from the first round with three draws.

* Yellow-Card Limit: Yellow Cards - cautions for serious fouls - received in the first round no longer count on a player's record in the second round. Otherwise, the rule holds, where a player receiving a second yellow card in the first round or two yellow cards in subsequent rounds will sit out the next game.

* Video Review: A 14-member dicipline committee will review videos of matches, if necessary, and could reverse a red-card -- ejections for flagrant fouls -- or yellow-card decision. It also could suspend a player for a flagrant foul that was not called during the game. After a red card, teams must play short-handed.

* Officials' Duties: Referees will no longer have veto power over a linesman's call.

* Offside: An attacking player must have two defenders between him and the goal when a pass is made by a teammate. This infraction no longer will be called when a referee or a linesman is in doubt, as was often the case in the past. The offside rule now states that if an offensive player has not passed one of the two defenders (including the goalkeeper) when pass is made by a teammate, the play is legal. If a player is in an offside position but is not considered in the play, it also is legal.

Forwards

Forwards score the most goals and are chosen for their speed and shooting skills.

  • Wings: They attack by penetrating or making crossing passes to teammates.
  • Striker: The chief scorer, he must be able to gain possession, control the ball and shoot under pressure.

  • Advanced Forward: Usually stays deep in the opposition's territory and usually is the forward who is strong, fast and with a good shot.
  • Withdrawn Forward: He is expected to drop back and link up the pass from the midfield to the advanced forward. He usually is the more skilled forward who is able to retain possession of the ball.

  • Dual Strikers: One usually is a strong, fast player with a good shot. The other usually is more skilled and able to retain possession of the ball.
Midfielders

Midfielders are the link between the defense and the forwards. They must have exceptional control of the ball to push it correctly to the forwards and must attack and defend.

  • Attacking Midfielder: Plays forward and stalls the opponent by cutting off passes.
  • Defensive Midfielder: Guards the opponent with the ball. On the attack, he receives passes and pushes the ball to forwards.
Defenders (Fullbacks)

Defenders are the last line between the goalkeeper and the opponents. They are the primary defenders of their goal and are responsible for terminating the opponents attack on the goal.

Goalkeeper

The goalkeeper is the only player who can touch the ball with his hands. The goalkeeper should be agile with quick reactions. He may touch the ball with his hands only while he is in the 18 yard penalty box. Once leaving this area, he must abide by the same rules as any other player. A goalkeeper may not use his hands to grab any pass from his teammate if it is made to inside the penalty box. However, if the teammate uses a head or chest pass to send the ball there, the goalkeeper may use his hands.

Penalty Kicks

The goalkeeper must stand on the goal line without moving his feet until the ball is kicked; if he moves the penalty is replayed. the kick is made at close range from a specified kick mark 12 yards from the goal.

Other Key Rules

  • Advantage: A discretionary decision of the referee, whereby he can refrain from stopping play for a foul if the offended team would be penalized in the process. For example, perhaps it is mounting an attack and still has possession of the ball despite the foul.

  • Dives: Since the 1990 Cup, FIFA has been cracking down on players taking dives in feigned agony after minor fouls or non-fouls. The referee can issue a yellow card for a player taking an intentional dive after the tackle. He also can order a player to be treated off the field if he thinks the injury is serious. He can issue a yellow card if the "injured" player is proved to have blatantly feigned injury by then returning to play too quickly.

  • Free Kicks, Direct Free Kicks: A kick awarded after a foul. The ball is played from the spot where the infraction occurred. Serious fouls merit a direct free kick and a shot may be taken directly at the goal. Lesser offenses result in an indirect free kick, and the ball must be touched by a second player before a goal can be scored.

  • Goal Kicks: An indirect free kick for the defending team after the ball has passed over the goal line, and out of play, having last been touched by the attacking side. The kick, which can be taken in any part of the goal area, must be kicked beyond the penalty area.

  • Goalkeeper Steps: The goalkeeper, when in possession of the ball in his hands, is allowed to take only four steps -- all of them in the penalty box -- before he must kick it or throw it.

  • Handball: An infraction in which a player -- other than the goalkeeper when he's inside the penalty area -- intentionally touches the ball with a hand or any part of the arm. The opposing side is awarded a direct free kick. Handballs called against the defense inside the penalty are results in a penalty kick: A one-on-one shot against the goalie from the mark 12 yards out.

  • Injury Time: Allowed at the end of either half to compensate for time lost because of an injury or any other stoppage. The decision to add injury time and how much to add is at the referee's discretion. He is the official timekeeper.

  • Obstruction: When a player, without trying to play the ball, intentionally places his body between an opponent and the ball or otherwise impedes his movement, the opposition is awarded an indirect free kick.

  • Penalty-Kicks Shootout: Tie-breaking procedure for World Cup games after the first round if a deadlock still persists after 30 minutes of extra time. Kicks are taken alternately by each team. If one team makes an insurmountable number before five kicks are completed, the shootout ends. If the shootout is equal after five attempts, the procedure continues until one team has scored more than the other after both have taken an equal number of kicks.

Definitions of the Game

  • Advantage: Rule that lets play continue after a foul, if stopping the action immediately would be disadvantageous to the team that was fouled.

  • Back pass: A pass made to a trailing player.

  • Bicycle kick: Overhead kick, also called a scissors kick.

  • Chip: A high pass over a defender's head to a teammate, or a shot on goal from close range away from the goalie's reach.

  • Corner kick: Method of restarting play after the defending team has cleared the ball over its own goal line. The ball is placed at the point where the sideline and goal line intersect, with the attacking team awarded a free kick that may be shot directly into the goal.

  • Dangerous Play: An action that could cause injury in the opinion of the referee. The offending team loses possession of the ball and the opponent is awarded a free kick from the point of the infraction.

  • Direct free kick: A free kick that can result in a goal.

  • Dribble: To control the ball on the ground with the feet.

  • End line: Boundary lines marking the ends of the field.

  • Far post: Goalpost farthest from the ball.

  • Field: Regulation Soccer field.

  • Formations: 4-3-3, for example, refers to four defenderes, three midfielders and three forwards. Formations are listed from the defense out.

  • Give-and-go: A player making a short pass to a teammate and then receiving the return pass. (Also referred to as the "Wall Pass" or the "Double Pass".)

  • Heading: Using one's head to score, pass or control the ball.

  • Header: The act of heading a ball.

  • Indirect free kick: Free kick that can't result in a goal unless it touches another player first.

  • Injury time: Time added to the end of each half to compensate for stoppages due to injuries, the scoring of a goal or time-wasting.

  • Midfielder: Player who functions primarily in the middle of the field, linking play between forwards and defenders.

  • Near post: Goal post nearest to the ball.

  • Obstruction: Illegally preventing an opponent from playing the ball by blocking their path to the ball. Results in an indirect free kick.

  • Offside: Infraction in which an offensive player does not have at least two defensive players between the offensive player and the goal line when the ball is last played forward by a member of the attacking team.

  • Penalty area: 44-yard by 18-yard area in front of the goal in which the goalkeeper may use their hands.

  • Penalty kick: Direct free kick from 12 yards in front of the goal.

  • Pitch: British term for the playing field.

  • Red card: Card held aloft by the referee to signify that a player is being ejected from the match.

  • Screening (or shielding): When dribbling, staying between the ball and the opponent to protect the ball.

  • Slide tackle: Stripping the ball from a dribbler by making contact with the ball while sliding on the turf.

  • Striker: Player whose main responsibility is scoring.

  • Sweeper: A roving defender who plays between the fullbacks and the goaltender.

  • Tackle: To take the ball away from an opponent by using the feet.

  • Through-pass (or ball): A pass that splits a group of defenders.

  • Throw-in: Method of returning the ball to play after it has gone over the sideline and was last touched by an opponent.

  • Touchline (or sideline): Line marking the side of the field.

  • Trap: To bring the ball under control with any part of the body.

  • Volley: Kicking the ball in or out of mid-air.

  • Wall: Group of defenders standing shoulder-to-shoulder to defend a free kick, usually near the goal.

  • Yellow card: Card held aloft by the referee to warn a player that any further misconduct will cause their ejection from the game.
