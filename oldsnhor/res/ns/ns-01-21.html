NAUTILUS ENCRYPTS VOICE COMMUNICATIONS VIA MODEM

The genie of encryption can't be stuffed back into the intellectual bottle. Nautilus is a program that lets you have encrypted voice telephone conversations using only a PC equipped with a sound card and a modem. It also works on Sun machines. The free beta program (source available) encrypts both ends of the line with a variety of strong algorithms (but not the intellectually protected RSA public key) using some mutually agreed upon key. Since - in the eyes of the US government - this is a deadly weapon, you have to go through a few extra steps to get it, but don't be surprised to see this software on international sites in short order (check on comp.privacy). This is a big deal, with potentially as much impact on your privacy as PGP encryption software. Access: "ftp://ripem.msu.edu/pub/crypt/GETTING_ACCESS" DOS version: "ftp://ripem.msu.edu/pub/crypt/msdos/" Source: "ftp://ripem.msu.edu/pub/crypt/other"

AOL BUYS GNN, WEBCRAWLER

AOL, in a bid to create an independent Internet online service, has acquired GNN for $9 million in stock and $2 million in cash. AOL will use GNN to launch a new commercial Internet service (or "brand" according to the PR) at the end of August. AOL aims to combine their massive marketing machine with ease-of-use access software and the depth of GNN web offerings. Reading between the lines, it sounds like they are out to give Microsoft a run for their money in the Internet services business. In a related development, AOL also bought out WebCrawler, a popular online search tool. Full text of the PR is at the following URL. "http://www.gnn.com/gnn/announce/release.html"

HELP PETITION AGAINST NET CENSORSHIP

As we go to press, the U.S. Senate is getting ready to vote on the ponderous telecom deregulation legislation which includes as a rider the infamous "Exon Communications Decency Act". Fortunately, there is a move afoot to replace the bill's language with an alternative approach offered by Senator Patrick Leahy (D-VT). This would basically mandate Congress to study less intrusive methods to keep kids away from pornography while keeping freedom of speech intact. Take a moment to acquaint yourself with opposing the Exon bill at the URL below. Our readers abroad can also help, so visit this site even if you don't live in the States. At press time the petition had 35,000 signatures already. "http://www.cdt.org/petition.html"

MESSAGE SCREENING MAY MAKE U.S. ONLINE PROVIDERS LIABLE

In a potentially ground-breaking decision, a New York state judge has ruled Prodigy Services Co. is legally a publisher and thus is accountable under libel law. Stratton Oakmont Inc. had sued Prodigy over discussion that took place on a Prodigy bulletin board. Judge Stuart Ain ruled that because Prodigy screens all posts for profanity and bigotry, it is responsible and liable for content. In 1991, a federal judge threw out a libel suit brought against CompuServe, saying that because CompuServe merely delivered the information in question and in no way edited material, it could not be held responsible. Prodigy may appeal Ain's ruling.

SAN JOSE MERCURY NEWS WINS WEB HONORS

Interactive Age's editors recently chose the winners of their 100 Best Business Web Sites contest, based on successful use of the WWW platform. First place was awarded to the Mercury Center Web, an offshoot of the San Jose Mercury News daily (right in our back yard). Interactive Age, a newspaper pertaining to electronic commerce, described the winner as "the undisputed leader in online news services." The publication estimates that there are 10,000 commercial sites on the Web. Among the other businesses receiving accolades were Eastman Kodak Co., Starwave Corp., Time Warner Inc.'s Pathfinder, Digital Equipment Corp, and IBM. Interactive Age: "http://techweb.cmp.com/ia" Mercury Center Web: "http://www.sjmercury.com"

USA TODAY GOES ONLINE

One of the most formidable media machines in the country has finally unveiled their Web site. You get the usual splashy graphics mimicking the style of this national daily paper, along with teaser access to digested headline stories. Access to full stories is not given. For that, you'll have to shell out $12.95 per month for 3 hours, with $2.50 per hour thereafter. What's worse, you'll need a loaded Windows machine with 5 MB of space to run their custom Web browser. These guys just don't get it, do they? Oh well, take a look at their content and try not to get lost among the bandwidth-sucking graphics. "http://www.usatoday.com/"

ART ONLINE

Art and art resources online

THIS HOT LITTLE NUMBER IS "@FASHION" BY MCI

MCI Communications has launched its own fashion information site on the Web. We're not exactly sure of the connection between taffeta and telecommunications, but with a splashy layout like this, who cares? "@fashion" features selected designers, runway video clips, and - perhaps most interesting - "Clothes Circuit", a fashion forecast from Jeanne Beker, host of the VH-1/MuchMusic show, "Fashion TV". Don't expect to see Calvin Klein, Donna Karan or Giorgio Armani, however. So far, "@fashion" is dedicated to the slightly less celebrated visionaries of the cloth. Currently, Todd Oldham, Isaac Mizrahi, and Cynthia Rowley are spotlighted. Up-and-coming designer Victor Alfaro's work is available for viewing, too. "http://www2.pcy.mci.net/fashion/index.html"

THE JEROME TUPA ART GALLERY

Fr. Jerome Tupa is a monk, a professor of French, and a darn fine artist. Each link in the gallery leads to a page with works from one of Tupa's exhibits, which have been shown from Rome to Santa Monica, Calif. From the energetic abstract nudes of "Feu d'Artifices" (Fireworks) and "Out of Eden" to the monolithic quality of "Inscriptions", Tupa's obvious talent shines through. "http://bingen.cs.csbsju.edu/~jtupa"

A BEAUTIFUL PHOTOGRAPHIC PORTFOLIO

If you have the time and a fast link to the Net, you'll enjoy browsing Steven Noreyko's photo site. It looks more like the showcase of a graphic artist than a photographer. He presents an extensive photo spread made up of images he's taken in his capacity as photojournalist, but the fancy site and portfolio page headers each take over one minute to load at 14.4 kbps. To balance the heavy self-promotion, he provides a number of links to other photo, graphic, and journalism sites. "http://www.cris.com/~okyeron/"

ART AND BANNED BOOKS

Cspace, an online gallery of work by artists from Montreal, is a loose collection of underground culture from the cultural hub of Canada. The site, still under construction (what isn't?), now boasts relatively few samples of the wealth of available talent, but both the quantity and the quality of this showcase should improve. The site has one interesting feature: "Naughty Bits - Do not follow this link", which points to "Banned Books On-Line", a collection of literature that at one time or another has been banned by one authority or another. The whole deal is definitely worth a bit of that precious download time. "http://tdg.res.uoguelph.ca:80/www/cspace/"

SITE WITH A BITE: ANNE RICE VAMPIRE CHRONICLES PAGE

You've read the books, seen the movie, and now, dear readers, it is time to surrender yourselves to an entire cyberworld inspired by the works of author Anne Rice. Here, you'll find "Interview with a Vampire" GIFs (yes, Brad Pitt is there), sound files, stories written by alt.books.anne-rice wannabes, the Anne Rice FAQ page, and - you guessed it - more bloody links to related bloody sites. Our only regret: with the busy background, the white type is a little hard to read. "http://www.xroads.com/pages/gpalmer/vampire.html"

STORYTELLER'S PAGES, ONLINE STORIES, AND RESOURCES

Once upon a time, Sherri Johnson at Swarthmore College prepared a site with links to storytellers' pages, children's stories, online stories, and resources for storytellers. The site was rich in story info of all kinds, including Native American stories, folk tales, and links to collections and references. There was even a story available via e-mail. Check it out for yourself, and live happily ever after. "http://www.cc.swarthmore.edu/~sjohnson/stories"

ALL ABOUT WORDS

Recommended by William Safire (NY Times, 12/93), this bimonthly newsletter contains amusing tidbits about the origins of different words and their usages. The graphics are clever, and the writing is both informative and entertaining. Did you know, for example, that "O.K." may have developed from either Andrew Jackson's or Woodrow Wilson's wordplay? To see a sample of the newsletter and get information about subscribing, check out the Word Detective home page. "http://www.escape.com/~words1/"

FRANKENSTEIN GOES HIGH TECH

Now, complete with graphics, you can relive the terrors available through Mary Shelley's "Frankenstein". The hypertext book is divided into three sections: Shelley's preface; letters from Dr. Frankenstein's listener, Captain Walton, to his sister in England; and the chapters that comprise Frankenstein's horror story proper. Note that you can navigate through the book without graphics if you choose. The introduction and arrangement are nicely done; literary mavens should approve. "http://www.medio.net/lit/frank/index.html"

CMC - COMPUTER TECHNOLOGY AND SOCIETY

The evolution of the Internet and the Web has placed a certain emphasis on the technical aspects of the new electronic global community, while the social and personal implications of this electronic revolution have received less attention. To help make up some ground, a slick e-zine called Computer-Mediated Communication Magazine (CMC), around since May 1994, focuses not only on new technologies and their impact on the Net, but keeps a sharp eye on political developments and social issues that directly influence the way the dynamic nature of the Internet will affect our future. This is definitely worth more than a peek. "http://sunsite.unc.edu/cmc/mag/1995/apr/toc.html"

WOMEN'S HEALTH HOT LINE NOW WEBBED

A newsletter that aims to provide the media with information on women's health, the Women's Health Hot Line contains short, succinct articles on a range of medical issues. The articles appear, however, to be geared more to consumers than to the media. Moreover, if you're of the male gender and interested in improving your health, some of the information here may even be helpful for you. The April/May issue, for example, contains an article on planting a "healthy garden", with tips for growing fresh herbs and a guide outlining which herb goes with what food. "http://www.soft-design.com/softinfo/womens-health.html"

SARDONIC WIT FOR THE LOWEST COMMON DENOMINATOR

If you feel you need an e-zine that will "pander to the very lowest common denominator", try subscribing to Ozone Layer at the URL below. CFCs are not welcome. SURFING SCIENCE

Knowledge is Good

TRULY SPECTACULAR SPACE PHOTOGRAPHY WEB SITE

And no, it's not NASA. Jason Ware's Galaxy Photography Web site contains some of the most spectacular space photography anywhere. You can view it in full color on the Web and if you're inclined, he has a listing of prints and slides for sale. Ware also provides info about the telescope used to make the photos and tips for beginning astrophotographers. As with all polite graphic sites, Jason has provided thumbnail images to preview the larger downloads of choice. The prices seem reasonable and the pictures are out-of-this-world (ha-ha). "http://www.galaxyphoto.com/galaxy"

NASA SITE INVITES EDUCATOR COMMENT

A prototype system developed to support public access to NASA's Planetary Data System (PDS) has been open for review by educators. The site now has the Magellan Venus Explorer's Guide, the Guide to Magellan Image Interpretation, the Magellan Mosaicked Image Data Record Collection, and Venus images from "Welcome to the Planets". Catalogue information from the PDS Data Set Catalog is also available. "http://stardust.jpl.nasa.gov/public/"

Does Congress know about this? And who's going to post the pictures? "http://www.hooked.net/alex/people/chuck/sexshuttle.html"

GET THE DIRT ON SEDIMENTOLOGY

Sedimentology, as you might have already guessed, is the study of big things like wind and waves moving little things like grains of sand. The patterns created over time can be quite telling for the clever geologist who's willing to investigate them. This textbook-like site offers you a whole bunch of images, QuickTime movies, plus - and this is a big plus - Mac and PC software to simulate your own ripple and dune bedforms and crossbedding. Go on, get your hands dirty. "http://walrus.sr.usgs.gov/docs/seds/Sedimentology.html"

A SITE FOR SORE BRONCHIAL TUBES

The American Academy of Allergy, Asthma, and Immunology (AAAAI) has an official Web presence, and the site has a plethora of information and links to medical sites and even newsgroups. You can find info on everything from the biology of disease to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Naturally, there are items about allergies and asthma, and the alt.support.asthma FAQ. You can learn more about the AAAAI at: "http://www.uwm.edu/~paulr/aaaai.html"
