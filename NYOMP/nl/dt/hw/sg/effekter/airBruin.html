The Making of Nike's "Air Bruin"

a short animation by Sam Chen
UCLA Animation Workshop 1991

Miniskirts..

It was during the period of 1991, when the sun seemed like it was up longer than ever; when the birds chirped a little louder; when the miniskirts got even shorter. It was definitely spring time at UCLA. As I struggled to make it through the last stretch of my 5-year stint at UCLA as a Computer Science/Engineering student, there was only one reason to wake up in the morning. It was to finish "Michael."

Dan MacLaughlin, the renown professor at the UCLA Animation Workshop, had given us less than 4 weeks to conceive, storyboard, pencil test, animate, and film our term project. It was the perfect antidote to those miniskirts! Dan gave us the mission to stretch our imagination and commit to film.

This was my first attempt at a childhood dream... to create a cartoon character and animate it. After many years of afterschool Bugs Bunny and Daffy Duck, it was time to find out whether any of it stuck with me and whether some of that Tex Avery'ism would rub off.

Since this was an "introductory" class, the requirement was to only animate 15 seconds of footage with no audio soundtrack. Sounded reasonable at first until simple math (15 sec * 24 fps = 360 frames) revealed exactly how many frames I had to ink and paint individually. Even though we were working in "2's," there was still lots of work to set up multi-layer cels for backgrounds and specialFX shots. It was quite clear that those miniskirts would have to wait.

"The Longest 15 Seconds of My Life...."

"15 seconds!!!"... I screamed... that's not enough time to say much or to tell an entertaining story. Even though, 15 seconds appealed to my Spring Fever, I was still driven by my ambitious passion to tell a longer story.

What could I possibly animate in 15 seconds that would both be entertaining and would make a statement? After many days of brainstorming, I decided that a commercial would be the perfect medium. I thought to myself, "Heck, if people can sell a product in a 15 or 28-second ad slots, then it must be possible for me to communicate an idea in that amount of time." So "Nike Air Bruin" was born.

A quick character profile of a Michael Jordan'ish character was sketched out and his various expressions drafted.

Storyboardin'...

The idea was to have "Michael" go up against this puny "USC Trojan Dude" that acted bigger than he really was. (Excuse the crosstown rivalry jabs) He was to take air in front of the historical landmark of Royce Hall (just about the most recognizeable building on campus) .... and slam dunk and crush his way into the closing shot. The point was to sell the shoes but at the same time, annihalate the "puny" one.

The whole process went by quite rapidly but I really found out how much work it was for 1 measly person to do it all. I didn't throw a "Cel Painting Party" like my classmates did where they would invite a whole bunch of their friends over and get cheap labor at the price of a few boxes of pepperoni pizzas. I had to solo this one.

The pencil tests went well but several key shots had to be eliminated due to the 15-second time limit. There was just so much more I wanted to show of "Michael" but there was only enough room for the key shots. One that would've been great was a low angle shot that followed Michael's feet as he's running down the takeoff strip. That one ended up on the cutting room floor as they would say. You can see this scene in one of the storyboard thumbnails below....

The last stretch of this project involved sequencing the individual cels according to the timing chart done earlier and double checking, triple checking the process. Then it was time to commit it to film.

Filming was done in the darkroom of the workshop where an old but functional 16mm film camera resided. It was brought to my attention that Hana Barbera had actually donated this machine to the department and that it might have been used for some of the stuff that made it to TV. That only added to the mystique of the whole experience. Shooting the cels was more like cooking than photography. It was a tedious process of administration mixed with paranoia that I was gonna screw up along the way. Since the whole class was working on 1 big roll of film, there was no way to redo anything.

...but before I knew it, I had hit the 360th frame and I was done!

Days of anticipation mounted as I waited anxiously to see the exposed film shown on the last day of school. Professor Dan had instructed that we invite our friends and bring along a tub of popcorn, and just show up for the final viewing. That was probably the funnest final I've ever shown up for.

The nerves set in as the class reel was rolled. When "Air Bruin" was on the screen finally, it was over before I knew it. The reel was shown a few more times as we savored the satisfying moment. It was both the longest and the shortest 15-seconds of my life. That's about all I can say.

Since then, I've added an obligatory Rock'n Roll soundtrack to "Air Bruin" and it actually enhanced the piece a bit. Sit back and enjoy "Air Bruin".....
