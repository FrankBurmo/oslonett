AN ALLURING HILL

The most popular and most visited summer tourist attraction in Norway is a ski jumping hill, even though jumpers do not jump there in the summertime.

As the Eiffel Tower signals Paris, Big Ben London, the Colosseum Rome and the Statue of Liberty New York, the Holmenkollen ski jumping hill, high on the skyline, signals Oslo Norway's capital.

A million visitors

Every year more than a million visitors, equal to a quarter of the population of Norway, flock to the Holmenkollen ski jumping hill. Most come in the summer, to wander through the Ski Museum at the base of the jump or to enjoy the view form the jump tower some 60 metres above. The hill is at the same place where a jump of 21.5 metres was made in 1892. In more than a century since then, the hill has been rebuild 14 times. Today the hill record is at 126,5 metres.

Olympic record

In 1952, the Olympic Winter Game ski jumping event drew a record 101 400 paying spectators to Holmenkollen, and the total crowd was estimated at 120 000 to 150 000, perhaps a record for a single Olympic event, summer or winter. But the allure of the hill lies not just in records or even in its principal use as a ski jumping hill, but in its versatility. Holmenkollen is a year-round facility. The dip of the outrun is in the middle of Besserud Pond, which is drained for wintertime ski jumping. A permanent stage, used for World Cup award ceremonies, spans the pond.

Summer 'kollen

In the summer, the stage above the full pond is the scene of the annual Summer Concert, attended by some 15 000 and sent live on TV for the tenth time in 1993 to more than 20 countries. The renowned Oslo Philharmonic Orchestra under the baton of Mariss Jansons is regularly featured, along with guest performers such as Isaac Stern and Kiri te Kanawa. Skiforeningen and The Norwegian Broadcasting, who jointly arrange the concerts, plan to invite other stars to future concerts, including Luigi Pavarotti, Placido Domingo and Jose Carreras.

The summer program now includes international musicals, staged the last three years to sold-out crowd of 18 000. The "Ta sjansen" ("Chance it ") family fun show draws some 15 000 every summer. It's a farce festival, a contest between costumed competitors on home-made, self-propelled vehicles who descend an inrun and try to cross the pond on a narrow walkway. The show is sent live on Norwegian TV.

Nordic dog sled racing

At Holmenkollen, competition is a year-round attraction, with sports including international Nordic Mountain Bike Championships, Nordic Dog Sled Racing Championships, ski Orienteering World Cup Final, international orienteering meets, the annual Children's Holmenkoll Day with 6000 children skiing cross-country, children's ski jumping meets on the mini hills alongside the outrun and summertime cross-country running relays in meets drawing thousands of elite and fitness runners. And there are outdoor social events, like the annual mass birthday party for anyone born in July, with their family.

Despite all the attractions, World Cup ski jumping reigns supreme in the program. The 1992 centennial meet drew some 70 000 spectators. and the 1993 meet drew 60 000. the traditional 50-km classical cross-country race is a spectacular event, both in the arena and along the 25- km course, which usually is lined with some 20 000 spectators; many ski out along the course and camp out in tents the night before.

Royal traditions

How did it happen ? What lies behind the tradition? What is the driving force? The answers are as simple as the program is varied. First, skiing is an indelible part of the Norwegian tradition of the centuries. The late King Olav V not only set a royal record in attending 72 Holmenkollen ski jumping meets, but also jumped in the meet in 1922 and 1923, when he was Crown Prince and was a fully qualified ski jumping judge as well. His son, H:M: King Harald V, chaired the Organizing Committee of the 1982 Nordic Word Ski Championships and the 1986 World Biathlon Championships while he was Crown Prince.

The Holmenkollen meet organizers, the "Foreningen til Ski-Idrettens Fremme" ("Society for the Furtherance of Ski Sports"), or Skiforeningen for short is active in the recreational life of the capital city. Every winter, Skiforeningen prepares some 1500 km of cross-country ski trails. of which 80 km are illuminated. On a good winter ski Sunday, as many as 100 000 skiers may use the trails. Skiforeningen also owns and operates several woodland ski lodges and four alpine skiing lifts.

No Olympic fears

As an international meet organizer, Skiforeningen stays abreast of developments. There's always something new at Holmenkollen; recent improvements include ceramic inrun tracks to assure good jumping even in low-snow years. The corps of some 1900 volunteers who help staff world cup events continually update their expertise.

"Even with the 1994 Olympic Winter Games at Lillehammer and the 1997 Nordic World Ski Championships at Trondheim, I am confident that Holmenkollen will retain its position in international skiing" says Rolf Nyhus, Director of the annual Holmenkollen Ski Festival. "And we intend to do so by offering high-quality meets and facilities to attract future World Cup events."

One and a half million sponsor dollars a year

"The competition for sponsorship backing is keen but Holmenkollen sells well year-round" smiles Nyhus. "In 1995 our total income from sponsors, in the form of goods, services and money, amounts to some 10 millions Norwegian kroner, about one and a half million dollars"

Businesses supply half the 6-mill.-kroner (about 1 mill. dollars) annual Holmenkollen Ski Festival cash budget", Nyhus explains. As other sports organizations in Norway feel pinched in the tight sponsorship market, Skiforeningen and Holmenkollen enjoy solid sponsorship backing.

And Nyhus knows that topic well: the annual Holmenkollen Ski Festival must finance itself, as it receives no support from the government or the city of Oslo. But not only can Holmenkollen finance itself: for the 1992 were upgraded at a cost of millions of dollars, of which a considerable portion came from sponsors.

Sponsors in a queue

The Holmenkollen Ski Festival has a waiting-list for sponsors. That enviable position resulted from a decisive long-term sponsor strategy aimed at exclusive exposure: never are contracts signed with two firms competing in the same product sector. And the policy of year-round follow-up of some 50 sponsor helps heighten awareness.

Survival of fitness

"Money is tight now; only the fittest will survive. And that includes ski meet organizers," maintains Rolf Nyhus.

But with commercial sponsorship at the rate of one and a half million dollars a year, certainly some business must see Holmenkollen sponsorship as charity. Nyhus does not deny that aspect:"I cannot deny that Holmenkollen is sort of a national monument , a symbol of a positive side of life. Skiforeningen is also a cultural institution on the sense that it nurtures an important part of our heritage. And we offer sports and recreation, year-round. Yet we always focus on the benefits we can supply for the support we receive form our partners; we require that they receive on their investments."

"Bumper years are vital in the long run," he observes. "In a little more than a decade we have had three: the Nordic World Ski Championships in 1982, the World Biathlon Championships in 1986 and the Holmenkollen centennial in 1992. Bumper years generate interest and payback for our partners."
