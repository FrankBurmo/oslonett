SOME FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS ABOUT ANIMAX and ANIMOTION

AniMAX and aniMOTION are trademarks of Boxer Technologies AS, Kristiansand, Norway. Animax and AniMOTION are software programs for production of computer animation.

AniMAX is UNIX-based and runs om Cromemco computers, whereas AniMOTION is PC-based and runs under MS- Windows 3.1.

The animation produced by these programs may be used in video production and interactive PC training programs.

Below are a number of questions and corresponding answers related to AniMAX and AniMOTION:

Q= Question

A= Answer

Q: Please comment on AniMAX's qualities compared to other similar tools.

A: AniMAX is (to our knowledge) one of a kind. It cannot be directly compared to any other existing software product. ANIMAX is a "video programming language", providing real -time computer animation. Thus, if someone needs illustrative animation for video production (presentation, instruction, education), ANIMAX is the answer! ANIMAX provides sequences (second, minutes, even hours) of continous computer animation (real-time streams), which can be fed directly onto video tape for further video editing. For prototyping, ANIMAX can control narration sound and background music itself, and hence generate complete video tapes automatically, without the need for video studio post processing. ANIMAX is parameterized, wich allows for rapid modifications of the visual outpout.

Q: How powerful is ANIMAX compared to other visualization products?

A: ANIMAX primarily works in the 2D and 2.5D environment. Although it is capable of simple 3D animation, it can not be classified as a true 3D tool. 3D animation is extremely CPU demanding (may require thousands and millions of calculations for the rendering of one single frame); thus 3D real-time aniamation can only be done on large computers and only if the complexity of the visual scenes are moderate. Noramally, 3D animation is performed on a frame-by-frame basis, also when the computer has calculated one single frame , it is fed to a video recording device, and the computers moves on to the next frame. It is not unnormal that the generation of one single frame requires 10 minutes, 30 minutes, even hours of computing time. And the European PAL video format requires 25 frames per second of animation! (The American NTSC-format operates on 30 frames per second.) Frame-by-frame computer animation is thus extremely costly, and is therefore mostly used in short commercials and TV-program logos. For extensive usage (minutes and minutes), and spesially in technical communication, ANIMAX is the proper tool!

Advanced 3D production tools (like Wavefront, Alias, NeoVisual, TDI) can generate impressing, nature-like, and fancy animations. However, the price tag for such animation is the balancing question; prices of USD 1.000 - 5.000 per second is not unnormal !

ANIMAX outpouts broadcast quality, provided the animation is recorded onto broadcast quality video tape. PAL specifies 625 lines of visual information (NTSC uses 525 lines); ANIMAX operates digitally with 1024 lines and 1024 pixels per line. One ca easily tell the difference between ANIMAX-animation and high-end 3D-animation. Both in visual appearance, and in terms of money! One minute of ANIMAX-animation is normally less expensive than one second of high-end 3D-animation.

And not all applications require the full 3D-appoach. Everyone cannot afford to drive a Rolls Royce, and a Beetle is more convenient in jamed city traffic.

There are, as a matter of fact, a number of low-cost software products which, in all honesty, can produce computer animation. Examples include Commodore Amiga's and Apple's. We think it is fair, however, to insist that ANIMAX out- performs products like these in terms of visual quality, flexibility, and even cost. In most cases, the alternative products do not meet the quality standards set by the professional video production houses, nor the expectations of most end-users.

We maintain, that in technical instruction and education, there is not an animation product available today wich can compate and win over ANIMAX in terms of cost-benefit.

Q: How user-friendly is ANIMAX? How long does it takes to learn?

A: ANIMAX, as originally implemented on a UNIX-based computer, does not have the modern graphical user interface. However, as a production tool in our own video production workshop, it functions adequately.

But ANIMAX is about to become available on a PC-platform, carrying the trademark of AniMOTION. AniMOTION will run in the Microsoft Windows environment, ensuring a familiar user interface to many potential users. And for computer novices, the windows system will provide a straight-forward and esay-to-learn interface to AniMOTION.

We expect a few weeks of self-study and experimentation with AniMOTION is all that is needed to become a productive and efficient AniMOTION operator.

And Boxer provides an instructional video tape to ease the initial phase when learning to use AniMOTION.

Q: What kind of hardware does AniMOTION require?

A: AniMOTION runs a regular PC computer. We recommend 486 CPU and 8MB RAM.

To secure broadcast quality on the PC-platform, an additional VISTA graphics card is mandatory, together with an RGB- .monitor (or at least a multi-sync monitor). Supplementary to the mouse and keyboard imput devices, a tablet may be preferred by some users. As a matter of fact, this AniMOTION/Vista architecture outperforms the older AniMAX predecessor.

To provide composite video outpout, an (internal or external) RGB/video conversion module is necessary. But AniMOTION is also avilable in a low-cost sVGA version. For interactive PC_based presentations and trainings programs, AniMOtion/sVGA is fully capable. This package requires no additional hardware to an ordinary IBM- compatible PC with sVGA graphics.

Q: What does AniMOTION cost?

A: the pricing structure has not been settled yet. AniMOTION/ Vista will be offered to the marketplace in software modules. We expect the basic module will cost approx. USD 5.000. The complete AniMOTION package will not exceed USD 20.000.

A turn-key system carrying all of AniMOTION on a 486-CPU/33MHz/8MB/200MB computer will cost less than USD 30.000.

As a comparison, a full Wavefront system on an Silicon Graphics Iris computer cost approx USD 250.00. On the other hand, a Conmodore Amiga-3000 package cost less than USD 10.000.

The AniMOTION/sVGA will sell at less than USD 1.000.

Q: How efficient is AniMOTION to work with?

A: Our own ANIMAX operators produce each, on average, between 1 and 10 minutes of animotion per day (7 hours). Modifications to existing ANIMAX-code takes only a few seconds (or minutes, if it is complicated).

It is expected that productivity will increase further with the AniMotion system.

Modelling of a complete 3D-scene (f.ex. 30 second) with traditional 3D-tools typically requires 1-2 days of labour. And the subsequent rendering process may last for several days, depending on the complexity of the animation. The outpout can only be examined thouroughly after complete recording onto tape. If there is a need for modification, the rendering process has to be repeated. It is not unnormal that one regeneration cycle takes one week or more.

Simple animations can be done readily with systems like Amiga (although the quality is questionable). More complex animations cannot be done with systems like Amiga.

Q: Does AniMOTION have any spesial capabilities, apart form those mentioned above, which can successfully be utilized in educational applications, especially interactive systems ?

A: Indeed!

First of all, AniMOTION is designed with the very purpose of offering advanced visual communication features to the applications of instruction and education, especially in the area of technical training and understanding.

AniMOTION is an outstanding partner in generating linear video tapes for such applications. From a record of more than 50 complete video tapes produced with ANIMAX by Boxer Technologies AS alone, 68 % of the video footage is computer animation (on the average; the low number is 31% and the high number is 100%).

Also, interactive training programs, running from a PC platform, may benefit from AniMOTION (especially the sVGA version). Extensive use of real-time animations will make the program interesting and challenging. The animations are generated as they are required, there is no longer a need to store the animations on video-disk. And the animations require just a few bytes of the hard-disk, as they are generated from parameter files in ASCII-format.

The language of AniMOTION (the texts as part of the animation) can be changed by a single keystroke. Prepare the training session in f. ex. English, and convert easily to German, French, Spanish, Noewegian, Russian, Swahili, Urdu, Thai, Sanskrit, and Umainakaina, among a number of other language !.

Training programs require maintenance! From now on the animation part of such programs represents no problem; just modify it, add new segments, and delete outdated portions, using the high level interface of the AniMOTION language!.
