World Cup USA 1994, Inc. (WCOC)

World Cup USA 1994, Inc. (WCOC) is the non-profit legal entity charged with staging the 1994 World Cup championship under the direction of the Fédération Internationale de Football Association (FIFA). The WCOC was established on May 6, 1987, to prepare the United States Soccer Federation's bid to host the 1994 World Cup, and now continues its task as the organizing committee.

The WCOC employs more than 300 people with executive, operations and marketing offices in Los Angeles and a press office in New York City. A joint marketing venture involving FIFA marketing representative ISL Properties AG, is located in the Washington, D.C., area, in the same location as the WCOC's government relations department.

The bicoastal offices allow the WCOC to coordinate successfully all aspects of business necessary to fulfill the committee's mission of staging the finest World Cup ever, and of leaving a legacy for soccer in the United States. In addition to the three central offices, the WCOC has set up offices in each of the nine venue communities, which will be hosting 1994 World Cup games.

Heading the WCOC is Alan I. Rothenberg, chairman and chief executive officer. Rothenberg, a partner in the law firm of Latham & Watkins, also serves as president of U.S. Soccer, a position he has held since August 5, 1990. Scott Parks LeTellier is managing director/chief operating officer, and responsible for the day-to-day activities. Elizabeth Primrose-Smith serves as managing director/chief administrative officer, and is responsible for the development and implementation of strategic and business plans, as well as managing the administrative and budgetary efforts.

WCOC Structure & Services

World Cup USA 1994, Inc. is divided into 16 departments, each one managed by a vice president or senior vice president, and reporting directly to either Chief Executive Officer Alan Rothenberg; Chief Operating Officer Scott LeTellier; or Chief Administrative Officer, Elizabeth Primrose-Smith. Some departments stand alone, while others have responsibility over several divisions. Combined, the 16 departments are charged with providing the services necessary to facilitate the WCOC successfully fulfilling its mission - - to stage the most successful World Cup ever, and to leave a legacy for soccer in the United States.

Chairman & Chief Executive Officer

Alan I. Rothenberg

Alan I. Rothenberg is chairman and chief executive officer of World Cup USA 1994, as well as president of the United States Soccer Federation (USSF). A distinguished lawyer and sports executive, Rothenberg was elected to the USSF presidency for a four- year term on August 5, 1990. Rothenberg is a renowned trial lawyer and a senior partner in the Los Angeles office of the international firm of Latham & Watkins, specializing in litigation and business and commercial law.

A respected lecturer on the field of sports law, Rothenberg was a regular commentator on ESPN's "Sportslook." As the commissioner of soccer for the 1984 Los Angeles Olympic Organizing Committee, he was responsible for the Olympic soccer tournament, which drew the largest crowds of any sport at the 1984 Games. Among other achievements in his 25-year involvement in soccer are his ownership of the Los Angeles Aztecs of the North American Soccer League, and his position as general manager and counselor to the Los Angeles Wolves of the United Soccer Association.

Rothenberg first entered sports as vice president and general counsel to Jack Kent Cooke, Inc. and California Sports, Inc., which, between them, owned the Los Angeles Lakers, the Los Angeles Kings, The Forum and substantial cable television interests. He was a member of the National Basketball Association Board of Governors from 1971- 1979 and from 1982-1990, and served from 1982-1989 as president of the Los Angeles Clippers.

Rothenberg has been a member of the State Bar of California since 1964, and served as the organization's president in 1989-1990. He also is chairman of the American Bar Association's Governing Committee on Entertainment and Sports Law. Deeply involved in numerous civic activities, Rothenberg has been a member of the Board of Trustees of the California Museum of Science and Industry Foundation, president and chairman of the Fraternity of Friends of the Music Center and a member of the board of the Bank of Newport.

Rothenberg, born April 10, 1939, is a 1960 graduate from the University of Michigan, where he received his B.A. in history. Three years later he earned his Juris Doctorate with distinction from the same university.

Managing Director/Chief Operating Officer

Scott Parks LeTellier

Scott Parks LeTellier is managing director/chief operating officer of World Cup USA 1994, Inc. Previously, he was a corporate and securities attorney in the Newport Beach, California, office of Pettit & Martin. He is responsible for the day-to-day activities of the organizing committee for the 1994 World Cup, directly overseeing the venues, security, government relations, protocol, legal and international departments, as well as the World Cup Accommodation Bureau and World Cup Travel Services divisions, and the organizing committee's relationships with FIFA, ISL Marketing AG (FIFA's marketing agent), EBU Sports International (the WCOC's host broadcaster) and ABC Sports and ESPN (the U.S. television rights holders).

As outside counsel to the U.S. Soccer Federation's 1994 World Cup bid committee, LeTellier drafted and negotiated agreements with 18 stadia proposed as potential 1994 World Cup sites, and was principally responsible for writing the U.S. bid document submitted to FIFA. He was also part of the five-member team which made the oral presentation to FIFA's executive committee on July 4, 1988, in Zurich, Switzerland, the day the U.S. was awarded the right to host the 1994 World Cup. Following the award, LeTellier established the organizing committee in Washington, D.C., as its first employee in February 1989.

A native of Milwaukee, Wisconsin, LeTellier earned a B.S. with high honors from the University of Tennessee, majoring in statistics. An accomplished athlete, he twice captained the Tennessee tennis team, and won both the Wisconsin and Tennessee State Open men's doubles championships.

From 1972 to 1974, LeTellier served as a voluntary representative of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Mormon) in Germany, where he acquired fluency in German and a deep interest in soccer through the 1974 World Cup.

LeTellier earned a juris doctorate from Brigham Young University in 1978. In 1981, he took a leave-of-absence from his law career to join the Los Angeles Olympic Organizing Committee (LAOOC), where his duties as legal counsel included negotiation of contracts with the major Olympic competition sites. During his tenure with the LAOOC, his duties also included staff responsibility for the 1984 Olympic soccer tournament and representing the LAOOC at the 1982 World Cup in Spain.

FIFA Interface

International Broadcast Center
The International Broadcast Center (IBC) is the coordinating facility from which will be produced all of the outgoing television signals from World Cup USA '94 . Although coverage of each game will be produced at the nine venues around the USA, all of these signals will be routed back to the IBC to be processed, synchronized and readied for distribution (again via satellite) to international broadcasters which have purchased the rights to show World Cup games.

The IBC will be housed in Dallas, Texas. A nearby 10,000-square-foot satellite compound will transmit signals serving the international broadcast facilities throughout the world to reach the projected 32 billion (cumulative) fans expected to view the 52 World Cup games.

U.S. Radio Broadcast Rights
The WCOC has the rights to market the English-language radio broadcast of the World Cup tournament in the United States.
The English-language rights have been awarded to One-on-One Sports Network. The 24-hour all-sports talk network will deliver game-of-day play-by-play coverage, beginning with the Opening Game on June 17 in Chicago between defending champion Germany and Bolivia.
One-on-One will broadcast at least 25 games, including 14 first-round games, four Round-of-16 games, all four quarterfinals, both semifinals, the third-place matchup and the championship game. Additional games may be added.
The One-on-One Sports Network is available in 46 states with 155 affiliates in the United States and Canada.

Managing Director/Chief Administrative Officer

Elizabeth Primrose-Smith

Elizabeth (Eli) Primrose-Smith serves as managing director/chief administrative officer for the WCOC. As CAO, Primrose-Smith is responsible for the development and implementation of strategic and business plans, as well as managing the administrative and budgetary efforts.

Primrose-Smith joined the WCOC from the Los Angeles Sports Festival Committee, where she served as president and executive director of the 1991 U.S. Olympic Festival. She managed the interaction with the U.S. Olympic Committee and its 37 national governing bodies.

From 1981-1984, Primrose-Smith served as associate vice president of the Los Angeles Olympic Organizing Committee, where her role consisted of strategic, operational and staffing plans, as well as developing ticketing plans for 30 venues. She also served as sports manager for aquatic sports and formulated operating procedures for two Olympic Villages housing 12,000 athletes, officials and coaches.

Primrose-Smith began her sports management career in 1981, serving as assistant executive director of the World Games in Santa Clara, California. Her other professional experience includes: director of external affairs/business development for McKinsey & Company, project planning, counseling, public relations, special events and fundraising work. A former All-America selection in swimming and water polo, she was a gold medalist in swimming in the 1963 Pan American Games in Sao Paulo, Brazil.

Primrose-Smith earned her B.A. in English from Stanford University, and M.B.A. from UCLA. She is a board member of the Southern California Committee for the Olympic Games, Los Angeles Sports Council and the American Athletes Development Foundation.

Primrose-Smith is responsible for the following departments: Ceremonies, External Affairs, Finance & Administration, Human Resources, Press Office, Support Services and Technology.

World Cup USA 1994 Departments [Return to TOC]

Ceremonies

The Ceremonies Department is responsible for promoting American and worldwide recognition of the 1994 World Cup, building momentum in anticipation of the event, and creating a World Cup "experience" by hosting numerous national and international events in the months leading up to, and during, the summer of 1994.

For the first time during a World Cup, with special approval from FIFA, there will be halftime entertainment in the form of video presentations at the 52 games, which will update and entertain the spectators and showcase the venue cities.

The Opening Ceremony is the first impression that millions of spectators and billions of television viewers will have of World Cup USA 1994. Spectacular pageantry, a cast of thousands, fabulous music and unending surprises will ensure the Opening Ceremony meets the goal -- Making Soccer History.

The Opening Ceremony, held prior to the Opening Game between defending champion Germany and Bolivia in Chicago's Soldier Field, will be 30 minutes in length and will begin 45 minutes prior to the beginning of the match. The Opening Ceremony will have the "heart" of an Olympic Games ceremony, and the "pace and sizzle" of a Super Bowl halftime show. But above all, it will have the importance of opening the sporting event of all time -- the 1994 World Cup.

The theme, "The USA Welcomes the World ... World Cup Unites the World!", will be emblematic of the Opening Ceremonies.

Each of the other eight venues will stage smaller opening ceremonies prior to the first game in the respective stadiums.

The Closing Ceremony takes place prior to the final championship World Cup game in the Rose Bowl, and will be one of the final impressions that the 100,000 spectators, and billions of television viewers worldwide, will have of the 1994 World Cup. It will "set the stage" for the climactic World Cup final game.

The Closing Ceremony will honor the two teams playing in the championship game, as well as all athletes, spectators, viewers and citizens of the world, who have been a part of "Making Soccer History."

The Closing Ceremony will take place on the afternoon of July 17, 1994, in the historic Rose Bowl in Pasadena. The ceremony will last approximately 30 minutes, and will begin 45 minutes prior to the start of the championship final.

Each of the other eight venues will host smaller closing ceremonies before the final game at the respective stadia.

There will be special World Cup Independence Day Celebrations on the Fourth of July, including fireworks, and staged musical and entertainment elements in many of the nine venue cities.

Corporate Marketing

The Corporate Marketing Division works in unison with World Cup USA '94 Marketing (the joint venture) and the WCOC's Marketing Services Department.

The Corporate Marketing Division is proactive in formulating the sponsorship marketing program for Major League Soccer (MLS), a professional Division I soccer league, which is scheduled to begin play in 12 cities in April 1995.

Entertainment Properties

The Entertainment Properties Department oversees the packaging and production of the official song, the official album, official videos, official film, and concerts, among other projects.

The Chicago Concert will feature top contemporary artists and will take place on June 16 to kick off the month-long World Cup events.

The Official Song, "Gloryland," will be the World Cup anthem and will be used in commercial and promotional capacities. "Gloryland," which captures the musical heritage of the United States, features Daryl Hall singing lead vocals with the world's best-selling gospel choir, Sounds of Blackness.

The Official Videos will capture the essence of World Cup USA '94 competition. The official videos distributed by PolyGram before the World Cup include: "World Cup Fever" and "The Official Preview: World Cup 1994." Following the World Cup, four to eight videos will be distributed. They include: "The World Cup Final Game," "The Heroes of the World Cup," "Goals from the World Cup," and "The Story of the World Cup."

The Official Film will tell the story of the 1994 World Cup games. It is different from the Official Videos, in that it will be a conceptual piece, with a storyline that provides continuity and cohesion. The 90-minute film will be produced by a well-known director who knows the sport of soccer, and will be released in theaters worldwide six months after the championship game (July 17, 1994).

The Official Album will consist of newly recorded contemporary pop music, as well as songs previously recorded by major recording artists. It will have a conceptual thread of anthemic recordings pertaining to the glory of the sport of soccer, and will be made available for commercial and promotional purposes by the WCOC and its sponsors and marketing partners.

1994 World Cup Week at the Hollywood Bowl. From July 11 through July 17, World Cup USA 1994 will produce a very special series of performances at the Hollywood Bowl -- the largest natural amphitheater in the world. The Bowl has always been the place to be for the best in classical music, rock, pop and jazz. From the Los Angeles Philharmonic, with special musical guests, to the superstars of rock, 1994 World Cup Week at the Hollywood Bowl will be the place for entertainment under the stars.

"Encore -- The Three Tenors" -- The World Cup USA 1994 Final Concert will reunite the internationally renowned Jose Carreras, Placido Domingo and Luciano Pavarotti in a spectacular performance on Saturday, July 16, 1994, at Dodger Stadium. "Encore -- The Three Tenors" is scheduled for the evening before the World Cup Final. The trio made history with their first performance in Rome in 1990, and "trio" fans have been impatient for an encore.

The History & Archives Division exists to ensure that all records generated by the WCOC are properly organized, cataloged and indexed, and eventually reposited in a place that will guarantee their safe-keeping. The division will assist all departments in the collection, organization, documentation and preparation of their records.

A variety of publications are produced by the Publications Division.

Public Relations

The Communications Department also fulfills a public relations role in non-sports areas not impacted by the WCOC's New York-based Media/Press Operations Department. The Communications Department also oversees numerous public relations agencies retained by the organizing committee to focus on a variety of World Cup events and activities.

Speakers Bureau

With an event that carries the importance of the World Cup, there are numerous requests for speakers who are proficient in all aspects of the 1994 World Cup tournament. The Speakers Bureau will provide speakers who might be called upon to address such issues as: ticketing, scheduling, economic impact, venue preparedness, the venue selection process, security procedures, technology and government relations, among others.

Visual Services

The Visual Services Division includes photography and videotaping. The Visual Services staff is responsible for chronicling and cataloging the important day-to-day developments inherent in the organizing and staging of the World Cup.

Education & Community Outreach

The WCOC's Education & Community Outreach Department has developed numerous programs that offer unique opportunities to advance the sport of soccer in the United States, while enriching American education. The goals and objectives of the Education & Community Outreach programs are to:

  • Establish World Cup USA 1994 as an unprecedented worldwide event among students, educators, parents and communities.

  • Strengthen education through programs that enhance existing teaching systems.

  • Heighten appreciation of soccer by incorporating World Cup USA '94 into the curriculum, thereby establishing a foundation for a legacy for soccer in the U.S.

  • Develop the following traits in young people: self-esteem, setting goals, responsible citizenship, academic excellence, sportsmanship and teamwork, as well as global and cultural awareness.

Programs [Return to TOC]

American Library Association -- World Cup USA 1994 joined forces with the American Library Association (ALA) to develop a poster promoting reading and the use of libraries, as well as building awareness of the 1994 FIFA World Cup and the sport of soccer. These posters are ideal vehicles to encourage young people to enhance their academic excellence, and thereby their self-esteem, through reading. They are being distributed to schools and libraries nationwide.

Fuji Photo Pals
Fuji Photo Pals incorporates World Cup and soccer into the existing Fuji Film Photo Pals program. This school program creates the opportunity to bring cross-cultural understanding to life in the classroom by encouraging photograph exchanges between students nationwide. Fuji presents this program through Scholastic magazine, reaching millions of elementary- and middle-school children.

The Education Center
One of the nation's leading publishers and distributors of instructional teaching material for elementary-school educators, the Education Center, collaborated with World Cup USA 1994 to produce workbooks featuring soccer and the 1994 World Cup event. These teaching guides were distributed to approximately 300,000 K through 6th-grade teachers nationwide.

The GOALS Program
"Gaining Opportunities to Achieve Lifetime Success" (GOALS) has been created by World Cup USA 1994 and the Constitutional Rights Foundation. GOALS is a self-contained, multi-disciplinary program for middle schools that integrates World Cup and soccer into social studies, language, math, sciences and physical education. Activities encourage the development of responsible citizenship, individual skills and team concepts, field experiences and competition, and community service. Lifetime Learning Skills, a unique feature of GOALS, includes the communication skills of reading, writing, speaking and listening, in addition to civic participation skills. Nationwide distribution to teachers began in March 1994.

Junior Achievement
"World Cup: Scoring Points for the Economy" was developed by the Junior Achievement organization in affiliation with World Cup USA 1994 as a supplemental economics education program for eighth graders. This Project Business lesson plan is being taught by Junior Achievement volunteers in middle schools across the country. The lesson demonstrates the economics of bringing World Cup to each of the nine cities and the effect upon the communities, businesses and people.

"Windows to the World"
With the Junior League of Los Angeles (JLLA), the WCOC created a teacher's resource of educational games based upon the 141 countries that participated in the 1994 FIFA World Cup qualifying phase. Questions revolve around world and United States geography, language and cultural diversity, as well as World Cup and soccer. These games are for use primarily in classrooms and are adaptable for use by community youth groups, libraries, youth soccer organizations, etc. The games stimulate teamwork, mutual respect, global awareness and academic excellence. Nationwide distribution to teachers began in March 1994.

World Cup Mural Contest
In Spring 1993, students in art classes throughout Los Angeles-area high schools interpreted the theme "Making Soccer History" and submitted entries to World Cup USA 1994. The objectives of the World Cup Mural Contest were to expand awareness of the World Cup event and promote teamwork and dedication to goals, while building a legacy for the sport of soccer. The murals were unveiled at the FIFA Final Draw in Las Vegas in December 1993, and now are displayed at the World Cup office in Los Angeles.

Environmental Design

The Environmental Graphics Program expresses, in three-dimensional form, all the energy and fervor associated with worldclass soccer. It accomplishes this through a palette of bold colors and crisp geometric shapes, along with some readily identifiable soccer icons, the World Cup logo and the mascot.

The combination of colors and shapes gives a sense of constant motion and the vigorous nature of soccer. These shapes and colors were first presented in a design preview poster in early 1993, and will be applied to the interiors of the stadia on press boxes, scoreboards, commentator and interview areas, as well as the FIFA Tribune of Honor.

An assortment of tents, kiosks and other enclosures have been developed for the "Main Street" concept at the venues, corporate hospitality, and public comfort and service areas. "Signature entrance" installations have been created for the venues, locating the primary identity elements to both enhance the stadium grounds and direct spectators from the shuttle drop-off areas into the stadium.

WCOC Marketing

The WCOC's Marketing Department is responsible for managing six divisions: advertising, client services, hospitality, World Cup mascot/trophy management, public information and soccer constituents. World Cup News Service (WCNS)

The World Cup News Service is designed to provide up-to-the-second results, historical data, current news and releases, and general information to World Cup participants. The WCNS audience will include VIPs, WCOC staff, official guests, players and the nearly 10,000 accredited media. WCNS will be available on Sun data base workstations at all WCOC locations and at the Media Centers. WCNS provides access to a sophisticated data base featuring:

  • Official FIFA Databank -- This includes historical results from previous World Cup competitions from 1930 through 1990, as well as all qualifying results for World Cup USA '94 . Final results from 1994 games will be included in the FIFA Databank when they are official and certified.

  • FIFA and World Cup News Bulletins -- These include news updates, announcements and bulletins from FIFA and World Cup organizers, as well as "Notes & Quotes" from newsworthy World Cup participants.

  • World Cup Results, Schedules, Statistics and Teams -- This will include current results, upcoming schedules of events (news conferences, press briefings and other media events), on-line match statistics and team information (rosters, biographies and player performance updates).

  • Local Organizing Committee and Venues -- Details about administration and preparation for the '94 World Cup and venue (city and stadia) profiles will be included, as well as information about the World Cup logo and mascot, sponsors and marketing partners, entertainment, government relations and security.

  • Word Processing Programs -- Accredited journalists will have access, to a simple word processing program in order to create and print confidential documents and stories.

WCNS will display information in multiple languages for our international audience. Users may select their preferred language when first entering the system, or switch to another language.

Games results will be collected by statisticians during the actual games and entered automatically into WCNS using Sun workstations in the Press Tribune. The sophisticated Commentator Statistics System will use Parallax video boards to relay games statistics as they are recorded to broadcasters over closed-circuit television.

Working with FIFA, WCOC has developed a globally accessible "FIFA World Cup Bulletin Board System (BBS)" for access to WCNS information before, during and after the 1994 World Cup. The system will allow the worldwide media and (newspapers, radio and television) and public to obtain historical information from the official data bank provided by FIFA, as well as updated news and results from qualifying and 1994 tournament games. The BBS started operation in March 1994 through the international CompuServe network.

The FIFA Databank, with the WCNS software, will be returned to FIFA at the end of the 1994 World Cup, to provide continued media and public support until 1998.

United States Soccer Federation (U.S. Soccer)

The United States Soccer Federation (U.S. Soccer), a member of the Fédération Internationale de Football Association (FIFA) since 1913, is the national governing body for soccer in the United States. Headquartered in Chicago, U.S. Soccer was named the host national association for the 1994 FIFA World Cup on July 4, 1988.

U.S. Soccer was one of the world's first soccer organizations to be affiliated with FIFA, the worldwide governing body for soccer. U.S. Soccer has continued to grow through the years to where it now has the second-largest membership of the 38 'Class A' members of the U.S. Olympic Committee.

The Federation has more than 50 full-time employees working to administer and serve a membership located in all 50 states. Originally known as the U.S. Football Association, the Federation's name was changed to the United States Soccer Football Association in 1945, and then to the USSF in 1974.

U.S. Soccer has altered its operating philosophy from that of a non-profit volunteer organization to that of a professionally managed sports business. It is administered by a national council of elected representatives. Much of the business is conducted by a 35- member board of directors representing three vast administrative arms -- approximately 1.7 million youth players (19 years and under), 200,000 senior players (over 19) and its professional division. The senior and youth divisions are divided into 51 and 55 state associations, respectively, with some states divided into two associations.

U.S. Soccer is responsible for supervising a registration system for thousands of players and referees, organizing national cup competitions, managing eight national teams that compete worldwide, arranging educational courses, staging international matches and processing international player transfers.

As the host national association for the 1994 FIFA World Cup, U.S. Soccer is proud of the many successes attained by the U.S. teams in 1991-1993.

The U.S. Men's National Team won the inaugural U.S. Cup '92, defeating Ireland and Portugal, and tying three-time World Cup champion Italy. The men's team upset England, 2-0, in the U.S. Cup '93, and finished second to Mexico in the 1993 CONCACAF Gold Cup. The U.S. Women's National Team won the first-ever FIFA Women's World Championship in 1991, hosted by China.

The United States is the only country to have qualified its national teams for each world championship in the current FIFA cycle. The U.S. Men's National Team won the 1991 CONCACAF Gold Cup; the U.S. Five-a-Side team won the silver medal; the under- 23 Team won the gold medal at the Pan American Games and advanced to the 1992 Olympics; the under-20 Team finished eighth in the 1993 World Championship; the under- 17 Team placed seventh in the 1993 World Championship; and the U.S. Women's Team finished second in the 1993 World University Games. Teams representing the U.S., and managed by U.S. Soccer, include five men's teams and two women's teams. Teams organized for the men include the U.S. National Team, Five-a-Side, under-23 Team, under-20 Team, and under-17 Team. Women's teams include the U.S. National Women's Team and the Women's under-20 Team.

National coaching schools are held regularly where interested persons may gain certification in six progressive levels of soccer coaching. A large referee program makes up another part of the U.S. Soccer scene, with more than 60,000 referees currently registered with the Federation.

1994 FIFA World Cup Qualifying Teams

Argentina -- The Argentines, seeded No. 1 in Group 'D,' have participated in 10 of the 14 World Cup tournaments (1930-1934, 1958-1966, 1974-1990). Argentina hosted the finals in 1978, winning the World Cup trophy that year, and won it again in 1986. It was the runner-up at the first World Cup tournament in 1930, and again at the last one in 1990. In its 48 World Cup games played, Argentina has 24 wins, nine draws and 15 losses.

Belgium -- The Belgians, seeded No. 1 in Group 'F,' have taken part in eight World Cups (1930-1938, 1954, 1970, 1982-1990). Their best finish was fourth place in the 1986 Mexico World Cup, losing 4-2 to France in the consolation game. In its 25 matches played, Belgium has seven wins, four draws and 14 losses.

Bolivia -- The Bolivians, in Group 'C,' have played in just two World Cup finals (1930, 1950). They have lost all three games in which they have played by scores of 0-4, 0-4, 0-8, but are looking for improvement in 1994.

Brazil -- The Brazilians, seeded No. 1 in Group 'B,' have more success than any other nation in the World Cup, qualifying for all 14 (1930-1990). Brazil was the first country to win three World Cup trophies (1958, 1962, 1970), and was the runner-up in 1950, the year it hosted the tournament. In its 66 games, Brazil has compiled an enviable record of 44 wins, 11 draws and 11 losses, outscoring the opposition by 148 goals to 65.

Bulgaria -- The Bulgarians, in Group 'D,' have participated in five World Cup finals (1962-1974, 1986). They are still seeking their first World Cup victory, having compiled a record of no wins, six draws and 10 losses.

Cameroon -- Cameroon, in Group 'B,' has a brief World Cup history, having qualified for two tournaments (1982, 1990). Its best finish was a 3-2 quarterfinal loss to England in 1990, with all three of England's goals coming on penalty kicks. In its eight World Cup games, Cameroon has a combined record of three wins, three draws and two losses.

Colombia -- The Colombians, in Group 'A,' have taken part in two World Cup finals (1962, 1990). Their best finish was a 2-1 loss to Cameroon in the second round in 1990. In its seven World Cup games, the South American team has a World Cup record of one win, two draws and four losses.

Germany -- The defending champion, the Germans, seeded No. 1 in Group 'C,' have appeared in 12 of the 14 World Cups (1934-1938, 1954-1990). Germany is one of only three countries to win three World Cup trophies (1954, 1974, 1990). Brazil and Italy are the other two. Having played as "West" Germany until this World Cup, the champs have an impressive World Cup history. In its 68 World Cup games, Germany has 39 wins, 15 draws and 14 losses, and has outscored its opposition by 145 goals to 90. In the last five World Cups, Germany has won twice (1974, 1990), and been the runner-up in two others (1982, 1986).

Greece -- When Greece, in Group 'D,' tied Russia, 1-1, in a qualifying game on May 23, 1993, it marked the first time the European nation had qualified for the World Cup finals.

Ireland (The Republic of) -- The Irish, in Group 'E,' are playing in only their second World Cup, also having qualified for the 1990 finals. But Ireland made the best of its one and only World Cup, making it to the quarterfinals (despite not winning a single game) before losing, 1-0, to the host Italians. In its five World Cup games, Ireland has no wins, four draws and one loss.

Italy -- Italy, seeded No. 1 in Group 'E,' is one of only three nations to win three World Cup titles (1934, 1938, 1982). Brazil and Germany are the other two. In addition, Italy was the runner-up in 1970. Italy has participated in 12 of the 14 World Cup finals (1934-1954, 1962-1990), hosting the 1990 finals. Its outstanding World Cup record includes 54 matches, with 31 wins, 12 draws and 11 losses.

Korea Republic -- The Republic of Korea, also known as South Korea, is drawn in Group 'C' and is appearing in its fourth World Cup finals, having qualified in 1954, 1986 and 1990. It has not registered a victory in eight World Cup games, drawing one and losing the other seven.

Mexico -- Mexico, in Group 'E,' has appeared in nine World Cup tournaments (1930, 1950-1970, 1978, 1986). It is the only nation to host two World Cup finals (1970, 1986). Mexico's best finish was the quarterfinals in both 1970 and 1986 (the years it served as host). In 1970 Mexico lost, 4-1, to Italy in the quarters, and in 1986 it drew with West Germany, 0-0, but lost 4-1 on penalty kicks. Its World Cup record includes six wins, six draws and 17 losses.

Morocco -- Morocco, in Group 'F,' is playing in its third World Cup, having qualified in 1970 and 1986. Its best finish was the second round in 1986, when it lost 1-0 to eventual runner-up West Germany. Morocco's World Cup record includes one win, three draws and three losses.

The Netherlands -- Often referred to as Holland, the tiny European nation has participated in five World Cups (1934-1938, 1974-1978, 1990). Drawn in Group 'F,' the Netherlands has never won the World Cup, but has finished second twice (1974, 1978). In 1974, it lost, 2-1, to West Germany, and in 1978 it dropped the championship game, 3-1, to Argentina. Holland's World Cup record includes eight wins, six draws and six losses.

Nigeria -- Nigeria, in Group 'D,' is one of three countries playing in its first World Cup (the others are Greece and Saudi Arabia), and one of four if you count Russia, which played previously as a part of the Soviet Union.

Norway -- The Norwegians, in Group 'E,' are participating in only their second World Cup tournament, the other in 1938, when they lost their one and only game, 2-1, to the eventual champion, Italy.

Romania -- The Romanians, in Group 'A,' have taken part in five World Cup finals (1930-1938, 1970, 1990). Romania's best finish was a 1990 second-round loss to the Republic of Ireland, 0-0 in regulation, and then 5-4 on penalty kicks. Its combined World Cup record is three wins, three draws and six losses.

Russia -- As Russia, this team has not competed in the World Cup, although as part of the Soviet Union it had a hand in seven finals. Drawn in Group 'B,' Russia has most of the top talent from the former Soviet Union team. The Soviet Union compiled a World Cup record of 15 wins, six draws and 10 losses, making it to the semifinals in 1966, losing, 2-1, to Portugal.

Saudi Arabia -- The Saudis, in Group 'F,' are participating in their first World Cup tournament, qualifying as one of two entries from the Asian group.

Spain -- The Spanish, in Group 'C,' have participated in eight World Cup tournaments (1934, 1950, 1962-1966, 1978-1990). Spain served as host for the 1982 World Cup. Its best finish was a quarterfinal loss to Belgium in 1986, 1-1 and 5-4 on penalty kicks. Spain's World Cup record includes 13 wins, seven draws and 12 losses.

Sweden -- The Swedes, in Group 'B,' have taken part in eight World Cups (1934- 1950, 1958, 1970-1978, 1990). Sweden served as host of the tournament in 1958, and had its best World Cup then, earning runner-up honors to Brazil, losing 5-2 in the final. In its 31 World Cup games, Sweden has 11 wins, six draws and 14 losses.

Switzerland -- The Swiss, in Group 'A,' have appeared in six World Cup tournaments, but have not participated in 28 years (1934-1954, 1962-1966). Switzerland's best World Cup finish was a 7-5 quarterfinal loss to Austria in 1954, when it served as the host nation. Its World Cup record includes five wins, two draws and 11 losses.

United States -- The U.S., seeded No. 1 in Group 'A,' has participated in four other World Cups (1930-1934, 1950, 1990). The best finish by a U.S. team in World Cup play was third place in the first tournament in 1930, where it dropped a 6-1 decision to eventual runner-up Argentina. In its 1950 appearance, the U.S. enjoyed its most celebrated World Cup moment with a 1-0 upset win over England. Its tournament record includes three wins, no draws and seven losses.
