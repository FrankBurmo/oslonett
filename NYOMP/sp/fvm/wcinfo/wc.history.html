World Cup History Book: 1930 - 1990

  • FIFA: Federation Internationale de Football Association

  • Some Soccer History

    • During a special soccer competition in 1930, the winning team received a gold cup. The games were named The World Cup and have been called that ever since.

    • Soccer teams had more players and longer "fields" back then too. Sometimes the goal was in the next village and the game would last for days. Native Americans played long games like this before the Pilgrims came.

    • The first woman soccer player to be admitted to the Soccer Hall of Fame was Alfredda Ingle hart in 1927. She taught soccer for over 30 years to many children who grew up to become well-known players.

    • The only U.S. team to ever win a world championship was a women's national team. They beat Norway in 1991, 2-1, to take the title. Several years earlier, in 1978, the U.S. women's team placed third in world competition. Both of these events were proud moments for the U.S. and major feats for women in sports.

  • The Original Soccer Ball

    The "ball" was made of animal skin on the outside and filled with hair on the inside. People kicked the ball across a "goal," but the game was much rougher than it is now. It was common to kick someone's shins, and players often suffered broken bones! 

  •  LOGO AND MASCOT
    The official logo for the 1994 FIFA World Cup was unveiled April 17, 1991, and contains a blend of symbols which says "America" and "soccer" in one eye-catching glance. The logo combines an image of the traditional Stars and Stripes along with a dynamic, speeding soccer ball. The symbol dramatically illustrates unity between a soaring soccer ball and a billowing American flag. The soccer ball and the stars and stripes were selected for the motif because of their recognizability and popular appeal. In combination, they link soccer with American culture and promote the 1994 World Cup as an international event.

    Beginning in the summer of 1990, World Cup USA 1994 conducted a competitive search for a symbol to represent the 1994 World Cup soccer championships. After a keen competition, the winning design was created by Pentagram Design, an internationally renowned design firm based in New York City.

    World Cup USA 1994 unveiled the official mascot for the 1994 FIFA World Cup on January 14, 1992. The mascot was developed by Warner Brothers Animation Studios, is a playful cartoon-character dog. The character represents the youth, vitality and excitement of soccer in the US as well as within the 178 members of FIFA. After a nationwide contest to find a name for the mascot, World Cup USA 1994 unveiled the winning name -- Striker -- on October 22, 1992.

    The "Name the Mascot" campaign was the first national promotion organized by World Cup USA 1994 and was sponsored by the 1994 FIFA WC official sponsors. The campaign was a six week promotion with more than 25,000 Americans voting for their favorite name. Striker, in soccer terms, is the player on the team who always gets the job done and who can be counted on to take the ball, score, and excite the crowd.

  • "Soccer" or "Football"?

    Did you know?

    • Soccer is called football in every other country except the United States, Australia, and Canada where its called 'soccer', and Italy where it is called 'calcio' .

    • In the United Kingdom, schools used soccer as part of their physical education. When two different schools decided to play a game together, they discovered they had different rules. For a long time, no one could agree on how soccer should be played. Some people wanted more players on the team. Others wanted to carry the ball with their hands instead of just kicking it.

    • Finally, a team in Cambridge agreed on a set of rules for a kicking game of soccer. They formed a group called the Football Association to oversee the game.

    • As immigrants arrived in the U.S. from many European nations, soccer came with them. The new citizens formed their own clubs and teams, and soccer continued to thrive as our country grew.

    • To distinguish the game from the football played in the U.S., it was called Association Football. It was then shortened to Assoc. Football. Some people just called it assoc. Then, because it was common to add an 'er' to words at that time, 'er' was added to 'assoc' and then just 'soc' to make the word soccer. The name has stuck ever since.
