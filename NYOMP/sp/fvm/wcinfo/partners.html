Technology

The Technology Department is essential to attaining the WCOC's mission to present the "greatest World Cup in history, and to leave a legacy for soccer in the United States," because of the role technology plays in supporting all WCOC operating departments. To that end, the Technology Department produced a high-caliber, state-of- the-art technology systems for implementation and operation of the 1994 FIFA World Cup, and will leave a technology legacy for FIFA and U.S. Soccer.

The Technology Department operates in three major areas: Information Technology, Telecommunications and Electronics. Given the geographical dimensions of the United States and the size of the event, these areas are crucial to most of the WCOC's operational programs and the success of the World Cup.

To accomplish the enormous tasks required in 1994, the WCOC secured the assistance of three industry giants in the technology arena -- Electronic Data Systems (EDS), Sun Microsystems and Sprint. With the aid of these "Big Three" technology partners, the WCOC is assembling worldclass packages in information services, telecommunications and computers.

Additional support is being provided by Official Sponsors Canon, JVC and Fuji for copiers, facsimile machines, camera repair, televisions, consumer electronics and film development.

Technology Equipment Suppliers Best, Parallax, Sybase, Symbol Technology and VMX are providing power protection devices; advanced video boards for Accreditation and broadcast statistics displays; database, development and multimedia software; bar code readers for access control and inventory; and voice mail and answering systems.

Information Technology

Information Technology delivers two broad areas of services: Office Management Applications (OMA) and Event Management Applications (EMA). OMA encompasses the integration and installation of software applications for office use (word processing, electronic mail, spreadsheets, etc.) networked into all WCOC locations. EMA includes development and integration of custom software applications for World Cup operations, including accreditation (with access control), logistics (scheduling people, equipment and services) and World Cup News Services (WCNS), among many other functions.

Electronic Data Systems (EDS) is the Official Information Technology Partner of the 1994 World Cup. EDS professionals are working as part of the WCOC Technology Department to develop, implement and integrate all applications and computer hardware for the 1994 World Cup.

EDS staff will install WCOC systems and software, and will direct network operations at their computer center in Dallas. A dedicated EDS development center in Anaheim is writing customized applications for WCOC operations. EDS systems and support will allow WCOC staff to operate on a nationwide basis using common OMA and EMA tools, sharing documents and data on the national network. The applications include:

  • Accreditation -- This system will store records and photographs for all accredited participants, staff, volunteers, media, guests and service personnel. The system will produce customized security badges in about five minutes with an individualized computer video picture. Providing video photographs over a nationwide network is a World Cup technological first. Parallax has provided advanced video boards that allow a picture from a JVC video camcorder to be displayed, stored and printed on the Sun workstations.

  • Logistics -- This is a group of applications that will allow WCOC departments to track inventories, staff, events, services, equipment and schedules for the 1994 World Cup. Applications will be customized for all major departments, and will facilitate national cooperation between operations centered in nine separate venues.

  • Information Services -- The major portion of this application will provide games information to press and official participants. The largest portion is the World Cup News Service, which is described below.

EDS is using tools provided by the Equipment Supplier, Sybase, for these applications. All EMA applications have a colorful World Cup look to facilitate ease-of-use for staff and volunteers. The EDS information technology applications will be left behind as a legacy for the future operations of U.S. Soccer and FIFA.

World Cup News Service (WCNS)

The World Cup News Service is designed to provide up-to-the-second results, historical data, current news and releases, and general information to World Cup participants. The WCNS audience will include VIPs, WCOC staff, official guests, players and the nearly 10,000 accredited media. WCNS will be available on Sun data base workstations at all WCOC locations and at the Media Centers. WCNS provides access to a sophisticated data base featuring:

  • Official FIFA Databank -- This includes historical results from previous World Cup competitions from 1930 through 1990, as well as all qualifying results for World Cup USA '94 . Final results from 1994 games will be included in the FIFA Databank when they are official and certified.

  • FIFA and World Cup News Bulletins -- These include news updates, announcements and bulletins from FIFA and World Cup organizers, as well as "Notes & Quotes" from newsworthy World Cup participants.

  • World Cup Results, Schedules, Statistics and Teams -- This will include current results, upcoming schedules of events (news conferences, press briefings and other media events), on-line match statistics and team information (rosters, biographies and player performance updates).

  • Local Organizing Committee and Venues -- Details about administration and preparation for the '94 World Cup and venue (city and stadia) profiles will be included, as well as information about the World Cup logo and mascot, sponsors and marketing partners, entertainment, government relations and security.

  • Word Processing Programs -- Accredited journalists will have access, to a simple word processing program in order to create and print confidential documents and stories.

WCNS will display information in multiple languages for our international audience. Users may select their preferred language when first entering the system, or switch to another language.

Games results will be collected by statisticians during the actual games and entered automatically into WCNS using Sun workstations in the Press Tribune. The sophisticated Commentator Statistics System will use Parallax video boards to relay games statistics as they are recorded to broadcasters over closed-circuit television.

Working with FIFA, WCOC has developed a globally accessible "FIFA World Cup Bulletin Board System (BBS)" for access to WCNS information before, during and after the 1994 World Cup. The system will allow the worldwide media and (newspapers, radio and television) and public to obtain historical information from the official data bank provided by FIFA, as well as updated news and results from qualifying and 1994 tournament games. The BBS started operation in March 1994 through the international CompuServe network.

The FIFA Databank, with the WCNS software, will be returned to FIFA at the end of the 1994 World Cup, to provide continued media and public support until 1998.

Computer Systems

Sun Microsystems is the exclusive computer supplier to the 1994 World Cup. Sun is the leading manufacturer of systems for client-server computing, including workstations (for individual users), servers (to allow workstations to share information over networks), systems software, printers, networking products and related technology that use the UNIX operating system.

Sun Microsystems workstations are much more powerful than personal computers. Their advanced internal and physical design represent the industry's state-of-the-art for general purpose workstations. They are recognized worldwide leaders in engineering, 2-D and 3-D graphics, and business applications.

Sun is providing over 1,000 powerful workstations and servers for 1994 WCOC operations. Their workstations provide support for both desktop office applications for individual users, and networked data base applications for both departmental and national operating groups. They also are providing their latest and most powerful central data base server, the SPARCcenter 2000, which is installed at the EDS Data Center in Dallas. The SPARCcenter server will provide a shared, secure, redundant repository for all WCOC data.

Power protection equipment for the Sun workstations, provided by the Equipment Supplier Best, will prevent damage from power failures during the 1994 games. Symbol Technology will provide radio-operated hand-held bar code readers to verify accreditation badges at access gates, and to read state-of-the-art 2-dimensional bar code control stickers attached to all major equipment items.

More than 100 Sun systems will be left to provide a legacy of continuing operations, using WCOC Information Technology for both U.S. Soccer and FIFA.

Telecommunications

Sprint's sponsorship of the 1994 World Cup and U.S. Soccer is the largest corporate sponsorship in the company's history. Sprint has become the first telecommunications company in the U.S. to have a major presence in long-distance, local and cellular communications. Sprint remains the only 100-percent fiber optic company, providing the highest quality voice, data and video transmission services.

Sprint is providing long-distance services for all WCOC locations and operations. They will be providing calling card services for the press and other official participants at World Cup games and other WCOC events. They have installed the WCOC's nationwide data network, and are providing data services to FIFA in Zurich. Their data services allow World Cup to provide very high-speed access to all World Cup cities, and key WCOC locations in each venue.

Other telecommunications services (some of which are provided by Sprint) include: telephone, paging, cellular and radio services, as well as frequency coordination for the wide range of transmissions from multiple broadcasters, entertainment productions, closed-circuit television and two-way radio communications.

VMX will provide voice answering, voice mail and voice response systems to augment WCOC telephone systems, at all offices and on the stadium grounds.
