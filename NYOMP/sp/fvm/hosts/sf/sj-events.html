Ethnic festivals have replaced blossoming fruit trees as star attractions in the city once known as the Garden City in the Valley of Heart's Delight. And no wonder. Along with the development of the valley's high tech industry, the area's ethnic diversity was taking root. Now home to some of the most prominent technology companies in the world, Silicon Valley at the same time boasts a population that is more than a quarter Hispanic and nearly 20 percent Asian. San José, as "Capital of Silicon Valley," is host to several breathtaking ethnic festivals celebrating these and other cultures from around the world.

With 300 days of sunshine each year, San Joséans hardly let a month go by without some type of celebration. In the beginning of the year, the Vietnamese community commemorates their New Year with a "Hoi Tet" festival, complete with table tennis tournaments and martial arts demonstrations. The Irish turn the city "emerald green" the week before St. Patrick's Day with a visit from Dublin's Lord Mayor and a Gaelic football game.

The Mexicans bring out their tortilla presses and mariachi bands for two rousing street festivals, Cinco de Mayo in May and Mexican Independence Day in September. And the African-American community celebrates in mid-June its rich heritage of music, plays and poetry.

One ethnic group with a strong, colorful presence in San José are the Japanese-Americans. They begin the first weekend of May with their Nikkei Matsuri, a Japanese-American Cultural Festival held on the streets of Japantown. In mid-June, the Children's Discovery Museum presents a Japanese Children's Festival with demonstrations and hands-on activities.

Next comes the Japanese Obon Festival, the weekend after the Fourth of July, honoring the deceased with beautiful Obon dances. Finally, in mid-September, fall is ushered in with the Aki Matsuri, an outdoor event featuring music and food.

Even festivals that celebrate American holidays, such as the Fourth of July and Labor Day, have a distinctive multi-cultural flavor in San José. San José America Festival on the Fourth of July weekend presents many ethnically themed arts and crafts booths, and multi-cultural performers. The Tapestry In Talent's Festival of the Arts on Labor Day weekend celebrates not only the arts but the ethnic diversity of the county.

People attend ethnic festivals for many reasons. Some come because of warm childhood memories of a culture no longer practiced. Others come because of past travels to a country where they enjoyed the people and customs. Still others come, plain and simply, for the food. Organizers of the San José Greek Festival, held at St. Nicholas Greek Orthodox Church, say that 95 percent of their attendees are non-Greeks who come year after year because of the delicious gyro, souvlaki, dolmathes, and pastitsio prepared by local Greek cooks from old world recipes.

The following are the most popular ethnic festivals in San José:

January/February

Hoi Tet Festival - The date of the Vietnamese New Year's Festival changes from year to year (it's February 11-13 in 1994), but the celebration, held at the Santa Clara County Fairgrounds, is always full of music, food booths, sports, games - even Miss Tet Pageant. Of special interest are table tennis and martial arts tournaments. 408/295-9210.

March

Irish Week Celebration, prior to St. Patrick's day, brings out new Irish-Americans, expatriates, and people who wish they were Irish. Events include a music concert at Plaza Park, soccer and Gaelic football competitions, luncheons and dinners. 408/629-3618.

May

Cinco de Mayo is the largest Mexican celebration in San José. Held the first Sunday prior to the fifth of May, the festival spills out all over the streets of downtown San José with a parade, entertainment, crafts, and art exhibits. 408/250-0663.

Nikkei Matsuri is a Japanese-American Cultural Festival held in Japantown between Fifth and Jackson Streets on the first Sunday in May. Cultural displays, food and entertainment are all part of the festivities. 408/298-4303.

June

San José Greek Festival prides itself in its wide variety and high caliber of Greek cooking. Also, many of the dance groups, wearing authentic costumes, are winners in dance competitions. The festival, held at St. Nicholas Greek Orthodox Church, is scheduled the last weekend in May if it doesn't fall on Memorial Day or the first weekend in June. 408/246-2770.

Japanese Children's Festival at the Children's Discovery Museum gives children a wonderful opportunity to experience the Japanese culture through demonstrations and hands-on activities. It is held the third Saturday in June. 408/298-5437.

Juneteenth Festival brings out the African-American community to Spartan Stadium near the San José State University campus. Held the third weekend of June, it features artists performing black music, plays, and poetry. 408/292-0157.

July

San José America Festival, produced by the San José Downtown Association as a Fourth of July annual event, has an extensive multi-cultural arts showcase, featuring the region's finest ethnic performing groups. 408/279-1775.

San José International Mariachi Conference & Festival highlights the romance in the music of Hispanic courtship traditions with workshops on music and dance, concerts, religious mass, a colorful procession and all-day festival. 408/241-3299.

Obon Festival, held the weekend after the Fourth of July, honors the deceased with beautiful Obon dances performed at the Buddhist Church. 408/293-9292.

September

Mexican Independence Day is celebrated in downtown San José with a parade, cultural exhibits, and plenty of lively entertainment. Held Sunday after Labor Day. 408/258-0663.

Aki Matsuri ushers in the coming of autumn with a traditional Japanese-American outdoor festival including children's activities and costumed dancers. Held at the Wesley Methodist Church, the festival is scheduled the third Saturday in September. 408/295-0367.

Octoberfest brings out German-Americans for Bavarian food, German beer and a rousing dance band. Held at the Germania at The Hochburg, the festival is held on the last two Saturdays in September and all Friday and Saturday nights in October. 408/295-4484.

October

Italian American Cultural Festival recreates an Italian village with decorated food, wine, and crafts booths. Festivities, held at the Santa Clara County Fairgrounds, include opera, booce ball and a street dance. 408/293-7122.

December

Las Posadas celebrates Christmas in the Hispanic tradition with a reenactment of the search for shelter by Jesus's parents in Bethlehem. Part of San José's annual month-long Christmas in the Park celebration, Las Posadas takes the form of a procession through downtown. 408/279-1775.

For a detailed calendar of events and other information about visiting San José, contact the San José Convention & Visitors Bureau, 333 West San Carlos Street, Suite 1000, San José, CA 95110. Or call the FYI Events Line, 408/295-2265.
