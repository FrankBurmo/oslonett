Information

Surrounded on three sides by the Pacific Ocean and San Francisco Bay, San Francisco's compact 46 square miles (125 sq. km.) crowd the tip of the San Francisco Peninsula. ``The City'' has a population of 723,959; nevertheless it looms large in the imagination as the hub of the Greater Bay Area. The nation's fifth largest metropolitan region registers a population of 6 million and earns its sobriquet ``Everybody's Favorite City'' by hosting over 13 million visitors, conventioneers and business travelers each year.

Weather

 Despite California's scorching reputation, San Francisco is blessed with a temperate marine climate and enjoys mild weather year-round. Temperatures seldom rise above 70°F (21°C) or fall below 40°F (5°C). Morning and evening fogs roll in during the summer months, but rarely persist. Visitors are most comfortable with a light jacket or coat handy, or in a suit. An all-weather coat will take the chill off cool evenings. Lightweight summer clothes are seldom practical in San Francisco.

Phone Facts

 All area codes 415 unless noted

Emergency Dialing Dial 911 to get help fast in any emergency situation. An operator will connect you to the police, fire department or ambulance.

Area Code On September 2, 1991 the 415 area code was split. The new 510 area code encompasses all of Alameda and Contra Costa counties in the East Bay, including the communities of Oakland, Berkeley, Concord, Pleasanton and Livermore. For dialing outside the 415 area code, callers must dial 1 plus the appropriate area code and seven-digit number.

Transportation

Airport

Just 14 miles south of the City, San Francisco International Airport is the fifth busiest in the United States, seventh in the world. Forty-two major scheduled carriers serve the airport. There are 36,600 flights a month, 1,260 a day. A $1.7 billion expansion project will be completed by 1995. The garage, expanded to accommodate 7,000 cars, is recommended for short-term, protected parking.

Bay Area Transit

San Francisco Municipal Railway 949 Presidio Avenue, 673-6864. San Francisco has one of the highest per capita transit riderships in the U.S. The fare is $1, seniors, disabled and youths 35 cents, under age 5 free. Exact change is required (except on cable cars); drivers carry no change!

Free transfers are valid for two changes of vehicle in any direction within two hours.

Muni Passports are available for one day ($6), three days ($10) or seven days ($15) and are valid on all Muni regularly scheduled service, including cable cars, and for a reduced fare to ballgames at Candlestick Park. Passports are sold at the San Francisco Visitor Information Center, lower level, Hallidie Plaza, Powell and Market Streets; TIX Bay Area, Stockton Street on Union Square; City Hall Information Booth; Muni Headquarters, 949 Presidio Avenue, Room 239.

Route information is published in the Yellow Pages or may be obtained by calling 673-MUNI. Complete timetables available on buses, Metro station agent booths, AC Transit ticket booth and elsewhere. An official map of San Francisco streets and Muni routes is $2.

Cable Cars operate along three routes: the Powell-Hyde line begins at Powell and Market Streets and terminates at Victorian Park near the Maritime Museum and Aquatic Park; the Powell-Mason line also begins at Powell and Market but terminates at Bay and Taylor near Fisherman's Wharf; and the California Street line runs from California and Market Streets to Van Ness Avenue. Cable car fares are $2 (one-way), $1 for seniors and disabled from 9 pm-midnight and 6-7 am.

Buses are numbered with destinations marked on the front. Stops are usually marked by signs, curb markings and yellow bands on adjacent utility poles.

Muni Metro Streetcars operate underground downtown and on the streets in the outer neighborhoods. Five lines are involved: J (Church), K (Ingleside), L (Taraval), M (Ocean View) and N (Judah).

Owl Service Buses serve major transit corridors from 1 to 5 am.

Note: All fares and costs are subject to change.

Bay Area Rapid Transit (BART) 800 Madison Street (Oakland) 788-BART. The 71-mile BART system is the ``tourist attraction that gets people to the other tourist attractions.'' The space-age fleet links eight San Francisco stations with Daly City to the south and 25 East Bay stations. Trains operate Monday-Friday 4 am to midnight, Saturday 6 am to midnight and Sunday 8 am to midnight. For a special $2.60 excursion fare riders may explore the $5 billion system, tour the architecturally interesting stations and see the countryside. Just don't go through the computerized exits at any of the stops as this will nullify the excursion ticket. Exit only at the point of entry. All tickets are dispensed from machines at the stations. Change machines for coins and dollar bills only are nearby. Before embarking study the color-keyed wall maps which show all destinations and routes.

AC Transit Transbay Terminal, First and Mission Streets, 800/559-4636. AC Transit operates the bus service to Treasure Island, communities in the East Bay -- Berkeley, Oakland, Richmond and other cities in Alameda and Contra Costa counties -- via the San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge.

Golden Gate Transit Transbay Terminal, First and Mission Streets, 332-6600. This bus service links San Francisco to Marin and Sonoma counties via the historic Golden Gate Bridge. Patrons may travel as far north as Santa Rosa or the city of Sonoma, in the heart of the wine country. Buses depart from the Transbay Terminal and follow several routes through the Financial District, the Civic Center area, or the Presidio.

Samtrans Transbay Terminal, First and Mission Streets, 800/660-4BUS. The San Mateo County Transit District's ``Mainline Service'' offers bus service from San Francisco to the San Francisco International Airport and communities on the Peninsula as far south as Palo Alto.

Ferry Service

The ferry tale in San Francisco has a happy ending. At one time 50 white arks crossed the harbor. The opening of the Golden Gate Bridge in 1937 signaled the end of a romantic era. Fifty years later ferries once again ply the bay either on scenic cruises or docking regularly at Alameda, Angel Island, Larkspur, Oakland, Sausalito, Tiburon and Vallejo.

Blue & Gold Fleet Pier 39 (Fisherman's Wharf) 510/552-3300. Daily round-trip service from Oakland's Jack London Waterfront and Alameda's Main Street Terminal to the Ferry Building and PIER 39. One-way fares are $3.50, seniors, disabled and military $2.50, children $1.50. A $25 discount book of 10 round-trips is available, and all fares include AC Transit and Muni transfers.

Golden Gate Ferries Ferry Building, Foot of Market Street, 332-6600, TDD 257-4554. Ferries depart from the south end of the Ferry Building to Sausalito and Larkspur. The M.V. Golden Gate makes frequent half-hour crossings to Sausalito. Three 725-passenger ferries provide frequent service to Larkspur on a 45-minute route. The one-way adult cash fare aboard the Sausalito ferry is $4. The one-way adult cash fare aboard the Larkspur ferry is $2.50 weekdays, $4 on weekends and holidays. A 50 percent discount for seniors (65+) and disabled persons is available; children age 6-12 are entitled to a 25 percent discount. Children under age 5 travel free when accompanied by an adult. ``Family Fare'' on weekends and holidays aboard either the Sausalito or the Larkspur Ferry: Children age 12 and under ride free when accompanied by a full cash fare paying adult. There is a limit of two children per adult.

Harbor Bay Maritime Ferry & Charter Service Pier 48-B, Fisherman's Wharf, 510/769-5500. Daily ferry service between the Ferry Building in San Francisco and Harbor Bay Island in Alameda. Five departures Tuesday-Thursday and eight on Mondays and Fridays between 7 am-6:30 pm; three departures on weekends from 11-5:30; please check schedule for additional summer arrival and departure times. One-way fare is $4, $3 with book of 10 tickets; age 5 and under free.

Red & White Fleet Pier 41 and 43 1/2, Fisherman's Wharf, 546-2700, 800/229-2784. Ferries depart daily from Pier 43 1/2 to Sausalito and Tiburon. Fares are $4.50 for adults, $2.25 for ages 5-11. Angel Island ferries depart San Francisco and Vallejo daily in the summer and on weekends in the winter. San Francisco ferries depart Pier 43 1/2. Fare is $8 round-trip for adults, $4 for ages 5-11; from Vallejo $10.45 for adults, $5.45 for ages 4-12. Commute ferry service on weekdays from Tiburon and Vallejo to the Ferry Building at the foot of Market Street. Ferries bound for Vallejo depart Pier 41. Fare is $15 round-trip for adults, $12 for seniors (62 and older) and ages 13-18, $6.50 for ages 4-12. Bus shuttle service available to Marine World Africa USA. Package fare includes round-trip boat, bus and admission to park, $36 for adults, $30 for seniors (62 and older) and ages 13-18, $20.50 for ages 4-12.
