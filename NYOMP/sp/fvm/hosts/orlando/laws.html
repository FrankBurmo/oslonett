Orlando's Laws and Regulations

Driving in Orlando

You must have a valid driver s license and vehicle registration in your possession while driving. In the U.S., you are required to drive on the right side of the road.

Speed Limits

Unless otherwise specified, the speed limit on interstate highways is 55 miles per hour. In cities and congested areas it will range from 20 to 55 miles (32-88.5 kilometers) per hour. All speed limits are posted in miles per hour and strictly enforced.

Seat Belt Laws

Florida law requires front-seat passengers, regardless of age, to wear a seat belt. Children under the age of 3 are required to be in a safety car seat. Children aged 4 and 5 must be in a safety seat or wear a seat belt. Most rental car companies can provide you with a car seat.

Drinking & Driving

If you are going out and drinking, take along a designated driver who will remain alcohol-free. Under the new Florida PST law, if you are caught driving with a blood alcohol count of .08 percent or higher, or if you refuse breath, blood or urine test requests by a police officer, your driver s license will be suspended immediately by the arresting officer. This law is strictly enforced!

Boating Laws

While boating in Orlando the basic boating rules should be obeyed. Children ages 6 and under must wear a PFD (personal flotation device) on all vessels while under way. Although there is no minimum boat operating age in the state, local cities and counties may have specific ordinances which preempt state law. Many lakes and rivers have boat speed limits, so it s important to know the rules before you get on the water. You must wear a PFD when riding a personal watercraft anywhere in the state, and you must wear a PFD while water skiing in Orange County. Florida PST laws also apply on the water.

Hunting & Fishing

Planning to hunt or fish while on vacation? Make sure you contact the Florida Game and Fresh Water Fish Commission at 407-422-6995. Licenses are required for both residents and non-residents. Licenses are available from county Tax Collector s offices or substations ranging from bait shops to discount stores.

State & Local Taxes

In Florida, there is a 6 or 7 percent sales tax on all merchandise except necessary food purchased in grocery stores. Items such as soda, ice cream and others are taxable. The tax is added to the price marked on the merchandise. There is a 2-4 percent resort tax levied on your hotel room; it is added to the hotel room rate, as is the 6 or 7 percent general sales tax.
