Welcome to Washington, D.C.

As mayor of the District of Columbia, it is my pleasure to assist you in planning your visit to Washington, DC.

You know Washington for its monuments, museums, grand architecture and beautiful avenues. Beyond these symbols of power is a city rich in tradition, arts and culture. It's a city of historic and colorful neighborhoods, parks and people.

Washington is an international gateway to trade, finance, communication and information. It's also home to more than 150 embassies and scores of think tanks, trade associations, financial institutions, universities and medical research facilities.

As you plan your visit, select one of Washington's 98 hotels, and be sure to take advantage of special reduced rates on weekends, summer and holidays.

Once here, I encourage you to experience our award-winning Metrorail system for fast, safe and convenient access around the city.

The nation's capital is rich in history and character with an exciting diversity found nowhere else in the world. Come and enjoy!

Sincerely,

Sharon Pratt Kelly
Mayor

Getting Around

Diamond-shaped, the District of Columbia is 10 square miles and divided into four quadrants: NW, NE, SW, SE with the U.S. Capitol at the center. North Capitol Street, South Capitol Street, East Capitol Street and the National Mall radiate like spokes from a wheel.

Lettered streets run east to west. After the alphabet has been exhausted, the east-west series continues with two-syllable names (Adams, Belmont) then three-syllable names (Allison, Buchanan). Numbered streets run north to south. Avenues named for U.S. states run diagonally and intersect with the east-west, north-south streets at traffic circles.

Before setting out, be sure that you know the right quadrant for your destination. A given address may be found in any of the quadrants.

Metro System

Metrorail and Metrobus provide a safe, clean and efficient way of getting around the nation's capital. Five rail lines combine with an extensive bus system, connecting the District and the Maryland and Virginia suburbs.

Train lines are named for colors: Red, Yellow, Orange, Blue and Green. Station entrances are marked by brown pylons capped by the letter "M" and colored stripes indicating which lines are available. Route maps are posted inside each station and subway car.

Trains operate 5:30 a.m.-midnight Monday-Friday and 8 a.m.-midnight Saturday and Sunday. Each train displays the name of its farthest destination. Buses travel to Georgetown and other areas not serviced by the subway.

Rail farecards must be purchased at vending machines located inside the stations. The base fare is $1.25 for travel Monday-Friday 5:30-9:30 a.m. and 3-7 p.m. and $1 at all other times. For convenience, there is a one-day pass available for $5. Riders may use this pass anytime after 9:30 a.m. weekdays and all day weekends and holidays.

For schedule information contact Washington Metropolitan Area Transit Authority, 600 Fifth St. NW, Washington, DC 20001. Phone: (202) 637-7000.

Taxis

DC cabs are plentiful and can be easily hailed on major streets. Taxis operate on a zone system instead of meters. By law, basic rates must be posted in the cab. There is a $1.25 charge for each additional passenger and a $1 surcharge during evening rush hours (4-6:30 p.m.). Telephoning for a cab adds $1.50 to the fare. A ride for one passenger between National Airport and downtown is about $10.

Suburban cabs have metered fares. Those with Virginia or Maryland license plates may transport you in and out of the District, but not between points within the District. For details, contact the DC Taxicab Commission, 2041 Martin Luther King, Jr. Ave. SE, Suite 204, Washington, DC 20020. Phone: (202) 767-8319.

Driving

During rush hour, certain major arteries change in favor of rush hour traffic; be sure to read street signs carefully. Parking is restricted during rush hours and during some weekend hours as posted on street signs. If your vehicle is towed Friday after 7 p.m or anytime on weekends, you will not be able to retrieve it until Monday after 9 a.m. For details, call the DC Department of Public Works towing information at (202) 727-9200 or (202) 727-9201.

Climate

Winters here are short, with more rain than snow. Warm weather usually prevails from April until as late as October. Monthly high and low average temperatures (Fahrenheit) are:

Disabled Travelers

Most of the monuments and museums are accessible to disabled visitors. Metrorail has elevator access from the street entrance and between platforms. For a copy of Access Washington: A Guide to Metropolitan Washington for the Physically Disabled, please call the Information Protection and Advocacy Center for the Handicapped (202) 966-8081.

Travel Tips

Around the City

  • Become familiar with your surroundings; carefully study maps and guide books before setting out.
  • Do not display cash openly; use travelers checks whenever possible.
  • Park in well-lit areas.
  • Remove all personal belongings from your car or lock them in the trunk.
  • Always lock your car and take the key.
  • At night, travel in well-lit areas and don't walk alone.
  • Aggressive panhandling is discouraged by DC law; please refrain from direct contact with panhandlers.

At Your Hotel

  • Secure valuables in the hotel safe or safety deposit box.
  • Locate fire and emergency exits and study evacuation procedures.
  • If you detect any suspicious activity or noise, contact the front desk or hotel security.

On the Metro

  • Property lost can be sought at Metro Lost and Found. Hours: Monday-Friday 11 a.m.-3:30 p.m. For details, call (202) 962-1195.
  • When in doubt, ask a uniformed station manager or transit police officer for assistance.

Taxis

  • Make a note of the cab name and number in case you lose something or want to make a complaint or compliment.
