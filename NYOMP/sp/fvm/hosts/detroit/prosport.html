DETROIT'S PROFESSIONAL SPORTS

Background:
Often referred to as "The City of Champions," Detroit is home to basketball, hockey, baseball and football professional sports teams.
The Detroit Pistons, 1989 and 1990 NBA World Champions, play at The Palace of Auburn Hills. The arena offers 20,000 seats, with each offering a fantastic view of the action on the court.
Downtown on the riverfront, the Detroit Red Wings play National Hockey League teams in the stadium named after the late African-American boxing hero who grew up in the city. Built in the 1979, Joe Louis Arena seats 19,000 and is also the site of many entertainment events.
The city's oldest team, the Detroit Tigers, play American League baseball at Tiger Stadium, the historic playing field at Michigan and Trumbull avenues. The Tigers captured the World Championship in 1984 with an incredible 35-5 starting record.
The National Football League Detroit Lions play their games at the Pontiac Silverdome, just 45 minutes from downtown Detroit. The Silverdome is the nation's largest air-supported domed stadium, with a seating capacity of 80,638.

Extra Special:
Detroit is also home to several additional sports teams and prestigious sports tournaments. The Detroit Turbo's are the city's rough-and-tumble professional indoor lacrosse league; professional indoor soccer is represented with the Detroit Rockers; the Great Lakes Invitational, college hockey's premier tournament, is played at Joe Louis Arena; each year in June the Detroit Grand Prix offers the excitement of Indy Car racing; the world famous hydroplanes take to the Detroit River each June for the American Power Boat Association's Gold Cup, the Indy 500 of hydroplane racing; and the PGA Senior Players Championship is played annually at the Tournament Players Course (TPC) in Dearborn.
