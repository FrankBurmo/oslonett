THE DETROIT SCIENCE CENTER

Background:
Established in 1978, the Detroit Science Center is an important link in providing informal science and math education not found in the classroom. The Center brings the wonder of science to more than 100,000 children and adults annually.

What's Special:
The Detroit Science Center is the home of Michigan's only Omnimax Theater. Omnimax is the largest image in motion picture history and has many stunning effects. The theater is 3 1/2 stories tall with a 16-speaker sound system more powerful than a jet engine. The film is projected on a domed screen and surrounds the audience with "larger than life" size images.
The Discovery Theater constructed in 1988 provides programs featuring inner and outer space exploration including: interactive video presentations, live solar and lunar presentations and a video microscope which projects microscopic images on to a 100 square foot screen.

Activities:

The Science Center features many special events and shows. Starting in February and showing until mid-summer is "Search for the Great Sharks," a large film format puts you "in the picture" and provides a unique sense of closeness to these creatures.
Also starting in February and showing through mid-summer, the Rolling Stones "At the Max" is featured in this giant screen concert.

Details:
The Detroit Science Center is located in the University Cultural Center at 5020 John R Street, Detroit, MI 48202. (313) 577-8400.
Science Center hours are 10 a.m.-5 p.m. Monday through Friday. The Science Center operates on a rotating schedule for show times. Admission is $6.50 for adults and $4.50 for children. Prices vary for shows offered.
