DETROIT GRAND PRIX

Background:
For three days each June, the excitement of world-class auto racing arrives in the motor city when 28 Indy cars take to the streets of Belle Isle Park. Each year, the western tip of the island is transformed into a state-of-the-art temporary street course. The 2.18 mile, 12-turn course is considered one of the most challenging in the world. The race is celebrating its 13th year in 1994.
More than 150,000 race fans stream onto Belle Isle Park over the three days to witness the racing action. Some of the top racing talent in the world participate, including: Mario and Michael Andretti, Nigel Mansell, Al Unser, Jr., and Bobby Rahal.
Fans also have the opportunity to see the who's who in automotive world, as many top car company executives and the industry's movers and shakers participate in the exciting weekend.
The race is sanctioned by Bloomfield Hills, Michigan-based Championship Auto Racing Teams (CART).

What's Special:
Three additional support races round out the racing action. The SCCA Trans-Am series, the Firestone Indy Lights series, the minor leagues of IndyCar racing, Barber-Saab racing series, and new in 1994, the Chrysler Neon Celebrity race. Rock musicians, Hollywood stars and local personalities will pilot identical Chrysler Neons in a 10-lap free-for-all.
Extra Special:
Race organizers traditionally welcome fans to a day of free racing on Friday of race weekend. Known as "Free Prix Day," all areas of the course are open to spectators as trials are run.
A 5K corporate challenge run will let runners compete on the island's raceway.

Details:
The Detroit Grand Prix is organized by Motormarketing International of Detroit, Inc., a division of IMG, 300 Riverplace, Suite 4000, Detroit, MI 48207, (313) 393-7749.
Tickets for the races vary in price from $10 to $125 per person. The 1994 event is scheduled for June 10-12.
