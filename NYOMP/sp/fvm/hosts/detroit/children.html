CHILDREN'S MUSEUM

Background:
The Children's Museum began as a joint venture between the Detroit Board of Education and the Museum of Art in 1917. In 1925, the Board of Education assumed full responsibility for the museum. Since then, it has developed as an educational unit of the Detroit Public Schools, expanding its collections and activities to include all subjects areas of the curriculum.
The shapes and patterns of the stars can be discovered in the Planetarium, and visitors immediately feel welcome as they pass (and are encouraged to climb upon) "Silverbolt," a life-size horse sculpture created out of automobile bumpers.
Activities:
The Museum has exhibits on West African Textiles, the Inuit (Eskimos), Indians of Michigan, Bridges to Peace, Indonesia- a country of many islands, and a Bengal Tiger. Games and impromptu make-and-take workshops are related to the exhibits.
Details:
