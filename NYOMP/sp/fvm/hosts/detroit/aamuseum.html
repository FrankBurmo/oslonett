JOHN FREEMAN WALLS HISTORIC SITE
AND UNDERGROUND RAILROAD MUSEUM

Background:
Opened to the public in 1985, this attraction recreates the journey enslaved people took to freedom on the Underground Railroad and concludes that journey at the attraction's log cabin built in 1896 as a terminal of the Underground Railroad.
The site includes an historical marker, the Freedom Train Museum, John Freeman Walls restored log cabin, and the Sir John Graves Simcoe Educational Resource Log Cabin.
Details:
The John Freeman Walls Historic Site and Underground Railroad Museum is located eight miles east of the Detroit/Windsor border in Maidstone Township (Highway 401,
Exit Puce Road, north one mile), (519) 727-6555.
The attraction is open daily July 1-Labor Day 10 a.m.-5 p.m. Groups by appointment, admission.

THE NORTH AMERICAN BLACK
HISTORICAL MUSEUM AND CULTURAL CENTRE

Background:
The idea for the museum was conceived in 1964 by Melvin Simpson, who believed that such problems as restricted housing and limited access to jobs and quality education could be addressed more effectively by a community with greater self awareness and pride in being African American. Simpson received support for his project from the Nazery A.M.E. Church of Amherstburg as well as the community.
Initially housed in a restored log house, the new museum and cultural centre were dedicated in September 1981. The complex now includes the museum, the restored log structure and the Nazery A.M.E. Church.
The museum features exhibitions and programs relevant to the history and culture of African Americans in Canada. The museum also has an outstanding collection of artifacts and other resources pertaining to the material culture of Canadians of African descent.

Details:
The museum and cultural centre is located just east of Ontario Highway 18, approximately a half hour drive from the Detroit/Windsor border on 277 King St., Amherstburg, Ontario, N9V 2C7, (519) 736-5433.
The complex is open April-Nov., Wed.-Fri. 10 a.m.-5 p.m., Sat. and Sun. 1-5 p.m.
Contact:Keith Kaminski, MDCVB, (313) 259-4333

UNCLE TOM'S CABIN HISTORIC SITE



Background:
This nationally known historic site consists of six buildings remaining from the pre-Civil War community established by Reverend Josiah Henson as a haven for escaped slaves. Born into slavery in 1830, he was later immortalized as Uncle Tom in Harriet Beecher Stowe's famous novel. Henson escaped to Canada with his family.
Henson helped to establish Canada's first vocational school as a way of helping people in his settlement become self-supporting.
A tour of the site includes the smoke house, made from the trunk of a giant sycamore tree, a former slave house, the home of Reverend Henson and his family's cemetery plot. The museum contains artifacts that were used to punish slaves as well as items and farm implements that were used by the early settlers.

Details:
The site is located in Dresden, Ontario (Kent County Road 40, one km southwest of Dresden off Park Street -- approximately 75 minutes from the Detroit/Windsor border), (519) 683-2978.
Hours are late May-last weekend in September, Mon.- Sat. 11 a.m. - 5 p.m., Sun. 1- 5 p.m. Post Labor Day, Mon.-Sat. noon-5 p.m., Sun. 1- 5 p.m., admission.
Contact:Keith Kaminski, MDCVB, (313) 259-4333

RALEIGH TOWNSHIP CENTENNIAL MUSEUM



Background:
The museum, officially opened in 1967, was Raleigh Township's Centennial Project as a memorial to the Elgin Settlement, haven for fugitives of the American system of slavery in the pre-Civil War years.
The Elgin Settlement, a significant last stop on the Underground Railroad, was founded in 1849. Under the guidance and supervision of Rev. William King, this historic black settlement soon flourished, becoming a self-sufficient community of some 1,200 to 2,000 persons. Now known as the town of Buxton, the Elgin Settlement is one of the few remaining Black Canadian settlements still in existence.
The museum's prime concern is the preservation of material and artifacts of Raleigh, with special emphasis on the history and accomplishments of the original settlers in the Elgin Settlement and their descendants. Among other things, it houses the bed, dresser, diary and copies of papers belonging to Reverend King, as well as many other articles and papers of historical significance.
The museum also has a research library and cultural room featuring works of several African-American artists of local origins.

Details:
The Museum is located in North Buxton (Highway 401 to Bloomfield Road, west on 8th Concession, south on County Road, #6), (519) 352-4799. The Museum is open May 1- Sept. 20, Wed.-Sun. 1-4:30 p.m., admission.
Contact:Keith Kaminski, MDCVB, (313) 259-4333
