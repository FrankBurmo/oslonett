NIGHT MOVES

You don't have to stop just because the sun's gone down. There's still plenty of action in local pubs and clubs.
By Mary O'Neill

Come nightfall, there's no lack of diversions in the Detroit area. From the high-profile glitz of downtown's Fox Theatre to Pontiac's multi-level dance house Industry to the rocking neighborhood bar Honest ? John's, the Motor City's always had its night life engines running on high octane.

Decisions, decisions. Take a gander at the following list of hot spots (each is geographically listed; refer to the map in this guide for location) and cross your fingers you'll be able to make up your mind. We know it won't be easy.

DOWNTOWN

A trip to Detroit without a stop at the Rhinoceros (265 Riopelle; 313/259-2208) would be like going to Paris and skipping the Eiffel Tower. Housed in a circa 1800 building that was home to one of Detroit's first saloons, the Rhino offers a full menu as well as some of the best jazz, top 40 and blues acts in the city.

Baker's Keyboard Lounge (20510 Livernois; 313/345-6300) is the quintessential Detroit jazz bar. Virtually every name in the biz has played here -- Sarah Vaughan, Sonny Stitt and Dexter Gordon, to name a few.

Stop by to see Kate Patterson in the third floor lounge at The Whitney (4421 Woodward; 313/832-5700). You'll say "Ah, Chanteuse!" as you listen to Detroit's most renowned songstress perform a Cole Porter-Irving Berlin-cabaret-Broadway repertoire.

For a romantic place to woo a date after dinner, try Club Penta (3011 W. Grand Blvd., Suite 15, in the Fisher Building; 313/972-3760). There's live jazz most nights, and you can catch the sounds of the big band era on the last Monday of each month with Bob Hopkins' 18-piece Jazz and Blues Orchestra.

Part Caesar's Palace, part Carnegie Hall, part movie house, part Broadway theater, the Fox (2211 Woodward; 313/396-7600) is the Big Daddy of Woodward's entertainment corridor. The restored Depression-era movie house has welcomed everything from ballets to plays to classic films.

As long as you're in the neighborhood, zip into the Elwood Bar & Grill (2100 Woodward; 313/961-7486). The refurbished art deco bar is a hot spot for Fox Theatre-goers and ice cream drink-lovers.

They come from miles for the bread pudding at Fishbones Rhythm Kitchen Cafe (400 Monroe, Greektown; 313/965-4600). Got a hankering for alligator? Satisfy it here while bopping to the beat of the Zydeco bands that stop in to play on their way through town.

Looking for a truly local Detroit hangout? Check out Honest ? John's Bar and No Grille (416 Field; 313/824-1243). Sunday nights, there's often a blues jam session.

Looking for something different? St. Andrew's Hall (431 E. Congress; 313/961-6358) is the tour stop for national "alternative" (read loud) bands. Fashion tip: Wear black, look bored. Downstairs, dance to house and rave music at The Shelter. Don't wear your Ralph Lauren -- they'll laugh you out the door.

Detroit's least predictable venue is the State Theatre (2115 Woodward; 313/961-5450). Mondays are offbeat movie night; Saturdays it's a dance party or "Club X" hosted by Windsor's super-hip alternative radio station, CIMX-FM. During the week it's a crap shoot, ranging from live bands to theme evenings. Crowd? Post-pubescent.

The Majestic Theatre (4140 Woodward; 313/833-9700) draws national names -- Richard Thompson, John Mayall and Paul Weller -- for audiences not enticed by those on the pop charts. Dine on Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cuisine at the Majestic Cafe and then take in one of the reggae, blues, alternative, avant garde or rock 'n' roll concerts.

Looking for a pleasure dome? Try Legends (415 E. Congress; 313/961-5005), where four floors of fun await you: the Game Room Floor (pool tables, basketball hoop, video games, darts, pinball), Dance Floor (top 40, rock 'n' roll, oldies), Sports Bar Floor (15 TVs) and Banquet Room Floor (private parties, overflow).

History buffs take note: The building that's home to The Soup Kitchen (1585 Franklin; 313/259-2643) has a 150-year history as a bar. Its current incarnation calls itself "Detroit's Home of the Blues." The crowd, the best urban mix you'll find in any city, fills all three floors. Thursday nights, take in the big band sounds of the 12-piece 251 Orchestra.

Back in 1905, Euphrasie Beuslinck rightly figured her grocery store would bring in more cash as a bar. The Woodbridge Tavern (289 St. Aubin; 313/259-0578) is still going strong with granddaughter Marcia Cron at the helm. Rub elbows with a young, upscale crowd and dance to classic and modern rock 'n' roll.

WINDSOR

Above a pizzeria, Fanny's Tin Pan Alley (53 Pitt; 519/971-8822) attracts an eclectic crowd ranging from autoworkers to college professors. Hear live blues five nights a week in this old brick, high-ceilinged loft.

Can-Usa: Windsor's Original Home of the Blues (400 Tecumseh Road E.; 519/253-0488), Windsor's blues institution, has got a neighborhood bar feel but draws crowds from far and wide. See The Garfield Blues Band, Chicago Pete and the Jesse Grooms Band up close and personal.

A night at Bernie's World (671 Ouellette; 519/977-7449) is like being on spring break. There's an indoor volleyball court, Velcro wall jump and Beat the Clock-like games. Music varies between alternative and top 40.

Dance, dance, dance is the modus operandi at Vertigo (800 Wellington; 519/258-2582), where a college crowd from the University of Windsor congregates to shake their booties to international acts and deejays playing techno and alternative dance music.

There's a little bit of Vegas in Windsor at the Top Hat (73 University E.; 519/963-3742). No gambling, but fans of the Eddie Fisher/Charlie Callas style of entertainment will want to book a table at this popular supper club. After dinner, dance to the house band.

DEARBORN AREA

Country/Western fans are back in the line-dance saddle again at Confetti Lounge (3932 Pelham, Dearborn; 313/561-5354). Urban cowboys and cowgirls kick up their spurs to the band Southern Rein. Monday nights: line-dance lessons.

Goodtime Charlie's (7499 Greenfield, Detroit; 313/581-0944) creates a new bar genre with this alternative sports bar. The music is alternative, but the dart board is regulation. Open jam sessions on Wednesdays.

Looking for something other than the boozy bar scene but still want to boogie to the blues? Sidle up to the full espresso bar at the Madhatter Cafe (1024 Monroe, Dearborn; 313/274-000), order some dessert and dance to rock, jazz and the blues seven nights a week.

In the mood for git-down, foot-stomping blues? Stop off at Sully's (4758 Greenfield, Dearborn; 313/846-5377). Leon Russell, Dr. John, Carl Perkins, U2 and the Black Crowes have. This is one of the places in metro Detroit where national bands occasionally go to party.

The Moby Dick Lounge (5452 Schaefer, Dearborn; 313/581-3650) attracts the 21- to 71-year-old crowd. "Everybody likes the blues" -- and some patrons like the pool table and dart boards, too.

SUBURBAN NORTH

Metro Detroit's "Hard Rock Cafe," the Metropolitan Musicafe (326 W. Fourth, Royal Oak; 810/542-1990) is chock full of rock 'n' roll memorabilia. The twentysomething crowd dances to live jazz and reggae during the week and deejays on weekends, when it's push-the-fire-code-to-the-limits packed.

It's the Royal Oak Theater (W. 4th Street, Royal Oak; 810/546-7610), but they call it Fourth Street. You figure it out. Weekends, a deejay plays alternative dance music. During the week, it's a concert hall featuring the likes of Violent Femmes, Squeeze and N.K.O.T.B.

One of Detroit's first comedy clubs, Mark Ridley's Comedy Castle (269 E. Fourth Street, Royal Oak; 810/542-9900) regularly features the likes of Tim Allen, Dave Coulier, Haywood Banks and David Brenner. Monday nights see "Totally Unrehearsed Theatre," Ridley's homegrown Second City-ish improv troupe.

You'll find everything from '70s retro nights to new alternative dance music to jazz ensemble at Industry (15 S. Saginaw, Pontiac; 810/334-1999). It's loud, it's multilevel, it's industrial-looking. Detroit's version of a trendy New York nightclub, Industry has hosted such notables as The B52s and David Cassidy.

Don't want to smell like an ashtray after a night on the town? Old Woodward Grill (555 S. Woodward, Birmingham; 810/642-9400), where ciggies are taboo, is your weekend blues refuge. Or, join the healthy suburban crowd every Wednesday night with The Blues Disciples, a group of the area's best blues musicians featuring Thornetta Davis and Terry Thunder on vocals.

A jukebox pumps out the tunes, but the 110 different beers keep 'em stacked five deep at Gusoline Alley (309 S. Center, Royal Oak; 810/545-2235), a hole-in-the-wall bar in downtown Royal Oak. The epitome of the neighborhood bar, you're lucky if there's no line at the door and an empty seat inside.

If pool's your game, don't scratch Breakers Billiards (2727 Woodward, Royal Oak; 810/435-0030) and Fifth Avenue Billiards (215 W. 5th, Royal Oak; 810/542-9922) off your list of night spots. These are not your smoky, dingy pool halls. They're where the upscale suburban "hustlers" play.

At Diamonds & Spurs (25 S. Saginaw, Pontiac; 810/334-4409) cowboys don't get the blues. Voted the best country/western bar in metro Detroit, it boasts the largest hardwood dance floor in southeast Michigan (it looks like a corral). Dance to national acts such as Tracey Byrd and Neal McCoy. (Also, Eastside Diamonds & Spurs at 39980 Groesbeck in Clinton Township; 810/790-9084.)

Lounge on comfy couches in '90s coffeehouse fashion at Brazil (305 S. Main, Royal Oak; 810/399-7200). Have your fortune told by a psychic on Tuesday nights, or come for the live jazz jam sessions on Sundays. The atmosphere is social and relaxed, and there's always great people-watching.

WEST BY NORTHWEST

It used to be a goat farm, of all things, but now the only livestock at Mr. B's Farm (24555 Novi Road, Novi; 810/349-7038) is the two-legged kind. Live music ranges from rock to blues. Test your crowd-pleasing abilities Sundays on open mike night. Who knows? You might not be that baaaaaad.

Thirteen TVs, a 10-foot big screen TV, dart boards, pool table, pinball machine, video games, sports stuff on the walls -- Sports Edition in the Novi Hilton (2111 Haggerty Road, Novi; 810/349-4000) is the sports fan's sports bar. And if dancing is your sport, there's a lot of that going on weekends to live top-40 music.

SUBURBAN SOUTH

Now here's a real deal: On Wednesdays and Thursdays, there's no cover charge at Sisko's on the Boulevard (5855 Monroe at Van Born, Taylor; 313/278-5340). This neat little blues spot features talented Detroit acts such as the Chisel Brothers on weekends. And there's lots of hot blues jams. Music usually starts at 10 p.m.; food is served until 1 a.m.

Like its sister in Royal Oak, the friendy neighborhood eatery Metropolitan Musicafe (2917 Biddle, Wyandotte; 313/282-8900) doubles as a rock 'n' roll dance spot at night. Quirky rock memorabilia like Aretha Franklin's white lace dress and a guitar from Soundgarden hang from the walls. Listen to a live rock band every Thursday through Sunday in the lower level; a deejay spins hits on the main floor.

NEAR EAST

Reggae, jazz, big band, rock 'n' roll -- there's something for everybody at Lido on the Lake (24026 Jefferson, St. Clair Shores; 810/773-7770), where the sailing set rubs elbows with the locals. And all with a great view of Lake St. Clair.

Buster's (30935 Van Dyke, Mt. Clemens; 810/731-5755) is the "Cheers" of Mount Clemens. Regulars dance to live top 40 and classic rock on weekends, play pool, pinball and darts, watch the big screen TV, or fill up on the house's signature sandwich, the "Belly Buster."

At the Impound (17320 Harper, Detroit, 313/343-9881), a neighborhood bar for the alternative crowd, local bands with names liked "Beer Horse," "Trash Brats" and "Feisty Cadavers" play to the 66-person-capacity audience.

Drinks for a quarter? A pitcher of beer for $1? It's happening at the Ritz (17580 Frazho at Gratiot, Roseville; 810/778-8150), where national acts like David Lee Roth and the Toobs entertain huge crowds of rock 'n' roll lovers of every generation.

Maybe they should call it the "Blues Goose Inn," because that's the live-music specialty at the Blue Goose Inn (28911 Jefferson, St. Clair Shores; 810/294-0690). A diverse crowd packs into this neighborhood meeting place on weekends.

Mary O'Neill is a free-lance writer based in Birmingham.

CAUCUS CLUB, 150 W. Congress, in the Penobscot Building, Detroit; (313) 965-4970. This is a fine hideaway to listen to the enchanting ivories (Music: Wed-Fri evenings).

OPUS ONE, 565 E. Larned, Detroit; (313) 961-7766. If the sumptuous eats at Opus don't lull you into sensual bliss, relaxing tunes by its Russian pianist will. A snazzy marble bar is the perfect spot to listen up Tue-Sat evenings.

PINKEY'S BOULEVARD CLUB, 110 E. Grand Blvd. at Jefferson, Detroit; (313) 824-2820. This is a classic. And remember, players here believe music is not a spectator sport. Come and enjoy Fri-Sat 9 p.m. 'til after midnight.

THE RHINOCEROS, 265 Riopelle, Detroit; (313) 259-2208. You're never disappointed by the talent at this funky joint, which ranges from hot R&B combos to terrific soloists. Music nightly.

DEARBORN AREA

SUBURBAN NORTH
