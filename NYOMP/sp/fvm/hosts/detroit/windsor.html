WINDSOR

Background:
Just south of Detroit, Windsor is an important travel route for North America by rail, highway, air or waterway. Situated on an international border, the city is also on the Detroit River connecting Lakes Erie and St. Clair, the world's busiest inland international waterway.
Visitors may travel to Windsor by the Ambassador Bridge, the longest international suspension bridge in the world or the Windsor-Detroit Tunnel which leads into the heart of downtown Windsor with its hotels, fine restaurants and boutiques. To enter Canada, U.S. citizens need a proof of citizenship, either a birth certificate or a voter's registration card.
Windsor shines with interesting attractions that reflect the historical and cultural vitality of the city. Ambassador and Alexander Parks are a few of the many waterfront parks. Dieppe Gardens, in the heart of Windsor, affords the best view of the Detroit skyline. Coventry Garden provides North America's largest floating fountain, and more than 12,000 rose bushes flourish in Jackson Park. The Hiram Walker Historical Museum, Willistead Manor, and the Art Gallery of Windsor are also main attractions.

What's Special:
Shopping is a special adventure in Windsor. Some of Canada's most exciting boutiques are tucked away in restored Victorian houses, in sleek buildings along Ouellette Avenue and Pelissier Street and in quaint shopping plazas.

Extra Special:
For many African Americans, Windsor is a special historical gathering place. Windsor was the final stop on the Underground Railroad and many historical sites located throughout Essex County celebrate its historical significance.
