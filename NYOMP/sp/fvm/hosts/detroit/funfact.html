DETROIT FUN FACTS

Detroit is known for its contributions to the automotive industry. So, it probably comes as no surprise that Detroit installed the first traffic light (1915), the first mile of paved concrete road (1909) and the first urban freeway in the nation (1942).

But did you know that Detroit:


  • is home to the oldest state fair in the nation, the Michigan State Fair, first held in 1849
  • is the potato chip capital of the world
  • features the nation's oldest municipal aquarium, located on Belle Isle
  • has the only floating post office in the world, the J.W. Westcott II
  • is the softball capital of the world
  • hosted the first and largest ethnic festival series in the U.S., the Riverfront Festivals
  • is the only geographic place where Canada is south of the U.S.
  • is home to Eastern Market, the largest flower-bedding market in the world
  • is the home of the Motown sound, founded by Berry Gordy in 1959
  • has the largest single library in the state and the fifth largest library system in the country
  • is home to the largest producer of ball park franks
  • is where Ulysses, the scandalous book by James Joyce, first entered the U.S.
  • is home to the oldest rowing club in continuous existence in the world, the Detroit Boat Club on Belle Isle
  • shares the first auto traffic tunnel between two nations, The Detroit-Windsor Tunnel
  • is the home of the tallest hotel in North America, The Westin Hotel Renaissance Center Detroit
  • is one of the five largest ports in the country, carrying nearly 30 million tons of cargo each year.
  • has the largest annual fireworks display in the world
  • has the largest island park within any city -- Belle Isle Park
  • has the largest NAACP membership of any U.S. city
  • is home of the largest sit-down fundraising dinner of its kind in the U.S. -- the NAACP Freedom Fund Dinner, attracting 10,000 guests
  • is the true home of the "Boston Cooler"
  • is home of the typewriter, patented in 1829 by Detroiter William Austin Burt
  • ·developed the phrase "the real McCoy," based on Detroit inventor Elijah McCoy
  • boasts Woodward Avenue as the first paved concrete highway in the U.S.
  • is home to the largest free jazz festival in North America, the Montreux Detroit Jazz Festival
  • is the site of the country's first electrical power plant developed by Detroit Edison Company
  • has the most registered bowlers in the U.S.
  • was the first city in the nation to assign individual telephone numbers in 1879
  • founded the world's first convention bureau, the Metropolitan Detroit Convention & Visitors Bureau, in 1896
