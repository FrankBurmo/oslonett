DETROIT HISTORY

The following are key dates in Detroit's history:
1701- Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac landed on what is now the Civic Center in downtown Detroit and started a fur trading center.
1760- Detroit was turned over to the British as a spoil of the French and Indian War.
1796- George Washington forced the British out of the city, and the American flag was raised over Fort Pontchartrain at Detroit.

1805- A devastating fire swept Detroit and destroyed each one of its 200 structures, except a stone warehouse.
1812- War of 1812. With the lessening threat of Indian attack, the development of the steamboat and the opening of the Erie Canal, Detroit began to experience dramatic growth.
1815- Detroit was incorporated as a city.
1837- Michigan was admitted to the Union as the 26th state, and Detroit had become an important station on the Underground Railroad.
1850- The manufacturing of stove and kitchen ranges becomes Detroit's leading industry.
Industrial Age -Emergence of Vernors ginger ale, Stroh's beer, and Sanders candy, cake and ice cream.
1896- Henry Ford built his first car in Detroit, and the world's first convention and visitors bureau, the Metropolitan Detroit Convention & Visitors Bureau, was founded.

1901- The world's first concrete road was built in Detroit.
1903- Ford Motor Company was established.
1913- Henry Ford introduces the assembly line, revolutionizing the auto industry.
1920- The city's population was multiplying rapidly thanks to the auto industry with nearly two million, foreign-born residents.
1928/1929- Tunnel and bridge access to Windsor commences with the opening of the Detroit-Windsor Tunnel and the Ambassador bridge.
1931- Gar Wood won the international prize for unlimited powerboat racing.
1935- The Detroit Tigers captured the World Series title.
1935- The Detroit Lions were National Football League champions and the Red Wings won the Stanley Cup.
1937- The Detroit Red Wings won the Stanley Cup and Joe Louis Barrow, The Brown Bomber, won the world's heavyweight boxing championship.
1940- Detroit plays key role in WWII victory as the nation's "Arsenal of Democracy."
1951- The city celebrated its 250th anniversary on a grand scale.
1950- Detroit began to construct its massive system of freeways and the city's population shifted to a metropolitan one.
1954- The nation's first shopping mall opened in Southfield; Northland Mall was the harbinger of a new suburban life style.
1960- Cobo Hall, Detroit's major convention center, opened.
1963- The Rev. Martin Luther King debuts his "I Have a Dream" speech in Detroit approximately two months before his famous delivery in Washington, D.C.
1967- New Detroit was founded as the United States' first "urban coalition," organized to improve education, employment, housing and economic development in the city, following the city's major civil disturbance.
1970- Detroit Renaissance, a group of business leaders, was founded to help formulate Detroit's future.
1971- Henry Ford II, head of Detroit Renaissance, Inc., announced plans for the construction of the largest privately-financed project in the world -- the Renaissance Center.
1974- Coleman Young took office as Detroit's first black mayor.
1977- The Renaissance Center opened as a symbol of Detroit's rebirth.

1980- Detroit hosted the 32nd Republican National Convention at the new Joe Louis Arena.
1982- The first Detroit Grand Prix was held on city streets, and Super Bowl XVI was held at the Pontiac Silverdome.
1984- The Detroit Tigers captured the World Series title.
1985- The Millender Center and Greektown's Trappers Alley Festival Marketplace opened.
1987- The Detroit People Mover opened, providing a new source of downtown transportation.
1989- $225 million Cobo Conference/Exhibition Center expansion completed.
1990- The Detroit Pistons capture their second consecutive NBA world championship title.
1992- Detroit was chosen as a site for World Cup Soccer 1994, and the Pontiac Silverdome earned the unique opportunity to host the first indoor soccer championship in World Cup history.
