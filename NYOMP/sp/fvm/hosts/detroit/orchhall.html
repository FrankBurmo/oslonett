ORCHESTRA HALL
(FORMER PARADISE THEATRE)

Background:
Dedicated in 1919, Orchestra Hall formerly served as the home of the Detroit Symphony Orchestra. In 1939, when the orchestra moved to the Masonic Temple Theatre, the facility was occupied by the Paradise Theatre, one of the nation's most famous stages for African-American musicians.
In the late 1950s, the building was abandoned and had fallen into disrepair. In 1964, it was headed for the wrecking ball, but local citizens rallied to save the great concert hall. These volunteers founded Save Orchestra Hall, Inc., to marshall citizen support for the retention and restoration of the building to its former architectural grandeur.
Orchestra Hall is once again the permanent home of the Detroit Symphony Orchestra. In the summer of 1993, phase II was completed, capping a multi-million dollar restoration effort.
Phase I of the project was completed with restoration work valued at more than $5.3 million. The work included restoration of the original box seating, reconfiguration of the box office, completion of the decorative painting of the outer ticket and inner oval lobbies, installation of new stage and aisle lighting and railings, improvements to the backstage and orchestra pit areas, and expansion of the restroom facilities. Phase II was valued at $1.5 million, bringing the total renovation cost to $6.8 million.

What's Special:
The Detroit Symphony Orchestra reaches a national weekly radio audience of 80,000 listeners, and is seen by more than 437,000 people annually.

Details:
Detroit Symphony Orchestra Hall is located on Woodward Avenue at Parsons Street, south of Detroit's Cultural Center. The executive offices are located in the Buhl Building, 535 Griswold St., Detroit, MI 48226.
