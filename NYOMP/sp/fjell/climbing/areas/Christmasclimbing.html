For Southern France it all depends on the wheather:
The Mediterranean coast between Marseille (Calanques) and Nice/Monaco will probably be the best idea; if the sun is shining, it's perfect for climbing, but I also experienced a number of cool, cloudy days (not too cold for climbing though, only difficult to get out of the tent in the morning!. raining rarely.

This is it for Southern France. A contribution which I didn't want to withheld for the public is a suggestion from Simon Walsh, sugest El Choro in Spain; He writes:
There is no doubt that one of the finest winter climbing areas in southern Europe has to be El Chorro. El Chorro town at the entrance to a river gorge situated approximately 40mls (60kms) north of Malaga.
The village is accessable either by train or car but what makes it a gem is the fact that you do not need to hire a car or even drive down, as a cheap flight to Malaga and then, using the railway terminal at the airport a simple journey to Malaga railway station then only requires a 360pts ticket to take you straight to the gorge where it stops at El Chorro. Easy.
Accomodation consists of either the hotel (15 per night), cottages run by a friendly english couple(30 week each with four per cottage), or camping which is quite rough and facilities sparse but may have improved since I was there in March 94.
There are a couple of shops in the village for food but a better plan is to stock up one stop down the line. Hint-study the timetable (available at Malaga railway station).
The climbs are all easily accessable via walking the railway line which runs through the entire gorge. This is safe because of the width of the line bed if somewhat noisy in the short tunnels to be negotiated.
What ever grade you climb there is something for everyone from 5+ to 8c (French). All routes all bolted but a rack of nuts will come in handy for some of the longer routes.
