A splendid week in El Chorro, Spain
-----------------------------------

It all started six weeks ago when my cousin Eyvind called me up one
day: "We're planning a trip to El Chorro in southern Spain. Do you
want to come along?" My immediate reaction was to say a loud "yes",
but as a typical slow and dull Norwegian I had to "think about it" for
a couple of days.

I first tried climbing some years ago, my cousin mentioned above
brought me into it. However, it never became more advanced than some
shaky top roping a few times. But one and a half year ago I decided to
climb at a more regular basis - joined a short course and got together
with a couple of friends at the same level. So since then I've climbed
quite often working my way through the grades (well, at least the few
easy ones). Mainly top roping unfortunately - tried leading a couple
of times but got psyched when things became difficult. I am fully
aware of my weaknesses, and was really excited about how this trip
would turn out.  I knew I would have to climb better than before in
order to just get on the rock - the amount of really simple routes is
limited.

We were four friends going together: Eyvind mentioned above, a very
capable and experienced climber who have climbed a lot of places in
Europe as well as US. Sverre, a young and very good sport climber -
technically briliant, the best of the four of us. Trygve, has been
climbing for a few years, technically about my own level but more
experienced and done more leading. And finally myself, climbing for
the fun, but with increasing ambitions...

After the trip was decided, we started a heavy indoor training period
as the outdoor season was just sunny memories here in the middle of
chilly Norway.  The benefit from indoor bouldering may be questionable
in some cases, but for me the advantages were obvious. The big
question was how things would be when I got outdoor on the crag with
the last bolt far below me - I was really excited and had climbing
dreams every night (no nightmares fortunately).

The big day arrived, and after a long journey on train and plane, we
arrived in Malaga, got our rented car and headed inland for El Chorro.
We arrived in the darkness, and after a long discussion with some
local folks at the bar (them speaking only Spanish, we tried a mix of
English and a few French words with an artificial Spanish accent -
well, it worked) we ended up at Finca La Campana where Mary and John
Mills, a nice English couple, have small appartments for rent - highly
recommended.  We were finally there, and we impatiently waited for the
morning sun to reveal the promised limestone walls.  We got up early
and stood for a long time admiring the amazing cliffs.  After a quick
breakfast we were off to the first crag.

El Chorro is the name of the small town, the climbing being partly in
the canyon of the river passing through. The canyon is narrow with two
gorgeous gorges (pun intended), there are about 400 routes at about 25
main locations - all limestone. Most of the routes are bolted, only a
set of quickdraws is needed for protection. Mostly single pitch
routes, but a few long multi pitch routes as well. Some of the single
pitches are long, so a 60m rope is recommended.

Through the canyon there is a railroad on one side which must be
followed in order to get to some of the climbs. There are several
tunnels, but they are spaceous, so there is no problem meeting a train
in them (bloody noisy though). On the other side of the canyon there
is a crazy walkway construction built directly on the vertical rock;
it's really narrow and not well maintained, so it lacks banisters(?),
often on the most airy spots. A large backpack is not a good choice
here. A couple of places the walkway itself is gone and you only have
a two inch rail to walk on with a thin wire to hold on. After such an
approach starting climbing feels safe...

After walking along the railroad for a short distance, we reached our
first choice - El Polvorin, a steep three star crag. Suddenly I found
myself wearing harness and shoes, tied in and ready to go. After a few
moments of nervous hesitation I realized I just had to jump into it.
The first few moves: heavy, small handholds - and where the heck are
the footholds? I was desparately grasping the rock, elegant as a horny
hippopotamus fighting my way upwards. I looked at the top anchor
slowly approaching; then a couple of good handholds - I started to
like this. "Click", my crab hooked into the top bolt and I had done
it: My first limestone climb, leading, an onsight flash 6a (all routes
are graded according to the French system). I was really happy when I
was lowered down, not because it was over, rather because it had just
begun. A large step for me, an ignorable step for humanity...

After a few more climbes at El Polvorin we went to another crag: El
Invento, not far from El Polvorin. However, the quality of the rock
and routes was much lower, so it was a bit disappointing (this point
of view is subjective and may be connected to the fact that I screwed
up some easy climbing)... Anyway, the first day was a success, and the
evening was spent cooking a good dinner, drinking wine and playing
cards.

Sunday morning we headed for a place called El Recodo which is located
in the lower part of the gorge - on the walkway side. Thus, we
carefully and gently moved along the walkway slightly leaning towards
the rock at the most scary places. But I was afraid of bumping into the
rock too, I felt like a ball in a flipper game - the rock would
probably just kick me into the gorge if I touched it, I thought.
Completely ridiculous of course. Anyway, we reached El Recodo and
rapped off as the clibs we were going for were under the walkway. The
rock was steep and good with rather long routes. From here we also got
a good view of the Hari Kiri - an insane 8c+ which looked smooth as an
egg and overhanging (no, I did not try that one:-). After climbing a
few routes we went back along the walkway, we were getting a bit more
used to it now and walked quite normally.

We went on climbing every day Monday to Thursday visiting many good
places in the area. Two particularly magnificent crags are El
Makinodromo and Poema Roca. Makinodromo is a huge overhang with some
really hard routes from 8a to 8c+. Impressive - imagine 30m of 8c+!
However, just to the right of the overhang are a couple of routes on
vertical rock. The first one, a 6b+, is about 20m and provides some
funny and pleasant climbing. There are large holes in the rock, some
so big that you can stand or sit comfortably. Other holes are smaller,
btw one had a pidgeon's nest in it (but only the nest fortunately).
Just to the right is a 6c, 30m+. This one is great! the start is up
along a stalactite(sp?) - spectacular climbing! The holds were good,
but required a style somewhat different from what I'm used to. I did
just fine upwards and really enjoyed the climb until I reached one of
the last bolts. Steep rock and small holds - I hesitated a couple of
seconds to decide wheter to clip the next bolt from where I was or
stretch (loooong) for the next apparently good hold. The hesitation
was too long, so I found myself unable to do anything. Tried to
downclimb in order to shorten the fall - then some loud &Y#%#)*&/%
(heavy swearing in Norwegian). But it was no problem - the fall was
short and the rock steep/overhanging and smooth. I knew what I had
done wrong, so after a short rest I continued upwards.  Placed my feet
differently and comfortably clipped the bolt. The rest was fairly
simple climbing on good holds.

Makindromo is a good crag even if it rains. It actually rained the day
we were there - the rain in Spain evidently does not only fall gently
on the plain...

The other crag I mentioned, Poema Roca, provides various kinds of
climbing. The route called Poema Roca has six pitches of quite heavy
climbing. The first pitch is a overhanging 7a starting at the bottom
of a large oval cave. The first part of the pitch follows several
stalactites - incredibly funny. Some of the stalactites are so large
that you can squeeze your arms and feet around them and just sit
there. I toproped this one as I knew it was beyond my capabilities
anyway.  The pitch is long - almost 30m - so I got really pumped at
the last part and had to hang for a couple of rests. Eventually I
reached the top and could try the belay seat mounted there. Airy! To
the right of this cave are three easier routes: 6a+, 6b and 6c. I
really enjoyed them all.

The good times don't last forever, do they? Our flight back to Norway
would leave on Friday, so Thursday evening we went for Malaga to spend
the night there. Friday morning we went to the beach for breakfast and
a short swim (cold!). We had got a topo of a small crag just to the
east of Malaga - I think it was called Cala del Moral. So after
breakfast we went there to really do the last efforts. The crag
appeared to be good, but we started to get pretty exhausted after one
week of climbing. However, the last route I tried was a 6c+ and I
flashed it! Man, I was so happy, a typical happy ending. 

A couple of hours later we were on the plane back home...
