dd 17 Februari 1995

I've just returned from El Chorro and would like to warn anyone planning a trip that all camping in the area has been banned! We planned a cheap two week camping holiday and ended up having a nice, but relatively more expensive trip. The status of things as of Sunday (12/2/95) is this:

There is no camping permitted in the immediate area and this seems to be rigorously enforced. We met people who were caught and had to pay a #75.00 fine. I got the feeling that this was mainly directed at tourists as we saw large groups of Spanish students/climbers walking in with water and large packs - presumably they know where to hide or are not bothered. There are a few caves on the hillside and these appeared to be populated by climbers.

There is camping further up the gorge (~9 km) near the lakes. If you have a car then camping here becomes a possibility. The fee is 400 pts/person and 300 pts/car. They have showers which are supposed to be hot, but they weren't when we were there.

In the area of the village there are several other options:

1) The Refugio de Escalandes 500pts/head/night. This is a very grim bunk house with a bar and and area for cooking. Not very pleasant, but your cheapest option in the area.

2) The Bells (a British couple) have some cottages and a small (12 beds) bunkhouse which is much more pleasant but also more expensive (The bunk house is 1200 pts/head/night - I don't know what the cottages cost but they can be booked in advance (see the guide) - probably a good idea as they seem to be full most of the time). The place is called Finca la Campana and is near Encantadas - a short walk from the station (uphill). Mrs. Bell also runs a small shop (mainly food items) but one thing worth noting is that she sells Epigas 500 canisters for 750 pts. The only other place you can get them is in Malaga (650 pts). You will not find them in Alora - the nearest town. The rumour that there is camping at the Bell's place is not true, as they were denied the license.

3) Apartments are also available at the Gargantua - a hotel/restaurant just across from the station. The price here is negotiable and isn't necessarily related to apartment size - the weather and how much they like you probably figure more highly in cost calculation. We spent several days here and paid 3400/night for two. Our apartment was very large (2br, livrm,kit, bath), clean and had plenty of hot water. We met people who were paying the same price for a bedsit! Brush up on your Spanish and you'll probably get a better deal!

4) One last suggestion - ONLY if you have a car is to stay in Alora. The price for a room in the center of town is comparable to those at the Apartments in El Chorro (I don't know about prices further outside the center). Of course you won't have cooking facilities and will be a bit isolated from other climbers. Alora is 12 km from El Chorro but remember you'll need a car if you plan to stay there as there are no EARLY trains to El Chorro. The town itself is lovely and certainly worth a visit on a rest/shopping day. There are several supermercatos and the prices are the same as in Malaga.

The current exchange rate is ~200 pts/#.

Enjoy your trip!

One more thing, if you have a car and plan a day or two in El Torcal, you may be able to camp. Officially there is no camping and if you ask they will tell you it is not allowed, BUT if you don't ask and just set your tent up in the area near the carpark (described in the guide) they probably won't bother you. We spent three days there and were never approached.
