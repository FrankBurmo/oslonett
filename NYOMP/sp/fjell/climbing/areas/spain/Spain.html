Clavijo

Location:
Clavijo, a very little village in La Rioja (Northern Spain). The easiest way to find it, is by starting from Logrono (La Rioja's capital), it's about 20 km far. A bus can take you to Albarique (9 km from Clavijo). The best way is going by car. The rock can be seen from Logrono, it has a castle on the top. Beautiful.
Kind of Rock:
Conglomeraat.
Height:
14-45 m, most of them around 25m.
Season:
I was there in Summer, but because of the wind, even then it was very cold some days, so I wouldn't go in Winter (if it's no wind it's a very hot place).
Bolted:
well protected.
Camping sites:
In Logrono there's a camping site, but you could camp in Clavijo (I don't know if it's allowed, but I've seen doing it).
Topo:
Avaible in Sherpa (the climbing club of Logrono)
Grades:
From 6a to 8b. There are about 35 routes, most of them in the range of 7.
Interesting Routes:
?
Other comments:
There are falcons flying around the castle. When we were climbing, a patrol of the Guardia Civil came. I thought it could be forbidden to climb in that season, but they were climbers too, and they just watched and chatted.

El chorro

Location:
Southern Spain, 60kms northof Malaga.
Rock:
limestone of the very best quality.
How to reach it:
The village is accessable either by train or car but what makes it a gem is the fact that you do not need to hire a car or even drive down, as a cheap flight to Malaga and then, using the railway terminal at the airport a simple journey to Malaga railway station then only requires a 350pts ticket to take you straight to the gorge where it stops at El Chorro. Easy.
Acces:
The climbs are all easily accessable via walking the railway line which runs through the entire gorge. This is safe because of the width of the line bed if somewhat noisy in the short tunnels to be negotiated.
Routes:
Routes to go for are Zeppelin and Africa.(both 6c+)
Area:
extremely beautiful.
Weather:
extremely hot, but at night it can be cold though.
Camping sites:
There are several places to stay. The bar Gargantua in the village costs about 1000pts a night. The Spanish bunkhouse up the road is about the same price but has a campsite (well its really a field of almost biblical stoniness). Best option is Finca la Campana, previously run by John and Mary Bell, now taken over by a Swiss couple named Jean-Bernard and Blancha Hofer. They bought the place from John and Mary Bell in May last year (1995).
A recent complained about camping can be found here.
Accomodation consists of either the hotel (15 per night), cottages run by a friendly english couple(30 week each with four per cottage), or camping which is quite rough and facilities sparse but may have improved since March 94.
Grades:
What ever grade you climb there is something for everyone from 5+ to 8c (French).
Bolts:
All routes all bolted but a rack of nuts will come in handy for some of the longer routes.
Topo:
Best option is Finca la Campana run by John and Mary Bell. They have a small shop and sell rock boots and guidebooks.
Story:
A climbing
story of El Choro, by Sivert Sorumgard.

In Etxauri, a little village near Pamplona (13 Km)
Rock:
limestone
Season:
All the year, but hot in summer, although there are shadow sectors.
Routes:
Lots of them
Grades:
from IV to 8c?
Bolts:
very good
Topo:
A very good guide published by SUA edizioak writen by C.Velazquez and M.Berazaluce
Comments:
You're supposed to climb 'El huso' a beautiful needle not very high (about 35 m.) For the most fanatics climbers there a quite lot of routes above the 8th degree. But, whatever your degree is, I can say you will find the appropriate routes for you. Notice there are about 350 routes in a beautiful place where you can enjoy the company of the vultures showing its powerful glide. Once your feet on the floor you can enjoy one of the most friendly and noble region in Spain and in Europe because of the people, the food, the wine and a many more things. If you decide travel to this wonderful area remember that in July remember you can enjoy one of the best fests in the world: San Fermin (6-14 july) famous for the runningbulls, the weather, the friendly spirit and the atmosphere. Siurana

Location:
Northern part of Spain,near Taragona.
Rock:
Limestone.
Height:
More than 100m, though the main areas are up to 25 m.
Routes:
The routes are high in grades and 6a or more difficult. All the routes are bolted very well.
Area:
Extremely beautiful in a medieval village on a mountaintop.
Weather:
In Summer very hot, in winter very cold. But there is always rock in the sun and in the shadow.
Camping sites:
You can camp in the village or on the parking. For free. There are toilets and water. No shopping in the village.
Topo:
Here is a topo available in the bar in Siurana. (not for sale) I bought my topo in Barcelona.
Comments:
This is one of the main climbing-area's in Spain.

Riglos

Location:
Near Jacca, North-west of Spain.
Rock:
Conglomeraat, Sandstone with pebbles in it.
Height:
Upto 200 m!
Routes:
There are a lot of easy (long) routes and also more difficult ones.
Bolts:
The easy and longer routes are reasonable protected, the more difficult routes are over-protected. Bring more than 14 quickdraws!
Weather:
I was there in Summer and it was very hot. Probably climbing is possible all through the year, but cold in winter.
Camping sites:
Not allowed in the village; there is a camping area in next village.
Topo:
Available in the bar (not for sale). I saw a vertical-topo with the twenty best spots in Spain and Riglos is included.
