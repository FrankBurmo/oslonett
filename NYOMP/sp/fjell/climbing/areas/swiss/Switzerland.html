Rock Climbing Areas In Switzerland

Saleve

Location:
The Saleve is about an hour from Chamonix and, if the weather is unstable, not a bad choice since, if it does rain, there's more (marginally) to do in Geneva than in Chamonix.
Near Geneva, but in France. The departure point for most climbs is the very friendly bar/resto known as the "Refuge du Saleve". Incidentally, patronize these guys - prices are reasonable, they are funny and helpful. They have lent us water bottles (rather than making us buy a drink), even a motorcycle helmet when some asshole stole Sissi's.
To get there, from Geneva or Annemasse, go to Collonges-sous-Saleve, stay left through town and keep going up the hill until you arrive at the tennis courts on the left. Just after the courts is the parking lot for the majority of the climbs.
By public transit take the H bus to the end and then walk (about 30-40 minutes). Hitching is best done from the traffic circle in Carouge (rondeau de Carouge). Sign essential. Write: Saleve (Coin), so people will know you aren't going to the summit. From Carouge, about 30 minutes by bike.
Gear and info:
Rock:
The best place to find gear is Cactus Sports in Carouge (Geneva), right on the #12 tram line at the stop Place des Armes (where the street is closed to cars). The address is: 
          4, rue du Pont-Neuf
          CH-1227 CAROUGE
          (22) 300 30 01

     The official hours are:
          Monday, Tuesday, Thursday: 9:00 to 19:00, non-stop.
          Wednesday, Friday: 8:00 to 12:00

Unless it is just my imagination, the store is often open wed. and fri. afternoons. The owner, Bernard, is also author of the guidebook to the Saleve. He has travelled a lot to the States and carries a lot of American gear. If you need a replacement pick for your BD axe, this is the place. I imagine he also speaks English pretty well. The guidebook is, of course, available here: LE GUIDE DU SALEVE, by Bernard WIETLISBACH, 1990 (about 20.CHF)

Routes:
The Saleve has a total of 450 routes, so here is only a small portion, in general routes rated 6a to 6c that in my opinion, are worth the effort.
Area:
Weather:
Summer days can be hot and humid, in the fall it rains a lot, and in the winter some climbs occasionally have some ice on them. In general the Saleve doesn't get much snow (altitude 650 meters at the foot of the climbs, 1210m at the summit), but winter not the season to visit. Summer afternoons and mornings are generally agreeable.
Camping sites:
There is a campground open at the last hairpin before reaching the refuge du Saleve. In addition, the Saleve abounds in good bivvy sites - lots of caves and so on. Get water at the spigot near the refuge.
Topo:
LE GUIDE DU SALEVE, by Bernard WIETLISBACH, 1990 (about 20.CHF)
Comments:
A few suggestions on climbs in the 6a - 6c range that are worth doing.
