Useful Information

Passports and visas

All travellers, except citizens of Denmark, Finland, Iceland and Sweden, must hold a valid passport. Visas to Norway are not required by visitors from:

Andorra, Argentina, Australia, Austria, Bahamas, Barbados, Belgium, Belize, Benin, Bermuda, Bolivia, Botswana, Brazil, Bulgaria, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cyprus, the Dominican Republic, Cuba, Ecuador, Eire, Fiji, France, Great Britain, Greece, Grenada, Guatemala, Guyana, Haiti, Honduras, Israel, Italy, Ivory Coast, Jamaica, Japan, Kenya, Korea, Croatia, Lesotho, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Malawi, Federation of Malaya, Mauritius, Mexico, Monaco, Morocco, Netherlands, Nicaragua, New Zealand, Niger, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, the Philippines, Portugal, Romania, El Salvador, San Marino, Sierra Leone, Singapore, Spain, Switzerland, Swaziland, Tanzania, Thailand, Tobago, Trinidad, Tunisia, Uganda, Uruguay, USA, Venezuela, Germany, Zambia.

Nor are visas required by holders of passports which are endorsed as a "British Passport"; on the front cover, and where the holder is described as "British Subject". Visitors from all other countries must obtain visas from the nearest Norwegian Consulate.

All foreign visitors (except visitors from Denmark, Finland, Iceland or Sweden) wishing to stay in Norway for more than three months, or intend to work during their visit, need a visa.

Customs

Upon arrival in Norway you must declare all goods in your possession. Someplaces of entry have a system of red and green areas for use in customs control. If you have nothing to declare, go through the green area. If you are in doubt, go to the red zone.

Customs restrictions for visitors to Norway are usually reduced to a minimum when articles imported are for personal use.

Persons over 20 years of age may bring 1 litre of wine or 1 litre of fortified wine and 1 litre of hard liquor or 2 litres of wine. In addition you may bring 2 litres of beer. European residents over 16 years may bring 200 cigarettes or 250 grams of other tobacco goods. Persons resident outside Europe, aged 16 years or more, may bring 400 cigarettes or 500 grams of other tobacco goods.

Chocolate and sweets up to 1 kg may be brought in duty free, and goods other than mentioned above up to a total value of NOK 1200. In addition to the tax free quote you may bring in 4 litres of wine or liquor against payment of tax. The import of narcotics, medicines and poisons, firearms, ammunition and explosives is prohibited.

Rabies

Prospective importers of dogs and cats must apply to the Division of Veterinary Services, Ministry of Agriculture for import permits. The requirements for bringing live animals into Norway depend upon whether or not the traveller comes from a country considered to be rabies-free or -infected. Animals from rabies-infected countries are required on arrival to spend 4 months in quarantine at the State Animal Quarantine Station in Oslo at the owner's expense. No exemptions are granted from the quarantine requirements even if the animal has been vaccinated against rabies. Further requirements for the importation's of animals are specified in the import permit. The importer himself is required to reserve quarantine accommodation, at the following address: Statens karantenestasjon for dyr (The State Animal Quarantine Station), Verkseier Furulundsv. 44, N-0668 Oslo, tel.: +(47) 22 30 05 06. Further requirements will be specified in the import permit. (Illegal import of live animals stands the risk of prosecution according to Norwegian law.)

Time

Time in Norway is one hour ahead of Greenwich Mean Time (+1GMT).

Norway has daylight saving time from the last Sunday in March until the last Sunday in September.

Climate

Thanks to the gentle currents from the Gulf of Mexico which follow Norway's coastline, and penetrate the waters of every fjord, Norway can boast a good climate.

The temperature averages about 20 Centigrade (about 45 Fahrenheit), warmer than in other areas situated at the same latitude.

Spring, summer and autumn temperatures may be compared to those in the New England States in the USA, Brittany in France, Galicia in Spain or Scotland. During the winter months, mercury seldom drops below -10 Centigrade (about 14 Fahrenheit) in Oslo. The city lies sheltered by hills and forests, enjoying a dry, inland climate. This means below zero temperatures are not as noticeable as they would be in cities with a high humidity. Snow-fun without feeling uncomfortable may therefore be arranged in Oslo.

What to wear

Besides a normal city wardrobe, a rain coat, light sweaters and sturdy walking shoes are recommended. For a winter visit you should wear a warm coat, woollen, warm underwear, lined boots and gloves. If you plan to participate in outdoor activities, you should bring casual sportswear to suit the different seasons. Individual styles are also respected in modern Norway, where informality is part of the way of life. During dinnertime, some restaurants will not admit persons wearing blue-jeans.

Tipping

In restaurants, hotels, taxis, at the hair-dresser etc. the service charge is included in the tariff. It is not necessary to add a tip, though you may do so at your own discretion to indicate appreciation of good service. At restaurants, diners often pay to the nearest round figure.

Alcohol restrictions

Wine and spirits can only be purchased in shops of the State Monopoly, called Vinmonopolet. The age limits for purchasing wine/spirits are 18/20 years. Beer can be bought in ordinary grocery-stores, the age limit being 18 years Beer in class 3 only at the Vinmonopolet. It is prohibited to consume alcohol in public areas, s.a. parks, streets etc.

Medical service

The national Health Plan of Norway only covers permanent residents of the country, and nationals of other Nordic countries with whom Norway has a mutual agreement. All other nationals are therefore recommended to arrange for a travel sickness- and accident insurance before leaving home. Oslo has an emergency hospital offering treatment to visitors from abroad, with doctors on duty around the clock.

Vaccination

There are no vaccinations required for entry into Norway.

Holidays in Norway

Banks, stores and offices are closed on official holidays. Permanent holidays are: New Year's Day, May 1st (Labour Day), May 17th (National Holiday), Christmas Day and Boxing Day (Des. 26th). Other public Holidays in Norway are: Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Ascension Day, Whit Monday.
