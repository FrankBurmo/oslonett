The Peace City

Oslo - the city which advances world peace

Oslo was the site of the peace negotiations between Israel and the PLO in 1993. Here the parties found both the right athmosphere and the right intermediaries to conclude this historical peace treaty.

The Norwegian Nobel Institute is located near the Royal Palace, in a building erected in 1867 and remodelled in 1903-05 by the architects Berner&Berner.

The Nobel Foundation was establish ed by the Swedish inventor and industrialist Alfred Bernhard Nobel (1833-1896). In his last will, dated 1895, Mr Nobel determinded that the annual proceeds from his estate of SEK 30 million should be divided into five equal parts, and the money should be given as a prize to persons who had contributed the most to mankind in the past year. The Peace Prize should be awarded by Norway, and the four other prizes by his native Sweden. The Peace Prize for 1994 was SEK 7 million.

The Nobel Institute in Oslo is the home of the Norwegian Nobel Committee, the body which selects the Peace Prize winner. This committee is elected by the Norwegian "Storting" (National Assembly). The Nobel Committee selects the Peace Prize winner based on the criteria set forth in Nobe l's will: "the person(s) who shall have done the most or the best work for fraternity between nations, for the abolition or reduction of standing armies and for the holding and promotion of peace congresses".

The Peace Prize ceremony took place in the Nobel Institute from 1905 through 1946 (only interrupted by the two World Wars). From 1947, the Oslo University Aula was used, and from 1990, the event has taken place in the Oslo City Hall. The ceremony takes place each year on Deember 10, which was the d ate when Alfred Nobel died in San Remo, Italy.

The Nobel Institute has a very extensive library (appx 155.000 books) which focus on international peace research, international economics, human rights, political science and modern political history. The library is open to the public.
