Shopping Areas

Many of the city's best stores are situated in the area around the Cathedral and up towards the Parliament (Stortinget). Hegdehaugsveien and Bogstadveien are also known for a great variety of shops. Along these streets which run from the upper part of the Royal Palace park to Majorstuen, you will find many small boutiques and some larger department stores.

There is no fish-market in Oslo, but Aker Brygge (across the City Hall, on the waterfront and next to the Norway Information centre) has a maritime atmosphere.

Aker Brygge is a modern shopping and restaurant centre, where several outdoor and indoor activities takes place. The shops are open till 8 p.m. (6 p.m. Saturdays)

New Oslo at Vaterland, east of centre, is Oslo's new shopping district. Here you can find Oslo City, Norway's biggest shopping and business centre. The Centre opened in November l988 and comprises approx. 100 shops and restaurants. Opening hours are from 9.00-5.00 p.m., but this can vary somewhat. Thursday is late shopping day for most shops which then close at 7.00 p.m. hrs., Saturday 09.00-02.00 p.m. Every first Saturday of the month is called "Super-Saturday". The shops in the city centre extend their opening hours and give special offers. Extended opening hours and special offers create a special atmosphere often related to the season. Some of the large stores also have late opening on Fridays. During the school summer holidays, opening hours are often shorter.

Persons residing outside the Nordic countries, making purchases totalling more than NOK 300 in one store, are entitled to a Value Added Tax refund upon their departure from Norway. 450 stores in Oslo offer special Tax-free service. Ask for a tax-free cheque when making your purchases. VAT refunds are made at airports, frontiers and on ferries on presentation of Tax-free cheque, passport and goods. Look for the "Tax-free" representative (not customs).
