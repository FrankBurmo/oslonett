MEADOW BROOK HALL

Background:
Constructed over a period of three years (1926-1929), the $4 million Tudor-style mansion is considered one of the finest examples of residential architecture anywhere in the world. Auto Baron John Dodge is credited with the building of the 100-room mansion, although the home was lived in by his wife Matilda Dodge Wilson and her new husband Alfred Wilson, following Dodge's death.
Most of the construction is of native materials, and all woodwork carvings and fixtures were accomplished by American artists. The house features 39 brick chimneys feeding 24 fireplaces. It retains the furnishings, art treasures, and many of the personal items owned by Matilda Dodge-Wilson.
One of the home's most unique features is Knole Cottage, a home built on 2/3 scale for 12-year old Frances Dodge. It was the first all-electric home in the Metropolitan Detroit area.
The estate was donated to the state of Michigan in 1957. Oakland University now stands on the accompanying 1,400 acres.

Extra Special:
Each August, the Concours d' Elegance takes over the grounds of Meadow Brook Hall with more than 250 classic automobiles. August also brings "Art of Meadow Brook," an art show and sale.
During the holidays, this home is turned into a holiday wonderland decorated with fresh flowers, Christmas trees, antique toys and ornaments.
Adjacent to the home is Meadow Brook Theatre offering classic plays throughout the year.

Details:
