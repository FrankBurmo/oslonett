THUNDERFEST

Background:
Each June, more than 500,000 fans, the largest single-day crowd for a sporting event in the United States, line the shores of the Detroit River to view the annual Thunderfest. During this four-day event, the sleek, aerodynamic, piston or turbine-powered hydroplanes often exceed 200 mph during time trials. Often described as the "Indy 500" of boat racing, the event has become a Detroit instition.

What's Special:
In 1993, the City of Detroit was designated the permanent "home of the American Power Boat Association's Gold Cup" because of its rich heritage and 87-year history with powerboat racing's most important event.

The event is produced by the Spirit of Detroit Thunderfest, an all-volunteer civic organization that has been sponsoring and staging hydroplane racing events on the Detroit River for 32 years.

Extra Special:
For a glimpse at Thunderfest history, the Dossin Great Lakes Museum, located on Belle Isle Park's southern shore and a short walk from the racing action, houses one of the classic off-shore boats, "Ms. Pepsi," that competed in the event in the 1950s.
