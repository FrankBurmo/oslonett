DETROIT HISTORICAL MUSEUM


Background:
In 1928, the Detroit Historical Museum was established to preserve and display three dimensional objects, demonstrating how people lived in the past and how Detroit has grown and progressed. Visitors experience the full scope of Detroit's fascinating and colorful history from Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac's landing on the banks of the Detroit River through its birth as America's industrial capital.

In 1951, the current museum opened, which offers the popular exhibition the "Streets of Old Detroit" chronicling three historic periods of Detroit history. The museum offers various programs, craft workshops and entertainment. Additionally, the museum has an extensive automotive collection and houses one of the largest miniature train displays, the "Glancy Train Collection."


What's Special:
In November 1987, the museum opened the Booth-Wilkinson Costume Gallery featuring more than 50,000 garments and accessories. The gallery is enhanced by an adjacent research facility containing books, slides and other kinds of costume information.

A special exhibit, "Striving to Succeed: African American Businesses in Detroit," highlights Detroit's African American businesses from the early 1800s through the present time. The exhibit features pictures, artifacts and a video presentation on Detroit-based Barden Cable, one of the nation's largest African American-owned businesses. The museum has also established a "networking" bulletin board to increase the awareness and communication between African American-owned businesses.

In November 1992, the museum opened a gallery "From Furs to Factories," which traces the history of Detroit from a fur trading post to an industrial giant. The gallery features a walk-in diorama from the 1750's, a railway station, a ribbon farm display and a mock celebration of the Detroit International Fair and Exposition 1889-1892.
