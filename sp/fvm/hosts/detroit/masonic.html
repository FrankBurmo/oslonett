MASONIC TEMPLE

Background:
Completed in November 1926, Detroit's Masonic Temple is the largest of its kind in the nation. Designed in the classic gothic style with more than 100,000 cubic feet of Indiana limestone, the ritualistic building is a construction wonder. The roof, which is made up of copper, concrete and ashaplt covers 80,000 square feet or nearly two acres.
The Temple houses three separate theaters under one roof. The Main Theatre seats 4,608, the Cathedral Theatre seats 1,567 and the Lodge Auditorium seats 950. At the time of its construction it was the only building in the U.S. that contained three theatres under one roof.
The total floor area is 550,000 square feet or 12.7 acres including dining areas for more than 7,200.

What's Special:
The Masonic today, besides serving the needs of the Masons in southeast Michigan, features Broadway plays, special events, concerts and banquets.

Details:
