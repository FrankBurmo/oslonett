MUSIC HALL CENTER FOR THE PERFORMING ARTS

Background:
Originally named the Wilson Theatre, Music Hall was built in 1928 by Matilda Dodge Wilson, the widow of auto baron John F. Dodge and lumber executive Alfred Wilson, for the production of legitimate stage productions and variety performances.
The theater was crafted by many of the same artisans who built Meadow Brook Hall. The exterior of the building is art deco, featuring Mankato stone columns and details of Greek masks sculpted in terra cotta by Italian sculptor Corrado Parducci, whose work also graces the Guardian Building and Diego Rivera Court at The Detroit Institute of Arts. The interior is Spanish Renaissance.
Entertainers who have performed here include: Lucille Ball, Leonard Bernstein, W.C. Fields, Jack Benny, Maurice Chevalier, Martha Graham, Dizzy Gillespie, Count Basie, Cleo Laine, Ray Charles, Ella Fitzgerald, Ginger Rodgers, Liilian Helman and Julie Harris.
The theater's doors have remained open for 63 years, during which it also served as home to the Detroit Symphony Orchestra (which changed its name to Music Hall in 1945), Michigan Opera Theatre and Cinerama.
Cinerama came to the theater in 1953 after steel executive Mervyn Gaskin purchased the building. Music Hall was the second Cinerama theater in the world. Some of the featured films were "This is Cinerama," "How the West was Won," Cinerama Holiday," and "Seven Wonders of the World."
Civic leaders saved Music hall from demolition in 1973, creating Music Hall Center for the Performing Arts, a non profit organization.

What's Special:
In 1992, Music Hall began a $3 million restoration which will culminate in 1995 with the completion of detailed ceiling carvings, return of the famous neon-lit rooftop sign and rebuilding of the side boxes.
Today, Music Hall is considered the area's home for dance. The Hall presents major dance troupes, theatrical productions, children's programming and concerts. More than 200,000 people attend events there each year. In 1994, Music Hall will present the 16th annual Montreux Detroit Jazz Festival.
