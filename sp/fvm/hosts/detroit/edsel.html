EDSEL AND ELEANOR FORD HOUSE

Background:
The Edsel and Eleanor Ford House, located on 90 acres along Lake St. Clair in Grosse Pointe Shores, reflects the style of the Cotswolds in Worchestershire, England. The outside is faced with Briar Hill sandstone and leaded glass windows, studded occasionally with heraldic stained glass inserts. The stone roof is imported from England. Expert workmen were also brought over to split the stones and lay them in an authentic Cotswold manner. In addition to the workmanship, the home preserves for posterity fine examples of the 16th, 17th and 18th century English paneling and the Modern Room is an outstanding work of the Art Deco style by a leading designer of the 20th century, Walter Dorwin Teague.
In this home, Eleanor and Edsel raised their four children, Henry II, Benson, Josephine and William. William Clay Ford is the owner of the NFL Detroit Lions.

What's Special:
Eleanor Ford loved the arts. The Edsel and Eleanor Ford House remains intact with many of the original furnishings and fine arts including many pieces from Europe, Africa, Asia, America, some of it dating before the birth of Christ.
In addition to the beauty within the 60-room home, the Activities Center opened in 1990 to accomodate non-profit organizations throughout the area. This 17,000-square-foot structure may be used for concerts, recitals, meetings, receptions and dinners. The Activities Center is also used for exhibitions and cultural programming at various times throughout the year.

Extra Special:
The Edsel and Eleanor Ford House hosts holiday activities including the annual toy exhibition and a Christmas brunch and tour.
