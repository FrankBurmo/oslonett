HENRY FORD ESTATE - FAIR LANE

Background:
Henry Ford built his Scottish Baronial home in 1914 along the Rouge River in Dearborn. As an inventor, engineer and auto pioneer, Ford wanted to build a self-sufficient home. Thomas Edison helped Ford build a massive six-story powerhouse to supply all the power, heat and light.
The mansion is made entirely of concrete and Marblehead limestone. It has 56 rooms including 15 baths and eight fireplaces.

What's Special:
Ford entertained some of the world's most influential people here including Charles Lindbergh, the Duke of Windsor and President Herbert Hoover. The main floor of the 31,000-square-foot mansion features a ballroom that can hold hundreds of people. A tunnel leads to the lower floor with a one-lane bowling alley and a recreation room where the Ford's hosted folk dancing sessions.

Extra Special:
The holidays receive special treatment with several annual events: Floral guided tours, Breakfast with Santa, A Santa Workshop, Holiday luncheons and a Candlelight Tour.

Details:
The Fair Lane Estate currently functions as a conference and cultural center on The University of Michigan-Dearborn campus and is located at 4901 Evergreen Road, Dearborn, MI 48128, (313) 593-5590
Guided tours are given April-Dec., Mon.-Sat. 10 a.m., 11 a.m., 1 p.m., 2 p.m., 3 p.m.; Sunday every half hour from 1-4:30 p.m.; Jan.-March, daily tour 1:30 p.m.
Visitors also have the opportunity to tour the outdoor portion of the estate. The Ford Discovery Trail is a year round, self-guided walking tour featuring a tree house, boat house, gardens, scenic vistas and other landscape treasures.
Admission is $6 for adults; $5 for seniors and students; children under five are free.
