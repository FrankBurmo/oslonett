GRAYSTONE INTERNATIONAL
HALL OF FAME JAZZ MUSEUM

Background:
The Graystone International Hall of Fame Jazz Museum is one of two public jazz museums in the United States and the only jazz museum specializing in the history of jazz in the world.
In the 1920s and 1930s, Detroit was a jazz mecca. Paradise Valley, located in the heart of the black community on the city's near east side, was one of the few areas where unsegregated groups could enjoy music together. Among the sea of clubs and dance halls located in Paradise Valley, the great Graystone Ballroom was considered the entertainment district's finest.
The Graystone was the only ballroom around that regularly hired local black jazz bands. Top bands lead by jazz greats Fletcher Henderson and Duke Ellington played at the Graystone and inspired the improvisational style that is associated with the jazz tradition.

What's Special:
In addition to housing a display of musical instruments, records and other jazz memorabilia, the Graystone promotes and conducts research which traces the development of jazz from its beginning African rhythms to its modern forms. Duke Ellington, Count Basie, Billie Holliday, Thomas Dorsey and Frank Sinatra are all featured in the museum's permanent collection.

Activities:
Twelve weeks a year, the museum sponsor's a weekly concert series called "Blue Mondays," attracting some of the most notable jazz performers today. The weekly concerts take place at the Detroit Press Club in the Renaissance Center.

Details:
The Graystone Hall of Fame International Jazz Museum is located at 1521 Broadway, Detroit, MI 48226, (313) 963-3813. The museum is also located near the People Mover's Broadway station. Museum hours are Tue.-Fri. 10 a.m.-5 p.m., Sat. 11 a.m.-4 p.m. Admission is $2 for adults, $1 for children.
