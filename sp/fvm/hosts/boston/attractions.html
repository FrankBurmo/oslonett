Areas of Interest

Of course, no visit to the Greater Boston area would be complete without a trip to any one of over thirty museums the city has to offer. Boston is the locale for the world's first Computer Museum, the largest New England Aquarium, and one of the few Omnimax Theatres in the world at the Boston Museum of Science.

The Museum of Fine Arts is just one of many places to view paintings and sculptures. Children and adults alike continue to enjoy the John F. Kennedy Library & Museum, Museum of Afro American History, Children's Museum, Boston Tea Party Ship and USS Constitution Museum, to name a few. Beyond Boston, there are museums and attractions in the historic towns of Salem, Plymouth, Lowell, Lexington and Sturbridge.

Rates, dates and times are subject to change.

The Black Heritage Trail

The Black Heritage Trail is a walking tour that celebrates the history of Boston's Black community between 1800-1900 on Beacon Hill. All sites must be visited with an arranged tour, except those whose hours are noted. Tours can be arranged by contacting the Boston African American National Historic Site at (617) 742-5415.

  • African Meeting House: 8 Smith Court. Open daily 10-4.
  • Smith Court Residences: 3-10 Smith Court.
  • Abiel Smith School: 46 Joy Street. Open daily 10-4.
  • George Middleton House: 5 Pinckney Street.
  • Robert Gould Shaw and 54th Regiment Memorial: Located at the entrance to the Boston Common at Beacon and Park Streets.
  • The Phillips School: Located at the corner of Anderson and Pinckney Streets.
  • The home of John J. Smith: 86 Pinckney Street.
  • Charles Street Meeting House: Located at Mt. Vernon and Charles Streets.
  • Lewis and Harriet Hayden House: 66 Phillips Street.
  • Coburn's Gaming House: Located at Phillips and Irving Streets.
