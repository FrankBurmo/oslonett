Points of Interest

Admission to the points of interest listed here is free unless otherwise noted; admission is charged for theatre and stage productions. Please be sure to call to confirm hours and exhibition schedules.

Albert Einstein Memorial
National Academy of Sciences, 2101 Constitution Ave. NW.
Sculpture Robert Berks' 21-foot high bronze statue depicts the genius pondering a 3,000 star galaxy carved on black granite. In his hand is a piece of paper with his ground-breaking E=mc2 formula. Metro: Foggy Bottom.

Anacostia Museum See Smithsonian Institution section

Ansel Adams Collection
The Wilderness Society, 900 17th St. NW, second floor, Washington, DC 20006. (202) 833-2300.
A permanent exhibition of 75 Ansel Adams' landscape photographs. Open Monday-Friday 10 a.m.-5 p.m. Closed all federal holidays. Metro: Farragut North, Farragut West.

Arlington House
Arlington National Cemetery, Arlington, VA 22211. (703) 692-0931.
Former home of Robert E. Lee is open daily 9:30 a.m.-4:30 p.m. (to 6 p.m. April-September). Metro: Arlington Cemetery.

Arlington National Cemetery
Arlington, VA 22211. (703) 692-0931.
America's largest national burial ground. Includes the graves of President John F. Kennedy, Robert Kennedy, Supreme Court Justice Thurgood Marshall and boxer Joe Louis. The changing of the guard at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier takes place every hour in the winter and every half-hour in the summer. Open daily 8 a.m.-5 p.m. (to 7 p.m. April-September). Metro: Arlington Cemetery.

Arthur M. Sackler Gallery See Smithsonian Institution section

Art Museum Of The Americas
201 18th St. Nw, Washington, Dc 20006. (202) 458-6016.
Located behind the Organization Of American States headquarters, the Museum features contemporary art of Latin America and the Caribbean, regular showings of Latin American art documentaries and the Aztec Gardens. Open Tuesday-Saturday 10 A.M.-5 P.M. Closed all national holidays.

Arts And Industries Building

Art, Science And Technology Institute Holography Art Museum Of The 3rd Dimension
2018 R St. NW, Washington, Dc 20009. (202) 667-6322.
Unique collection of the best holographic artwork and applications, including one of the largest holograms in the world. Tours given by experts in holography. Open Tuesday-Sunday 11 a.m.-6 p.m. Closed Christmas, Easter and Thanksgiving. Admission. Metro: Dupont Circle.

The Awakening
Hains Point In East Potomac Park SW.
Set up temporarily for an art convention, this giant sculpture of a reclining man whose massive limbs appear to be literally rising from the earth has become a highly regarded part of the landscape. Open daily 6 a.m.-midnight.

B'Nai B'Rith Klutznick Museum
1640 Rhode Island Ave. Nw, Washington, Dc 20037. (202) 857-6583.
Permanent collection of Jewish ceremonial and folk art and special exhibits. Guided tours in Hebrew, French and Russian, advance notice required. Donation suggested. Open Sunday-Friday 10 a.m.-5 p.m. Closed all Jewish and national holidays. Metro: Farragut North.

Bureau Of Engraving And Printing
14th And C Streets SW, Washington, DC 20228. (202) 874-3019, (202) 874-3188 (Tape).
See currency being printed at the rate of 7,000 sheets per hour on the 25-minute self-guided tour; tickets required May-August. Special guided tours available by contacting your Congressional representative's office at least three months in advance of desired date. Open Monday-Friday 9 A.M.-2 P.M. Closed all federal holidays. Metro: Smithsonian.

Busch Gardens

A 360-acre theme park located 150 miles from Washington in historic Williamsburg, VA. Days and hours of operation vary; please call for information.

Capital Children's Museum
800 Third St. NE, Washington, DC 20002. (202) 543-8600
A unique educational complex that encourages children to touch, feel, smell, taste and wear the exhibits. Open daily 10 A.M.-5 P.M. Closed Christmas, New Year's Day, Thanksgiving, Easter. Admission, children under 2 are free. Metro: Union Station.

Christian Heurich Mansion
1307 New Hampshire Ave. NW, Washington, DC 20036-1507. (202) 785-2068
Former home of local brewer Christian Heurich, this Victorian mansion is now headquarters of the Historical Society of Washington, DC. Hourly room and Victorian garden tours. Open Wednesday-Saturday noon-3 P.M. Closed Dec. 22-28 and all federal holidays. Admission, children grades K-12 free. Metro: Dupont Circle.

Corcoran Gallery Of Art
500 17th St. NW, Washington, DC 20006. (202) 638-3211
Washington's oldest art collection features American art from Colonial times to the present, Dutch and Flemish masterpieces and French Impressionists and Post-Impressionists. Open Tuesday-Sunday 10 A.M.-5 P.M., Thursday to 9 pm. Closed Christmas and New Year's Day. Admission, members and children under 12 free. Metro: Farragut North, Farragut West.

Dar Museum
1776 D St. NW, Washington, DC 20006. (202) 879-3254, (202) 879-3241
The 33 period rooms feature a Superb collection of early American furniture and fine arts. Open Monday-Friday 8:30 A.M.-4 P.M., Sunday 1-5 P.M. Closed Monday of holiday weekends and two weeks in April. Metro: Farragut West, Farragut North.

Decatur House Museum
748 Jackson Place NW, Washington, DC 20006. (202) 842-0920, (202) 842-0917
Eight period rooms featuring 19th-century decorative arts, Chinese porcelain and memorabilia of naval hero Stephen Decatur. Open Tuesday-Friday 10 A.M.-3 P.M., Saturday-Sunday Noon-4 P.M. Admission. Metro: Farragut North, Mcpherson Square.

Dumbarton House
2715 Q St. NW, Washington, DC 20007. (202) 337-2288
Headquarters of the National Society of the Colonial Dames of America, this federal house museum in Georgetown includes period furnishing and art collection featuring a Charles Wilson Peale painting. Open Tuesday-Saturday 9:30 a.m.-noon. Closed all national holidays. Admission. Guided tours available. On-site parking. Metro: Dupont Circle.

Dumbarton Oaks
1703 32nd St. NW, Washington, DC 20007. (202) 338-8278; (202) 342-3212 (Tours). Splendid 19th-Century mansion and 10 acres of formal gardens near Georgetown. Museum open Tuesday-Sunday 2-5 P.M., donations accepted. Gardens open 2-6 pm daily (April-October). Closed all national holidays. Admission.

Folger Shakespeare Library

Metro: Capitol South.

Jefferson Memorial
Tidal Basin, south end of 15th Street SW. (202) 426-6841
The 19-foot bronze statue of the third president stands beneath a rotunda inscribed with passages from the Declaration of Independence and Jefferson's other famous writings. Open daily; park ranger in attendance 8 a.m.-midnight. Closed Christmas.

John F. Kennedy Center For The Performing Arts
2700 F St. NW, Washington, DC 20566. (202) 467-4600, 1-800-444-1324
An independent, self-supporting national performing arts center. Six magnificent theaters present drama, dance, music, comedy, film and commissioned works. Home of the National Symphony Orchestra, Washington Opera and the American Film Institute. Free guided tours daily starting at 10 a.m. Metro: Foggy Bottom-GWU.

Kenilworth Aquatic Gardens
Anacostia Avenue and Douglas Street NE, Washington, DC 20019. (202) 426-6905
Thousands of water plants, waterlilies, lotuses, water hyacinths and bamboo grow in ponds along the Anacostia River. Flowers bloom May-early fall. Open daily 7 a.m.-4:30 p.m. Guided tours available. Metro: Deanwood.

Lafayette Square
Pennsylvania Avenue and 16th Street NW. (202) 755-7798
Named for the Marquis de Lafayette following his triumphant visit to America in 1824, this meticulously landscaped square overlooks the White House. The Andrew Jackson statue is surrounded by tulips in spring. Metro: Farragut West.

Library Of Congress
101 Independence Ave. SE, Washington, DC 20540. (202) 707-5000; hours, (202) 707-6400; tours and exhibits, (202) 707-8000; use of reading rooms, (202) 707-6500; directions (202) 707-4700
The world's largest library with more than 100 million items in three buildings, including one of the world's three perfect copies of the Gutenberg Bible. Public tours depart daily from Room LM-139. Exhibition halls open Monday-Friday 8:30 a.m.-9:30 p.m., Saturday to 6 p.m. Closed Christmas and New Year's Day. Metro: Capitol South.

Lincoln Memorial
West Potomac Park at 23rd Street NW. (202) 426-6841
This grand memorial, overlooking the Reflecting Pool, was patterned on classic Greek temple. Inside the 19-foot marble statue of the 16th president is flanked by inscriptions of the Gettysburg Address and his Second Inaugural Address. Open daily; park ranger in attendance 8 a.m.-midnight. Closed Christmas. Metro: Foggy Bottom.

Mall
Between Constitution and Independence Avenues
A splendid green park area extending approximately two miles from the U.S. Capitol to the Washington Monument. Lining either side of the park near the Capitol are 200-year-old American elm trees, several of the Smithsonian Institution museums, National Archives, National Gallery of Art and U.S. Botanic Gardens. Metro: Smithsonian, Federal Triangle.

Marine Corps Memorial (Iwo Jima Statue)
Route 50, near Arlington National Cemetery, Arlington, VA.
One of the largest bronze statues ever cast, this 78-foot memorial commemorates all the marines who have died in battle since 1775. Felix W. de Weldon created the piece from a famous World War II photograph. Open 24 hours.

U.S. Department of Commerce Building, 14th Street and Constitution Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20230. (202) 482-2826, (202) 482-2825 (tape).
The nation's first public aquarium houses rare sea turtles, American alligators, sharks, piranha and a Touch Tank with hermit crabs and sea urchins. Open daily 9 a.m.-5 p.m. Closed Christmas. Admission. Metro: Federal Triangle.

3501 New York Ave. NE, Washington, DC 20002. (202) 475-4815 (tape).

National Archives And Records Administration
Seventh Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20408. (202) 501-5205, (202) 501-5000 (tape), (202) 501-5044 (TTY for the hearing impaired).
Houses more than 3 billion records and documents, including the Declaration of Independence, Constitution and Bill of Rights. Open daily 10 a.m.-5:30 p.m. (to 9 p.m. April-Labor Day). Closed Christmas. Guided tours available. Metro: Archives-Navy Memorial.

National Building Museum
401 F St. NW, Washington, DC 20001. (202) 272-2448
Housed in the historic Pension Building, the museum presents exhibits, lectures, films, family programs and workshops on all aspects of architecture, historic preservation and engineering. The Great Hall, site of 13 presidential inaugural balls, houses the world's largest Corinthian columns. "Washington: Symbol and City" chronicles the building of the city and the monuments. Open Monday-Saturday 10 a.m.-4 p.m., Sunday noon-4 p.m. Closed Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. Public tours Monday-Friday. Metro: Judiciary Square, Gallery Place-Chinatown.

National Firearms Museum
1600 Rhode Island Ave. NW, Washington, DC 20036. (202) 828-6253. 1-800-368-5714
One of the largest firearms collections in the U.S.A. with one-of-a-kind guns and firearms of famous people. Headquarters of the National Rifle Association. Open Monday-Saturday 10 a.m.-4 p.m. Closed all major federal holidays. Metro: Farragut North, Farragut West.

National Gallery Of Art
Fourth Street and Constitution Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20565. (202) 737-4215
One of the finest art museums in the world. The West Building contains works by the Old Masters 13th-19th centuries. The East Building contains a giant Calder mobile and other contemporary works. Open Monday-Saturday 10 a.m.-5 p.m., Sunday 11 a.m.-6 p.m. Closed Christmas, New Year's Day. Guided tours available. Metro: Archives-Navy Memorial, Judiciary Square.

National Geographic Society Explorers Hall
1145 17th St. NW, Washington, DC 20036. (202) 857-7000, (202) 857-7588 (tape).
Featuring Geographica, an interactive exhibit dedicated to teaching about the earth, its geography and the fragile balance among its inhabitants. Earth Station One simulates an orbital flight and features the world's largest unmounted globe. Open Monday-Saturday 9 a.m.-5 p.m., Sunday 10 a.m.-5 p.m. Closed Christmas. Metro: Farragut North, Farragut West.

National Law Enforcement Officers Memorial
Judiciary Square between E and F, Fourth and Fifth streets NW. (202) 737-3400
The elegant gray marble walls display the names of the more than 13,000 law enforcement officers who died in the line of duty, including the very first officer killed in the line of duty in 1794. Commemorative items and free walking tour brochures available at the Visitor Center, 605 E St. NW. Open 24 hours. Metro: Judiciary Square.

Smithsonian Institution

The world's largest museum complex with more than 100 million specimens and artifacts, the Smithsonian Institution is sometimes called the nation's attic. The nine museums on the Mall, five other museums and the National Zoological Park are free. Hours are 10 a.m.-5:30 p.m. daily; summer hours are determined annually. Closed Christmas. Tours available for the visually and hearing impaired; call for details.

Smithsonian Institution Information Center
1000 Jefferson Drive SW, Washington, DC 20560. (202) 357-2700, (202) 357-2020 (tape), (202) 633-9126 (Spanish tape), (202) 357-1729 (TTY for hearing impaired).
The oldest of the 14 Smithsonian museums in Washington, the Castle houses the crypt of James Smithson, founder of the Smithsonian Institution, scale models of Washington, interactive touch-screen program in six languages and two electronic wall maps, plus multilingual information and assistance. Metro: Smithsonian.

Anacostia Museum
1901 Fort Place SE, Washington, DC 20560. (202) 357-2700, (202) 357-1729 (TTY for the hearing impaired).
Changing exhibitions dealing with African-American history, art and culture in Washington, Maryland, Virginia and the Carolinas. Open 10 a.m.-5 p.m. Metro: Anacostia.

Arthur M. Sackler Gallery
1050 Independence Ave. SW, Washington, DC 20560. (202) 357-2700, (202) 357-1729 (TTY for the hearing impaired).
Houses nearly 1,000 works of art from Southeast Asia, including a number of important 20th-century Chinese paintings. Metro: Smithsonian.

Arts And Industries Building
900 Jefferson Drive SW, Washington, DC 20560. (202) 357-2700, (202) 357-1729 (TTY for the hearing impaired).
Exhibits in the second oldest Smithsonian building recapture the 1876 Philadelphia exposition and the Victorian era. Highlights include steam motors and a 42-foot model of the naval cruiser Antietam. Metro: Smithsonian.

This outstanding collection of Asian art and works by 19th- and 20th- century American artists has reopened after nearly five years and $26 million expansion and renovation. National Air And Space Museum
Sixth Street and Independence Avenue SW, Washington, DC 20560. (202) 257-2700, (202) 357-1729 (TTY for the hearing impaired).
The most visited museum in the world houses the Wright brothers' 1903 flyer, Spirit of St. Louis, Apollo 11 lunar command module and an incredible collection of aviation and space technology treasures. The Samuel P. Langley Theater shows four films throughout the day and spectacular IMAX films nightly (admission). The Albert Einstein Planetarium presents free programs on the workings of our universe Tuesday and Thursday at noon. Cassette tours in English, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese and Spanish. Audio description available in Langley Theater in French, Spanish, German and Japanese by prearrangement. Metro: L'Enfant Plaza.

National Museum Of African Art
950 Independence Ave. SW, Washington, DC 20560. (202) 357-2700, (202) 357-1729 (TTY for the hearing impaired).
The only U.S. museum devoted to the collection, study and exhibition of African art with particularly emphasis on works from south of the Sahara. Metro: Smithsonian.

Eighth and G streets NW, Washington, DC 20560. (202) 357-2700, (202) 357-1729 (TTY for the hearing impaired).
American paintings, sculpture, folk art, photographs and graphics from the 18th century to the present. Changing American art exhibitions as well as selections from the permanent collection. Guided tours available. Metro: Gallery Place.

National Museum Of American History
14th Street and Constitution Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20560. (202) 357-2700, (202) 357-1729 (TTY for the hearing impaired).

Neighborhoods

If you haven't ventured beyond the monuments, you've barely scratched the surface of what there is to see and do. Walk through Washington's colorful, historic neighborhoods and you'll find some of the city's greatest treasures.

Adams-Morgan
Along Columbia Road between 18th Street and Kalorama Park NW. One of Washington's most colorful neighborhoods with an international array of restaurants, boutiques, hip specialty stores and late-night entertainment.

Anacostia
East from the Anacostia River to Pennsylvania Avenue and Southern Avenue SW. Named after its Native American inhabitants, Anacostia dates back to the arrival of John Smith in 1607. The Frederick Douglass National Historic Site and the Anacostia Museum and Woodlawn Cemetery are located here.

Brookland
Along 12th Street, Michigan and South Dakota avenues NE. The second oldest neighborhood where African-Americans moved during the Depression has been home to Pearl Bailey, Nobel Peace Prize winner Ralph Bunche and poet Sterling Brown.

Capitol Hill
The shady residential streets include rows of charming town houses, art galleries, boutiques, restaurants and cafes. Eastern Market, an old-style farmer's market, attracts hundreds of residents every weekend.

Chinatown
Located in the blocks around G & H streets, between Sixth and Eighth streets NW. Washington's small Chinatown neighborhood is marked by the colorful Friendship and contains most of the city's Oriental restaurants and shops.

Downtown
Between Fourth and 17th streets and from Constitution Avenue north to M Street NW. The downtown area east of the White House has undergone a massive renovation in recent years and now boasts refurbished hotels, smart shops, wonderful restaurants, Pavilion at the Old Post Office, Warner Theater and National Theatre.

Dupont Circle
Along Connecticut Avenue from N Street through T Street NW. This lively neighborhood has all types of bookstores, unique shops and restaurants.

Embassy Row
Northwest of Dupont Circle, most of the city's 150 foreign embassies are found along Massachusetts Avenue between Sheridan Circle and Observatory Circle.

Foggy Bottom
South of K Street and Pennsylvania Avenue between 17th and 25th streets NW. Home of the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, George Washington University and the Watergate Complex.

Georgetown
Between Rock Creek Park and Georgetown University. Once a thriving colonial port and a strategic point on the Underground Railroad, Georgetown is a charming, historic area of specialty stores, nightclubs and intriguing restaurants.

Ledroit Park
North of Florida Avenue to W Street between Second and Fifth streets NW, this historic area with 19th-century Romantic Revival houses, was home to African-American poet Paul Laurence Dunbar and author/educator Anna J. Cooper. Current residents include Walter Washington, the first African-American mayor of Washington, and Jesse Jackson. Griffith Stadium was located here until the 1960s when the Howard University Hospital was built.

Southwest
The entire area south of the Mall is home to the Tony Award-winning Arena Stage, Benjamin Banneker Circle and Fountain and L'Enfant Plaza. For several blocks along Maine Avenue, the colorful Waterfront has developed into a shimmering array of piers, sailboats, yachts, old-time steamboats, fishing boats, seafood markets and restaurant.

Shaw
Bounded by North Capitol Street on the east, 15th Street on the west and M Street on the south. This was the business and retail hub of the city's African-American community and center of its nightlife until the end of segregation in the early 1950s. Landmarks in this prominent neighborhood include the O Street Market, Howard Theater (currently under renovation), Bethune Museum and Archives and the Association for the Study of Afro-American Life and History.

Anacostia Museum

1920 Martin Luther King Jr. Ave. SE, Washington, DC 20020. (202) 678-7625. (202) 678-7625 (tape).
Gallery and on-going performance art activities. Open daily 11 a.m.-6:30 p.m. Contribution requested for performance art. Metro: Anacostia.

A. Philip Randolph Statue
Union Station, 50 Massachusetts Ave. NE, Washington, DC. This bronze statue honors the founder of the Sleeping Car Porters Union and organizer of the famous 1963 March on Washington. Metro: Union Station.

Banneker Circle And Fountain
L'Enfant Plaza near Maine Avenue and Water Street SW, Washington, DC. A tribute to African-American mathematician and astronomer Benjamin Banneker. In 1791, Banneker assisted Pierre Charles L'Enfant with the original design for the city of Washington. According to legend, when L'Enfant was relieved of his duties and took all copies of the plan back to his native Paris, Secretary of State Thomas Jefferson relied on Banneker to reconstruct the plan from memory. Metro: L'Enfant Plaza.

Bethune Museum & Archives
1318 Vermont Ave. NW, Washington, DC 20005. (202) 332-1233.
This repository and research center for African-American women's history is located in the former home of Mary McLeod Bethune, founder of the National Council of Negro Women. Tours available. Open Monday-Friday 10 a.m.-4:30 p.m. Metro: McPherson Square.

Directs visitors to sites in historic neighborhoods that illustrate aspects of African-American history. Ebenezer Methodist Church

Emancipation Statue
Lincoln Park, East Capitol Street between 11th and 13th streets NE, Washington, DC. (202) 690-5155.
Built almost entirely with funds donated by former slaves, the statue depicts Archer Alexander, the last slave captured under the Fugitive Slave Law, breaking the chains of slavery while Abraham Lincoln reads the Emancipation Proclamation.

Former residence of famed African-American abolitionist, orator, diplomat, essayist and auditor of the U.S. Treasury. Visitor center features a film and exhibits on Douglass's life. Tours available. Open daily 9 a.m.-5 p.m. (April 15-Oct. 15), 9 a.m.-4 p.m. (Oct. 16-April 14). Closed Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year's Day. Metro: Anacostia.

Howard University
2400 Sixth St. NW, Washington, DC. (202) 806-6100, (202) 806-0999 (events tape).
One of the most prestigious, historically black universities in the country. Lincoln Park
East Capitol Street between 11th and 13th streets NE. (202) 690-5155.
This historic park celebrates the abolition of slavery in the District of Columbia. The park features the Emancipation statue and the Mary McLeod Bethune statue.

Surrounding Area

Listed here are additional sights and attractions that are an easy day's driving distance from Washington. For more information, contact the state and local visitors offices.

Annapolis, MD
Once a busy seaport, Maryland's historic state capital is home to the U.S. Naval Academy, the 17th-century Maryland State House, cobblestoned streets, quaint shops, sailing and pleasure boating.

Baltimore, MD
Just an hour away, Baltimore's Inner Harbor shopping and dining pavilion features the National Aquarium and U.S.S. Constellation. In the surrounding neighborhoods are town houses, elegant shops, ethnic dining and galleries.

Colonial Williamsburg
Three hours south of Washington, this 18th-century colonial village features more than 500 restored buildings and costumed townspeople.

Luray Caverns
P.O. Box 748, Luray, VA 22835. (703) 743-6551.
Largest and most popular caverns in the eastern U.S.A. Near Skyline Drive and Shenandoah National Park. Tours available.

Paramount's King Dominion
I-95 and Route 30, Doswell, VA 23047. (804) 876-5000.
Movie theme park with more than 100 rides, shows and attractions. Open daily Memorial Day-Labor Day; weekends April-May and September-October; call for exact operating days and hours. Admission.

Shenandoah Mountains

Explore the charming small towns full of antique shops and many natural caves that the Shenandoah Valley has to offer.
